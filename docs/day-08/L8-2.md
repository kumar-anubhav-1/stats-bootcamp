## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "Who are our most valuable customers, and what is a customer worth?"

=== "Finance"

    > "How well do we retain card customers by cohort?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 RFM

    Score each customer 1–5 on **R**ecency (weeks since last purchase), **F**requency (baskets) and **M**onetary (spend), and sum.

    - Customers scoring 13–15 are **18.8%** of customers and **39.0%** of revenue.
    - The top 10% by spend deliver **37.2%** of revenue (concentration — and bulk buyers, R07).

    ### 2.2 A simple CLV

    $$\text{CLV} = m \times \frac{1 + d}{1 + d - r}$$

    with annual margin per customer m, retention r and discount rate d. Year-1 customers who bought again in year 2: **r =
    65.8%**; m = 19.2; d = 10% → **CLV ≈ 48** for the average customer and
    ≈ 194 for the top decile.

    Assumptions: constant retention and margin, no growth, everyone alike. In non-contractual retail, "churn" is unobserved (customers
    just stop coming) — probabilistic models (BG/NBD for purchases + Gamma-Gamma for spend) estimate the chance a customer is still
    "alive". Use the simple formula for orders of magnitude; use the models for targeting.

    ### 2.3 Cohort retention (finance)

    Share of accounts **not closed within 12 months**, by origination year: 2023 98.4% · 2024 98.5% · 2025 98.4%. Year-1 attrition is
    low; churn happens later. And **71.9%** of accounts have not churned by the end of the panel — **censored**:
    we don't know when (or if) they will. Averages of "time to churn" over churned accounts only are badly biased; use survival
    methods (L9.2).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        RFM ranks customers simply; CLV puts a value on them (with assumptions); cohorts show retention over time. Censoring and
        concentration change the answers.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Spend acquisition money against CLV ranges**, not point estimates; the top decile's value is ~4× the average.
    - **Concentration is risk**: a few bulk buyers drive a large share of revenue (R07).
    - **Retention metrics must handle censoring** — otherwise recent cohorts look artificially good or bad.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Which retention and margin assumptions drive this CLV, and how does it change if retention drops 10 points?"*

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R8-4 · Basic · RFM concentration"
    **Business framing:** Who matters most?

    **Deliverable:** Share of customers and revenue with RFM 13–15; top-10% revenue share.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 18.8% of customers → 39.0% of revenue; top 10% → 37.2%. *(tolerance: ± 1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Revenue is concentrated; protect the best customers.

        **Common wrong answer:** Ignoring bulk buyers (R07) inflating M.

    **Leader drill:** What retention action for the top RFM group?

??? example "R8-5 · Applied · Simple CLV"
    **Business framing:** Finance wants a customer value.

    **Deliverable:** Retention year 1→2, annual margin per customer, CLV (d = 10%), top-decile CLV; sensitivity ±10 pp retention.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Retention 65.8%; margin 19.2; CLV 48.0; top decile 194.0. *(tolerance: ± 5%)*

        **Model answer:** CLV is very sensitive to retention; report a range.

        **Common wrong answer:** Single-point CLV without assumptions.

    **Leader drill:** How much would you pay to acquire a customer?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F8-2 · Basic · Cohort retention"
    **Business framing:** Are newer cohorts retained as well?

    **Deliverable:** 12-month retention by origination year; censored share.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 2023 98.4% · 2024 98.5% · 2025 98.4%; censored 71.9%. *(tolerance: ± 0.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Early attrition is low; most accounts censored — survival methods needed.

        **Common wrong answer:** Average time to churn over churned accounts only.

    **Leader drill:** Which retention KPI would you adopt?

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Elasticity with the right controls

    Poisson regression of weekly units on log(shelf ÷ list price), with SKU, store and week fixed effects and promotion/display controls,
    excluding stock-out weeks (R13):

    | Category | Elasticity | Without promo controls | Planted |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Snacks | **-1.824** (SE 0.037) | -1.859 | -1.8 |
    | Dairy | **-0.568** | -0.992 | -0.6 |

    Without controls, promotions (lower price *and* displays, extra lift) make demand look more price-sensitive than it is — Dairy's
    estimate almost doubles. Displays and promotions are confounders (L4.4).

    ### 2.2 What a +5% price change does (constant elasticity, cost ≈ 67–68% of list)

    | Category | Units | Gross margin |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Snacks (elastic) | -8.5% | 5.2% |
    | Dairy (inelastic) | -2.7% | **+12.7%** |

    With elasticity above 1 in absolute value, the profit-maximising price under constant elasticity is cost × e/(1+e) —
    **2.21 × cost** for Snacks (47.6% vs current list). That is an upper reference,
    not a recommendation: competitor reaction, price image and cross-category effects limit it. For inelastic Dairy, the model says
    "raise prices" without limit — a sign the constant-elasticity model breaks down outside observed price ranges.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="elasticity" data-caption="Hover along the curves: Snacks (elastic) loses units fast; Dairy (inelastic) keeps volume — margin moves differently."></div>

    ### 2.3 Promotions: uplift, cannibalisation, ROI

    - **Uplift:** promoted Snacks SKU-store-weeks sell **+116.6%** vs the same SKU-store's non-promo average
      (average discount 21.6%).
    - **Cannibalisation (R10):** promoting Brand X 400 ml cut Brand X 200 ml's share by 21.7% — about
      **20.0%** of the promoted SKU's share gain came from its sibling. Net uplift = gross − cannibalised − pull-forward.
    - **ROI:** incremental margin ÷ discount given to baseline volume ≈ **0.61** — each 1.00 of discount on sales that
      would have happened anyway returns 0.61 of incremental margin (before halo and traffic effects). Promotions earn
      only 11.7% margin on promoted volume.

    ### 2.4 Marketing-mix modelling (awareness)

    MMM regresses sales on media spend over time with **adstock** (advertising effects decay over weeks) and **saturation** (diminishing
    returns), plus price, promotions, seasonality. It estimates channel ROI and response curves for budget allocation. Our data has no
    media spend; the prompt card shows how to set it up when it does. Calibrate MMM with experiments (geo tests).

    ### 2.5 Finance: rate sensitivity

    Take-up vs offered rate needs **randomised rate tests** or offer-level data (approved but not accepted applications); historical
    rates are set by risk (L5.1) so a naive regression of take-up on rate mixes price sensitivity with risk.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Elasticity needs controls (promotions, displays, stock-outs) and fixed effects. Promotions must be judged on net, incremental
        margin after cannibalisation and pull-forward. Optimisation outputs are references bounded by strategy.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Dairy is the safer margin lever** (+5% price costs only ~2.7% of units). A Snacks price rise also lifts
      margin on paper but loses ~8.5% of units — with traffic and basket effects at stake, test before moving.
    - **Promo ROI below 1 is common** — justify promotions by traffic/halo with evidence (tests), or cut depth/frequency.
    - **Price tests beat price models** for big decisions; use models to choose what to test.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Elasticity with and without promo controls, net promo uplift after cannibalisation, and the margin impact in money."*

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R8-8 · Basic · Elasticities"
    **Business framing:** Pricing asks how sensitive Snacks and Dairy are.

    **Deliverable:** Run both elasticity configs; with and without promo controls.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Snacks -1.824 (no controls ≈ -1.859); Dairy -0.568 (no controls ≈ -0.992). *(tolerance: ± 0.1)*

        **Model answer:** Snacks elastic, Dairy inelastic; omitting promo controls overstates sensitivity.

        **Common wrong answer:** Regressing units on price without controls.

    **Leader drill:** Which category would you test a price rise in?

??? example "R8-9 · Applied · Price simulation"
    **Business framing:** What does +5% do?

    **Deliverable:** Units and margin change for +5% in Snacks and Dairy.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Snacks units -8.5%, margin 5.2%; Dairy units -2.7%, margin 12.7%. *(tolerance: ± 0.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Both raise margin on paper; Dairy loses little volume.

        **Common wrong answer:** Ignoring traffic/basket effects of Snacks volume loss.

    **Leader drill:** What guardrails for a price test?

??? example "R8-10 · Stretch · Promo ROI and cannibalisation"
    **Business framing:** Do Snacks promotions pay?

    **Deliverable:** Uplift vs baseline, average discount, ROI; Brand X 200 ml cannibalisation.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Uplift +116.6%; discount 21.6%; ROI 0.61; cannibalisation -21.7% (20.0% of gain). *(tolerance: ± 5%)*

        **Model answer:** Big volume, ROI below 1 before halo; part of the gain is cannibalised.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting gross uplift as profit.

    **Leader drill:** Which promotions would you cut?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F8-4 · Basic · Rate sensitivity design"
    **Business framing:** How sensitive is take-up to rate?

    **Deliverable:** Design a randomised rate test.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Randomise offered rate within risk bands; measure take-up and later default; size with rcp_power. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Historical rates are risk-based — confounded.

        **Common wrong answer:** Regressing take-up on historical rates.

    **Leader drill:** What guardrails (risk, fairness)?

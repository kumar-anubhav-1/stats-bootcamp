# L11.2 · Image classification, augmentation and transfer learning

<p class="lesson-meta">Day 11 · Optional module · Basic → Advanced 75 min · Leader 30 min · Prerequisites: L6.4–L6.6, L11.1 · Recipe: prompt-driven (scikit-learn; optional PyTorch)</p>

<div class="you-are-here">📍 <b>You are here:</b> Predict an outcome for each customer/account › Images, text, sequences at scale › <a href="../../navigator/#N-ML-DL">Deep learning (awareness) — usually not better than GBM on tabular data</a> <code>N-ML-DL</code></div>

**Objectives** — you can: frame a vision problem as classification ("is this slot empty?"); compare a simple rule, hand-made
features, raw pixels and a small neural network on a fair split; explain why accuracy misleads when gaps are rare; use **data
augmentation** to make a model robust to lighting; and explain **transfer learning** — reusing a model pretrained on millions of
photos.

!!! info "In plain words"
    We cut every shelf photo into narrow windows and ask a model one yes/no question per window: *empty slot or not?* Then we test it
    on stores and conditions it has never seen — because that is where vision models fail.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > *"Train it on six stores, roll it out to all of them. Will it still work in store S07, whose camera went dim, or in S03, whose
    > camera is out of focus?"*

=== "Finance"

    > *"Before reading the amount, sort each scanned image: cheque, ID card or utility bill. Same idea — one label per image."*

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The set-up

    Each photo is cut into 16-pixel-wide windows every 4 pixels. A window is labelled **gap** if a person's box covers ≥ 75% of it,
    **product** if it covers none, and left out if in between. Training: stores S01, S02, S04, S05, S06, S08
    (9,850 windows, only 6.6% gaps). Testing: unseen stores S09 and S10
    (3,232 windows), plus the dim camera (S07, weeks 5–8) and the blurry one (S03).

    ### 2.2 Four approaches, four failure modes

    | Model | Accuracy (new stores) | Gaps found (recall) | Flags that are real gaps (precision) | Accuracy, dim camera | Gaps found, dim | Gaps found, blurry |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Brightness rule (dark window = gap) | 57.9% | 74.0% | 13.7% | 5.8% | 100.0% | 64.4% |
    | Logistic regression on hand-made features | 99.8% | 98.9% | 98.6% | 77.0% | 100.0% | 97.7% |
    | Logistic regression on raw pixels | 99.8% | 100.0% | 97.2% | 99.0% | 100.0% | 14.7% |
    | Small neural network on raw pixels | 100.0% | 100.0% | 99.6% | 95.1% | 16.7% | 99.4% |
    

    Read it row by row:

    - **Brightness rule** — dark packs look like gaps: only 13.7% of its
      flags are real, and in the dim store almost everything looks dark.
    - **Hand-made features** — excellent in normal light, but in the dim store the colours shift and accuracy falls to
      77.0% (a flood of false alarms).
    - **Raw pixels + logistic regression** — copes with dim light but finds only
      14.7% of gaps in blurry photos.
    - **Small neural network** — near perfect on new stores, yet in the dim store it finds only
      16.7% of gaps while still scoring
      95.1% accuracy.

    ### 2.3 The accuracy trap

    Gaps are rare, so a model that says "no gap" everywhere is right most of the time. That is why the table reports **recall**
    (share of real gaps found) and **precision** (share of flags that are real) — exactly the lesson of L6.5, now on images.

    ### 2.4 Data augmentation

    Make extra training images by changing what *shouldn't* matter — here, brightness (random 50–110%). The model learns that a gap
    is a gap in any light. Retrained with augmentation, the same network finds **100.0%** of gaps on the dim camera
    (was 16.7%) and 100.0% on the blurry one, without losing
    anything on new stores (100.0% recall, 99.6% precision).

    Other common augmentations: small shifts and rotations, flips (if the meaning doesn't change), crops, blur, colour jitter, noise.

    ### 2.5 Transfer learning

    Real shelf photos are bigger and messier than ours. Instead of training from scratch, start from a CNN **pretrained** on millions
    of everyday photos (e.g. ResNet, EfficientNet, a vision transformer). Its early filters already detect edges, textures and shapes;
    you replace the last layer and **fine-tune** on a few thousand labelled shelf images. This is how most industrial vision
    systems are built, and it needs far less data and compute.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Vision models fail on conditions they didn't see in training. Test on new stores and bad cameras, report recall and
        precision, augment for what shouldn't matter, and start from pretrained models.

    **Judgement points**

    - **"99% accurate" is meaningless for rare events** — ask for gap recall and precision by store and camera.
    - **Insist on a hold-out of whole stores** (not random windows), or performance will look better than reality.
    - **Augmentation and transfer learning are cheap insurance**; ask whether they were used.
    - **Label quality caps model quality** (L11.5).

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"How does it do on the worst camera, not the average one?"*

## 5. Output anatomy

| Item | Read it as |
| --- | --- |
| accuracy | misleading when the event is rare |
| recall (gaps found) | missed gaps = lost sales nobody sees |
| precision (true flags) | false flags = wasted staff walks |
| per-camera results | where the model will fail in rollout |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Symptom | Cause | Fix |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | great on test, poor in one store | different light/camera | augmentation, more varied training data |
    | high accuracy, low recall | rare class | report recall/precision, adjust threshold |
    | fails on blurry images | quality | fix camera; blur augmentation |
    | little labelled data | — | transfer learning |

=== "Leader"

    | Claim | Ask | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | "98% accuracy" | recall/precision per store and camera | rare-event metrics |
    | "ready for all stores" | hold-out stores, worst-camera results | test where it will run |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R11-4 · Basic · Four models"
    **Business framing:** Pick a gap classifier.

    **Deliverable:** Rule, hand features, pixel logistic, MLP: accuracy, recall, precision on new stores.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Brightness rule (dark window = gap): recall 74.0%, precision 13.7%; Logistic regression on hand-made features: recall 98.9%, precision 98.6%; Logistic regression on raw pixels: recall 100.0%, precision 97.2%; Small neural network on raw pixels: recall 100.0%, precision 99.6%;  *(tolerance: ± 2 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Report recall and precision — accuracy hides rare gaps.

        **Common wrong answer:** Choosing by accuracy.

    **Leader drill:** Which metric goes on the store dashboard?

??? example "R11-5 · Applied · Worst camera test"
    **Business framing:** Will it work in every store?

    **Deliverable:** Gap recall on S07 weeks 5–8 and on S03 for each model.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** MLP dim recall 16.7% (accuracy still 95.1%); pixel logistic blur recall 14.7%. *(tolerance: ± 5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Each model fails on a different condition.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting only the average.

    **Leader drill:** Roll out or not?

??? example "R11-6 · Stretch · Augmentation"
    **Business framing:** Make the network robust to light.

    **Deliverable:** Retrain the MLP with random brightness 50–110%; compare.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Dim recall 16.7% → 100.0%; blur 100.0%; new stores unchanged. *(tolerance: ± 5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Augment for what shouldn't matter.

        **Common wrong answer:** Collecting more bright images only.

    **Leader drill:** What other conditions would you augment for?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F11-3 · Basic · Document type classifier (design)"
    **Business framing:** Sort cheques, IDs and bills.

    **Deliverable:** Frame as classification; data and metric.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Labelled images per type, split by branch/date, report recall per class; transfer learning from a pretrained CNN. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** One label per image.

        **Common wrong answer:** Random split across duplicate scans.

    **Leader drill:** Which misclassification is most costly?

??? example "F11-4 · Applied · Transfer learning plan"
    **Business framing:** Few labelled IDs.

    **Deliverable:** Explain fine-tuning a pretrained model.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Reuse early filters, replace the last layer, fine-tune with augmentation (rotation, blur, brightness). *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Less data, faster, better.

        **Common wrong answer:** Training a CNN from scratch on 500 images.

    **Leader drill:** What data agreement covers the pretrained model?

??? example "F11-5 · Stretch · Augmentation for scans"
    **Business framing:** Scans vary by angle and lighting.

    **Deliverable:** List augmentations that keep meaning, and ones that don't.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** OK: small rotation, blur, brightness, noise. Not OK: flipping digits (6↔9), large crops cutting fields. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Augment only what doesn't change the label.

        **Common wrong answer:** Horizontal flips on text.

    **Leader drill:** How would you validate the choice?

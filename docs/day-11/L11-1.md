# L11.1 · Images as data: pixels, filters and features

<p class="lesson-meta">Day 11 · Optional module · Basic → Advanced 60 min · Leader 25 min · Prerequisites: L1.4, L2.7 · Recipe: prompt-driven (NumPy)</p>

**Objectives** — you can: explain that a photo is just a grid of numbers; describe colour channels and resolution; explain what a
filter (convolution) does and why it finds edges; build simple hand-made features from an image; and judge image quality
(brightness, sharpness) before trusting any model.

!!! info "In plain words"
    A computer doesn't "see" a shelf. It sees a table of numbers — one number per pixel per colour. Everything in computer vision is
    statistics and machine learning on those tables.

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 A photo is a grid of numbers

    Each NorthStar shelf photo is **48 pixels tall × 192 wide × 3 colours** (red, green, blue) =
    **27,648 numbers**, each from 0 (dark) to 255 (bright). The course has 400 photos from 10
    stores over 8 weeks, with 379 out-of-stock gaps labelled by staff.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="cv_shelves" data-caption="Switch cameras: a normal one, one that goes dim, and one that is out of focus. The red boxes are the gaps a person labelled."></div>

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="cv_pixels" data-caption="Zoom into one corner: switch to each colour channel to see the actual numbers the model receives."></div>

    ### 2.2 Filters: how a model notices edges

    A **filter** (or *kernel*) is a small 3 × 3 grid of weights. Slide it across the image; at each position multiply the nine
    pixels under it by the nine weights and add them up. A filter with −1s on the left and +1s on the right gives a big number only
    where brightness jumps from left to right — a **vertical edge**, like the side of a pack.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="cv_convolution" data-caption="Slide through filters: blur smooths noise, edge filters light up pack outlines and price labels, sharpen exaggerates detail. A CNN learns thousands of such filters by itself."></div>

    A **convolutional neural network (CNN)** stacks many learned filters: early layers find edges, later layers combine them into
    shapes (a label, a pack), and the last layer decides "gap" or "product". That is the only new idea compared with L9.8.

    ### 2.3 Hand-made features vs raw pixels

    Before deep learning, analysts summarised each image region with a few **hand-made features**: average red, green and blue,
    their spread, colour saturation and how many edges there are (8 numbers instead of
    504 raw pixel values per window). They are quick and explainable — and, as L11.2 shows, fragile when the
    lighting changes.

    ### 2.4 Check image quality first

    | Check | How | Our data |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Brightness | average pixel value per image | store S07 drops from 0.534 to 0.293 from week 5 |
    | Sharpness | average jump between neighbouring pixels | S03 scores 5.46 vs about 12.32 elsewhere — out of focus |
    | Resolution | pixels per product | a 16-pixel-wide window covers about one pack |

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="cv_drift" data-caption="Brightness by store and week. A model trained on bright photos may fail silently on the dim camera — exactly the drift idea from L9.6."></div>

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Images are tables of numbers. Models learn filters that detect edges and shapes. Image quality — light, focus, angle — is
        the first thing that breaks.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Ask about the cameras before the model:** placement, lighting, focus, how often images are captured.
    - **Quality monitoring is cheap:** brightness and sharpness per camera, every day, flag outliers (one dim camera here).
    - **Privacy:** shelf cameras can capture shoppers and staff; blur faces, restrict access, keep only what you need.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Show me ten random images from every camera before you show me any accuracy number."*

## 5. Output anatomy

| Item | Read it as |
| --- | --- |
| image shape (48, 192, 3) | rows × columns × colour channels |
| pixel value 0–255 | dark → bright, per channel |
| filter output | big where the pattern in the kernel appears |
| brightness / sharpness per camera | image-quality monitoring |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | You notice | Likely cause | Do |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | one camera darker than the rest | lighting failure, dirty lens | flag, fix camera, augment training |
    | low sharpness | out of focus, motion | re-focus; exclude or flag images |
    | different image sizes | mixed cameras | resize consistently before modelling |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R11-1 · Basic · Inspect the photos"
    **Business framing:** Operations wants to know what the cameras see.

    **Deliverable:** Show one photo per store with labelled gaps; report image shape and number of labelled gaps.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 400 photos of [48, 192, 3]; 379 labelled gaps. *(tolerance: exact)*

        **Model answer:** Every photo is 27,648 numbers; labels are left/right pixel positions of gaps.

        **Common wrong answer:** Modelling before looking at the images.

    **Leader drill:** Which camera would you check first?

??? example "R11-2 · Applied · Camera quality check"
    **Business framing:** Are all cameras healthy?

    **Deliverable:** Brightness and sharpness per store and week; flag outliers.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** S07 brightness 0.534 → 0.293 from week 5; S03 sharpness 5.46 vs ~12.32. *(tolerance: ± 0.02)*

        **Model answer:** One dim and one blurry camera (V1, V2).

        **Common wrong answer:** Averaging across stores and missing both.

    **Leader drill:** Who fixes cameras, and how fast?

??? example "R11-3 · Stretch · Filters by hand"
    **Business framing:** Understand what a CNN learns.

    **Deliverable:** Apply blur and vertical-edge filters with NumPy; explain the output.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Edge filter lights up pack sides and label borders; blur removes sensor noise. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Filters are weighted sums over 3 × 3 neighbourhoods.

        **Common wrong answer:** Treating filters as magic.

    **Leader drill:** Why do learned filters beat hand-made ones?

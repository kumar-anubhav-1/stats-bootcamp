# L11.4 · Document AI: reading cheque amounts (OCR) and knowing when not to trust it

<p class="lesson-meta">Day 11 · Optional module · Basic → Advanced 60 min · Leader 30 min · Prerequisites: L6.3, L6.6, L11.2 · Recipe: prompt-driven (scikit-learn)</p>

<div class="you-are-here">📍 <b>You are here:</b> Predict an outcome for each customer/account › Images, text, sequences at scale › <a href="../../navigator/#N-ML-DL">Deep learning (awareness) — usually not better than GBM on tabular data</a> <code>N-ML-DL</code></div>

**Objectives** — you can: explain optical character recognition (OCR) as image classification of characters; compare classifiers
on real handwriting; see why a 6-digit field is much harder than one digit; route low-confidence reads to people with a
**confidence threshold**; and spot image-quality drift in a scanner batch.

!!! info "In plain words"
    Reading a cheque amount is six small image-classification problems in a row. If any one digit is wrong, the whole amount is
    wrong — so the machine must know when to ask a person.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > *"Read supplier invoices and price labels automatically, and send doubtful ones to the finance team."*

=== "Finance"

    > Meridian's operations director: *"Automate cheque amount entry. I can accept fewer automatic reads, but not wrong amounts —
    > the error rate on what we automate must stay below 0.5%."*

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Real handwriting, small images

    We use 1,797 real handwritten digits (8 × 8 pixels = 64 numbers each; bundled with
    scikit-learn), half for training and half for testing.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="cv_digits" data-caption="Switch between digits read correctly and misread ones. Misreads are usually digits a person would also squint at."></div>

    | Classifier | Digit accuracy (test) |
    | --- | --- |
    | Logistic regression | 96.1% |
    | Support vector machine | 98.3% |
    | Small neural network | 97.4% |
    

    Best: **Support vector machine**. Most common confusion: a 4 read as a
    8.

    ### 2.2 Why the whole amount is harder than one digit

    If each digit is right 98.78% of the time, a 6-digit amount is right only about
    0.9878⁶ ≈ **92.9%** of the time. Simulating 5,000 cheques gives
    **92.8%** — so about 7.2% of amounts would be wrong if we trusted
    every read. Small errors multiply.

    ### 2.3 Confidence routing: automate what's safe, send the rest to people

    Each read comes with the model's confidence (its top probability). Take the *lowest* of the six digits' confidences; automate
    only if it clears a threshold.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="cv_confidence" data-caption="Blue: share of cheques processed automatically. Red: share of those automatic reads that are wrong. Move right along the curve to trade automation for accuracy."></div>

    | Threshold | Automated | Wrong among automated |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | 0.0 | 100.0% | 7.2% |
    | 0.5 | 84.8% | 2.17% |
    | 0.7 | 62.7% | 0.0% |
    | 0.8 | 46.1% | 0.0% |
    | 0.9 | 14.7% | 0.0% |
    | 0.95 | 1.6% | 0.0% |
    | 0.99 | 0.0% | 0.0% |
    

    The lowest threshold meeting the 0.5% target is **0.7**, automating
    **62.7%** of cheques. The rest go to a person — **human-in-the-loop**.

    ### 2.4 Scanner drift

    A batch from a smudged scanner (noise added) drops digit accuracy from 98.3% to
    **95.2%**. Monitor accuracy on a small daily sample keyed by people, and image quality per scanner (L11.1).

    ### 2.5 Modern document AI

    Today's systems read whole lines with CNN + sequence models or transformers, find fields with detectors (L11.3), and check
    results against business rules: the amount in figures must match the amount in words, the date must be valid, the account must
    exist. Cross-checks catch many machine errors before a person ever looks.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        OCR is classification of characters; whole fields fail more often than single characters; route by confidence and
        cross-check with business rules.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Set the error tolerance first** (e.g. ≤ 0.5% wrong among automated) and let the threshold follow; automation % is the result.
    - **Ask for field-level, not character-level, accuracy** — vendors often quote the flattering one.
    - **Keep people in the loop** for low-confidence and high-value items; sample automated reads for audit.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"What share is automated at our error tolerance, and how do we catch the errors that still get through?"*

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Symptom | Cause | Fix |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | good digits, poor amounts | errors multiply across digits | confidence routing, rule cross-checks |
    | sudden accuracy drop | scanner or form change | quality monitoring, retrain |
    | confidences all high | poorly calibrated model | calibrate (L6.6) before routing |

=== "Leader"

    | Situation | Action | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | automation business case | quote automation % at the error tolerance | field-level metrics |
    | high-value cheques | always human review above a value limit | risk-based review |

??? example "F11-9 · Basic · Field accuracy"

    **Deliverable:** Field accuracy for 6-digit amounts; compare with digit accuracy⁶.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"

??? example "F11-10 · Applied · Confidence routing"

    **Deliverable:** Automation % and error % by threshold; pick threshold.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Threshold 0.7 → 62.7% automated. *(tolerance: ± 3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Automate the confident share; people handle the rest.

        **Common wrong answer:** Automating everything at 92.8% field accuracy.

    **Leader drill:** Is the staffing saving worth it?

??? example "F11-11 · Applied · Smudged scanner"
    **Business framing:** Accuracy drops in one batch.

    **Deliverable:** Accuracy on noisy images vs clean.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 98.3% → 95.2%. *(tolerance: ± 1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Image quality drift; monitor and route.

        **Common wrong answer:** Retraining before fixing the scanner.

    **Leader drill:** What daily sample size detects this?

??? example "F11-12 · Stretch · Business-rule cross-checks"
    **Business framing:** Catch OCR errors cheaply.

    **Deliverable:** List checks for cheque fields.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Figures vs words, valid date, account exists, amount within customer's usual range, duplicate cheque number. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Rules catch errors models miss.

        **Common wrong answer:** Relying on the model alone.

    **Leader drill:** Which check gives the most protection?

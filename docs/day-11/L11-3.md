# L11.3 · Object detection: finding gaps, IoU, precision–recall and on-shelf availability

<p class="lesson-meta">Day 11 · Optional module · Basic → Advanced 75 min · Leader 30 min · Prerequisites: L6.6, L11.2 · Recipe: prompt-driven (scikit-learn, NumPy)</p>

<div class="you-are-here">📍 <b>You are here:</b> Predict an outcome for each customer/account › Images, text, sequences at scale › <a href="../../navigator/#N-ML-DL">Deep learning (awareness) — usually not better than GBM on tabular data</a> <code>N-ML-DL</code></div>

**Objectives** — you can: explain how a detector turns window scores into boxes; measure the overlap between a predicted and a true
box (**IoU**); count hits, misses and false alarms; read **average precision (AP)** at different overlap standards; and turn
detections into the business number — **on-shelf availability (OSA)** per store.

!!! info "In plain words"
    Classification says *"this window is a gap"*. Detection says *"there is a gap from pixel 80 to pixel 104"*. To grade it, we check
    how much the predicted box overlaps the box a person drew.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > *"Give me, every morning, the on-shelf availability of each store and a list of the gaps to fix — and tell me how far I can
    > trust the number."*

=== "Finance"

    > *"On a scanned cheque, find where the amount, date and signature are before reading them."* (Same technique: boxes around
    > fields.)

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 From window scores to boxes

    The augmented network from L11.2 gives each window a probability of "gap". Neighbouring windows above 0.5 are merged into one
    predicted gap, scored by their average probability.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="cv_scores" data-caption="Each point is one window. Shaded = true gaps. Switch views: clean store, dim store without augmentation (peaks vanish), dim store with augmentation."></div>

    ### 2.2 IoU: did we find the right place?

    **Intersection over union** = overlap ÷ total area covered by the two boxes. 1 = perfect, 0 = no overlap. A prediction counts
    as a **hit** if IoU ≥ 0.5 (the usual standard), otherwise it is a false alarm and the true gap is a miss.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="cv_iou" data-caption="Slide the predicted box: the IoU and the hit/miss verdict update. A box shifted by half its width already fails."></div>

    ### 2.3 Precision, recall and average precision

    | | New stores (S09, S10) | Dim camera, with augmentation | Dim camera, no augmentation |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | True gaps | 82 | 17 | 17 |
    | Recall (gaps found, IoU ≥ 0.5) | 75.6% | 88.2% | 23.5% |
    | Precision (flags that were real) | 86.1% | 93.8% | — |
    | AP at IoU 0.5 | 0.639 | 0.871 | 0.235 |
    | AP at IoU 0.75 (stricter) | 0.053 | 0.224 | — |

    **Average precision** summarises the whole precision–recall curve as you lower the score threshold (1 = perfect). It drops
    sharply at IoU 0.75 because our 4-pixel window steps can't place edges precisely — the detector knows *where* gaps are, roughly,
    not exactly. Detection benchmarks quote **mAP** (mean AP over object types and IoU levels).

    ### 2.4 The business number: on-shelf availability

    OSA = share of shelf width with product. Predicted vs actual on new stores: **85.0%** vs
    **89.5%** — the detector slightly over-flags, so it under-states availability by about
    4.5 points. Report OSA with that known bias, or
    calibrate it against a monthly manual audit (a small, random sample of shelves checked by staff).

    ### 2.5 How real detectors work

    Production systems use detectors such as YOLO, SSD or Faster R-CNN: one network outputs many candidate boxes with scores, then
    **non-maximum suppression** removes overlapping duplicates. The evaluation — IoU, precision, recall, AP — is exactly what you
    computed here.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        A detector draws boxes; IoU decides whether a box is right; precision and recall at a stated IoU tell you how many gaps you
        find and how many alerts are real.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Ask "precision and recall at what IoU?"** — numbers without the overlap standard are not comparable.
    - **Translate to operations:** missed gaps = lost sales; false alarms = wasted walks; choose the threshold by cost (L6.6).
    - **Audit the KPI:** OSA from cameras carries a known bias — calibrate with a small manual sample each month.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"If store managers act on every alert, how many walks are wasted and how many gaps are still missed?"*

## 5. Output anatomy

| Item | Read it as |
| --- | --- |
| IoU | overlap quality of one box (0–1) |
| precision / recall @ IoU 0.5 | alert quality / coverage at a stated overlap standard |
| AP, mAP | whole precision–recall curve in one number |
| OSA predicted vs audited | business KPI and its bias |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Symptom | Cause | Fix |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | good AP@0.5, poor AP@0.75 | coarse box edges | finer stride, box regression |
    | many duplicate boxes | no suppression | non-maximum suppression |
    | OSA consistently low | over-flagging | raise threshold, calibrate on audits |

=== "Leader"

    | Situation | Action | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | rollout decision | pilot stores, audited OSA, alert-cost estimate | KPI bias known and stated |
    | vendor quotes "mAP 0.8" | ask dataset, IoU levels, your images | test on your own shelves |

## 7. Planted traps and reuse drill

!!! warning "Traps"
    Precision/recall quoted without an IoU standard; OSA reported without its bias; V1 dim camera halving recall without augmentation.

**Reuse drill:** change the hit standard to IoU ≥ 0.3 and ≥ 0.7 — how do recall and AP move, and which standard fits store operations?

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R11-7 · Basic · IoU by hand"
    **Business framing:** Was this alert right?

    **Deliverable:** Truth gap 80–104, prediction 90–118: compute IoU; hit at 0.5?

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Overlap 14, union 38, IoU 0.37 → miss and false alarm. *(tolerance: ± 0.01)*

        **Model answer:** IoU = overlap ÷ union.

        **Common wrong answer:** Dividing overlap by the true box only.

    **Leader drill:** Which IoU standard suits store staff?

??? example "R11-8 · Applied · Detection quality"
    **Business framing:** How good are the gap alerts?

    **Deliverable:** Precision, recall, AP@0.5 and AP@0.75 on new stores.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Precision 86.1%, recall 75.6%, AP50 0.639, AP75 0.053. *(tolerance: ± 3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Finds most gaps; edges are coarse.

        **Common wrong answer:** Quoting AP without the IoU level.

    **Leader drill:** Is it good enough to replace walks?

??? example "R11-9 · Applied · On-shelf availability"
    **Business framing:** The KPI for the COO.

    **Deliverable:** Predicted vs labelled OSA on new stores.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 85.0% vs 89.5%. *(tolerance: ± 1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Known downward bias; calibrate with audits.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting camera OSA as exact.

    **Leader drill:** How big should the monthly audit sample be?

??? example "R11-10 · Stretch · Augmentation in detection"
    **Business framing:** Dim store gap list.

    **Deliverable:** Detection recall on S07 weeks 5–8 with and without augmentation.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 23.5% → 88.2%. *(tolerance: ± 5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Robustness carries through to the KPI.

        **Common wrong answer:** Testing only on clean stores.

    **Leader drill:** What would have happened to S07's reported OSA?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F11-6 · Basic · Field detection (design)"
    **Business framing:** Find amount, date, signature boxes.

    **Deliverable:** Evaluation metric for field detection.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** IoU per field, precision/recall at IoU ≥ 0.5, AP per field type. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Same evaluation as gap detection.

        **Common wrong answer:** Counting detections without overlap checks.

    **Leader drill:** Which field must never be missed?

??? example "F11-7 · Applied · IoU standard"
    **Business framing:** Signature box checks.

    **Deliverable:** Why a stricter IoU may be needed for small fields.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Small boxes: a few pixels of error change IoU a lot; choose the standard by what the next step needs (reading needs the whole field). *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Standard depends on use.

        **Common wrong answer:** One IoU for everything.

    **Leader drill:** Ask the vendor which IoU they report.

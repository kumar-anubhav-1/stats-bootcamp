## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Benford's law

    In many naturally occurring amounts that span several orders of magnitude, the first digit is 1 about **30.1%**
    of the time, 9 under 5%: P(d) = log₁₀(1 + 1/d). Fabricated numbers tend to spread digits evenly and favour "middle" digits and round values.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="benford" data-caption="Compare the blue bars with the red Benford curve; L2.6 shows which branch breaks the pattern."></div>

    ### 2.2 When should data follow Benford?

    | Likely to conform | Unlikely to conform |
    | --- | --- |
    | amounts spanning ≥ 2 orders of magnitude (transactions, invoices) | prices, capped or minimum values, narrow ranges |
    | sums and products of many factors | assigned numbers (IDs, phone numbers) |

    Bank 3-month transaction amounts span ~2.22 orders of magnitude — honest branches conform (peer median MAD
    0.0075). Retail line revenues span only ~1.64 orders — even honest stores deviate
    (peer median MAD 0.0305). **Compare with peers, not only with the curve.**

    ### 2.3 Measuring conformity

    **MAD** = mean absolute difference between observed and Benford digit shares. Nigrini's first-digit bands (rules of thumb): < 0.006
    close conformity, 0.006–0.012 acceptable, 0.012–0.015 marginal, > 0.015 nonconformity. Small groups deviate more by chance: a genuine
    Benford sample of n = 575 has an expected MAD of about 0.0098, so branch
    BR17 (MAD 0.0136, "marginal") is consistent with chance.

    ### 2.4 Rounding test

    Invented numbers are often round. Share of S033 revenues that are whole multiples of 0.50: **71.5%** vs
    3.1% for the median store.

    ### 2.5 The findings

    | Unit | MAD | Peers | First digit 1 | Other evidence |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Store S033 | 0.0767 | median 0.0305, max 0.0419 | 6.9% | 71.5% rounded; revenue ≠ qty × price (L1.2) |
    | Branch BR07 | 0.0746 | median 0.0075, max 0.0136 | 9.6% | χ² p ≈ 8.47e-172 (n = 1,457) |

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Benford is a screening test for naturally varying amounts. Compare units with peers, adjust for sample size, add a rounding test, and
        corroborate with other evidence. A deviation justifies an audit, never an accusation.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Escalation policy:** flag a unit only when it is far beyond peers after a sample-size adjustment *and* another indicator agrees
      (rounding, accuracy rule failures, outlier patterns). S033 meets all three.
    - **Avoid false accusations:** small branches land in "marginal" bands by chance. The recipe's expected-MAD column exists for this.
    - **Chain of custody:** audit should receive the evidence (files, configs, data hash) — not a slide.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask the junior: *"Would an honest unit of this size look like this by chance?"* If they cannot answer with the expected MAD, they are
        not ready to escalate.

## 5. Output anatomy — `rcp_benford`

| Column | Meaning | Read as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MAD | deviation from Benford | rank units by it |
| expected MAD at this n | deviation a genuine sample would show | subtract before judging |
| Nigrini band | rule-of-thumb label | only meaningful for data that should conform |
| peer z (excess MAD) | distance from peers | > 5 is far outside |
| rounded share | invented-number signal | compare with peer median |
| χ² p | formal test | tiny for large n — not a ranking tool |

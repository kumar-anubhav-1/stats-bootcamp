## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Law of large numbers (LLN)

    The average of many independent draws gets closer to the true mean. Running mean of randomly drawn baskets:
    **167.4** after 10 draws → 58.1 after 100 → 50.8 after 3,000 (true
    50.52). With heavy tails, small samples can be wildly wrong.

    ### 2.2 Sampling distribution and standard error

    If you repeated a study many times, the estimate would vary. Its standard deviation is the **standard error**: for a mean,
    SE = σ ÷ √n. Simulated vs formula SE for n = 30 baskets: 12.61 vs 12.34 — the formula works.

    ### 2.3 Central limit theorem (CLT)

    Averages of many independent values are approximately normal, whatever the shape of the data (if the variance is finite). How fast
    depends on the tails. Basket value has skew 10.5; skewness of simulated sample means:

    | n | skew of the mean | SE (simulated) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | 5 | 4.87 | 30.06 |
    | 30 | 1.93 | 12.61 |
    | 200 | 0.73 | 4.75 |

    "n ≥ 30" is a rule of thumb for *moderately* skewed data. With heavy tails, use larger samples, a bootstrap (Day 3) or a transformation.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="clt" data-caption="Drag n: averages of real, skewed NorthStar baskets become bell-shaped as n grows — the CLT on our own data."></div>

    ### 2.4 Monte Carlo simulation

    When an outcome depends on uncertain inputs, **simulate**: draw the inputs many times from their distributions, compute the outcome each
    time, and look at the distribution of outcomes.

    **Rollout P&L.** The Hair Care test estimated a lift of 13.6% with SE 4.1%. Suppose the rollout
    costs the margin on a 10% lift (16,314 per year on 81,572 baseline units). Drawing the lift
    100,000 times: mean P&L **5,895**, 90% range -5,136 to 16,980, and
    **P(loss) = 19.1%**.

    **Portfolio loss.** With PD 4.0%, random LGD and a shared economic factor, simulated annual loss has mean
    8,910,153 and 99th percentile 23,530,395 — **2.64×** the average year.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        LLN: averages settle as n grows. CLT: averages become normal — slowly for heavy tails. SE = σ/√n. Monte Carlo turns uncertainty
        into probabilities of outcomes you care about.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Early numbers lie.** A day-one KPI from a few dozen transactions is noise. Set minimum sample sizes before anyone looks.
    - **Ask for P(loss), not only expected P&L.** A positive expectation with a 19.1% chance of loss is a risk-appetite decision.
    - **Correlation fattens tails.** Independent defaults would make bad years rare; a shared economic factor makes P99 several times the mean.
    - **Garbage in, distribution out.** Monte Carlo is only as good as the input distributions and their correlations — ask to see them.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        When a junior presents a single forecast number, ask: *"What is the 5th and 95th percentile, and what drives the spread?"* Then ask for
        a Monte Carlo if they can't answer.

## 5. Output anatomy — a Monte Carlo

| Output | Meaning | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mean outcome | expected value | is it positive? |
| P5–P95 | plausible range | is the downside acceptable? |
| P(loss) | risk of a bad decision | within risk appetite? |
| input assumptions | drivers of the spread | which ones are guesses? |
| seed, number of draws | reproducibility | rerun gives the same numbers? |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Pattern | Meaning | Next |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | sample means still skewed at n = 30 | heavy tails | larger n / bootstrap |
    | simulated SE ≈ formula SE | assumptions fine | use the formula |
    | Monte Carlo results change between runs | too few draws or no seed | more draws, set a seed |

=== "Leader"

    | Pattern | Action — why / when / when not | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Positive EV, P(loss) 10–30% | **Stage the decision** (pilot, holdout) when reversible; *don't* when irreversible without more data | Risk appetite statement |
    | Inputs assumed independent | **Ask for correlations** (economy, season) | Stress tests |
    | Point forecasts in plans | **Require ranges** | Plan at P50, buffer for P90 |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R2-10 · Basic · Watching the central limit theorem"
    **Business framing:** Basket value is extremely skewed (skew 10.5). How skewed are averages of n baskets?

    **Deliverable:** Skewness and SE of 4,000 simulated sample means for n = 5, 30, 200; SE formula σ/√n.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Skew of means: n=5 4.87, n=30 1.93, n=200 0.73. SE (simulated vs formula): n=30 12.61 vs 12.34. *(tolerance: ± 0.3 skew; ± 0.5 SE)*

        **Model answer:** Means become more symmetric as n grows, but with heavy tails 'n = 30' is not enough — the sampling distribution is still clearly skewed. The SE formula works well at every n.

        **Common wrong answer:** Assuming n ≥ 30 always makes means normal.

    **Leader drill:** When would you insist on a bootstrap instead of a t-interval?

??? example "R2-11 · Applied · The law of large numbers"
    **Business framing:** Track the running mean of randomly drawn baskets.

    **Deliverable:** Running mean after 10, 100 and 3,000 draws vs the population mean.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 167.4 → 58.1 → 50.8 (population 50.52). *(tolerance: seed-dependent; direction must match)*

        **Model answer:** Small samples of heavy-tailed data can be wildly off; the average settles as n grows. 'Early results' from a few stores or days are not reliable.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting a weekly KPI from a handful of transactions as if it were stable.

    **Leader drill:** How do you stop teams reacting to day-one numbers?

??? example "R2-12 · Stretch · Monte Carlo rollout P&L"
    **Business framing:** Use the Hair Care test result (L3.4 preview) to simulate the annual rollout P&L, where rollout costs equal the margin on a 10% lift.

    **Deliverable:** Simulated lift ~ Normal(estimate, SE); P(loss), mean P&L, 5th–95th percentile.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Lift 13.6% ± 4.1% (SE); P(loss) = 19.1%; mean P&L 5,895; 90% range -5,136 to 16,980. *(tolerance: ± 1 pp for P(loss))*

        **Model answer:** The expected P&L is positive, but there is about a one-in-five chance of losing money. Monte Carlo turns an interval into a decision-relevant risk number.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting only the expected P&L.

    **Leader drill:** Would you approve the rollout at this risk? What would reduce it?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F2-10 · Stretch · Monte Carlo portfolio loss"
    **Business framing:** Simulate 2,000 years of losses with one shared economic factor (correlation 0.05), PD 4%, LGD ~ Beta(3, 2).

    **Deliverable:** Expected loss, 99th percentile loss, and their ratio.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** EL 8,910,153; P99 23,530,395 (2.64× EL). *(tolerance: ± 5%)*

        **Model answer:** Correlated defaults fatten the loss tail: a bad year costs several times an average year. This is the intuition behind capital and VaR (Day 9).

        **Common wrong answer:** Assuming defaults are independent (tail far too thin).

    **Leader drill:** Which number belongs in the budget and which in the capital plan?

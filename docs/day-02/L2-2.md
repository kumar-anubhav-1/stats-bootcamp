**Objectives** — you can: match a count or yes/no process to the Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, Poisson, negative binomial,
hypergeometric, discrete uniform or beta-binomial distribution; check over-dispersion; and use each to answer a business question.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "How variable are weekly Dairy sales in a store — and what distribution should safety stock assume?"

=== "Finance"

    > "Do vintages differ in default rate more than chance would allow? Is our audit sample big enough?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The discrete family

    | Distribution | Counts… | Mean / variance | Course use |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Bernoulli(p)** | one yes/no | p / p(1 − p) | did this account default? |
    | **Binomial(n, p)** | successes in n independent trials | np / np(1 − p) | defaults in a vintage |
    | **Geometric(p)** | trials until the first success | 1/p | baskets until the first promo basket |
    | **Poisson(λ)** | events in a fixed exposure | λ / λ | claims per policy |
    | **Negative binomial** | counts with extra variation | μ / μ + μ²/r (> μ) | weekly SKU units |
    | **Hypergeometric** | successes when sampling *without replacement* from a finite lot | — | audit sampling |
    | **Discrete uniform** | equally likely values | — | random store allocation |
    | **Beta-binomial** | binomial whose p varies between groups | > binomial variance | conversion that varies by store |

    ### 2.2 Over-dispersion — the most useful check

    For counts, compare variance with the mean. Poisson requires them to be equal. Weekly Dairy units in store S001: mean
    71.0, variance 310.2 → **dispersion 4.4**. Across regular SKUs chain-wide
    the median dispersion is 7.1. Retail demand is almost always over-dispersed → negative binomial.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="dist_negbin" data-caption="Drag k down: the same average (2) with far more zero months and occasional spikes — that is over-dispersion."></div>

    ### 2.3 Binomial: is there one default rate?

    If all accounts shared one PD, vintage default counts would vary only binomially. Vintage rates range from
    1.93% to 10.95% (chi-square p ≈ 9.22e-114). Excluding the two newest vintages,
    p = 0.203: the older book behaves like one binomial process, the newest does not.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="dist_binomial" data-caption="Drag p: how many of 200 new loans default if the true rate is 1%, 5% or 15%?"></div>

    ### 2.4 Hypergeometric: sampling without replacement

    Audit draws 50 of 200 files; 10 are faulty. P(find at least one) = **94.8%** (hypergeometric) vs
    92.3% (binomial approximation). For 95% detection you need 51 files.

    ### 2.5 Poisson and its failure mode

    Claims per policy: mean 0.0865, dispersion 1.154. The zero share fits well
    (92.26% vs 91.71%), but Poisson predicts only 0.353% of policies with 2+ claims
    against **0.8%** observed. Risk heterogeneity lives in the tail.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="dist_poisson" data-caption="Drag λ: small averages give skewed counts; large averages look bell-shaped (the Poisson → Normal limit)."></div>

    ### 2.6 Extra variation between groups

    Store return rates vary with SD 1.08 pp, against 0.84 pp expected from binomial
    noise alone: stores genuinely differ (a beta-binomial view), partly because of the returns-abuse cluster.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Yes/no → Bernoulli/binomial. Counts → Poisson only if variance ≈ mean; otherwise negative binomial. Sampling a large share of a
        small population → hypergeometric. Groups with different underlying rates → beta-binomial / hierarchical.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Assuming Poisson for demand under-stocks.** Variance 4.4× the mean means safety stock based on Poisson is far too low.
    - **"One PD" is a modelling choice to test, not assume.** The newest vintages break it — provisioning must reflect that.
    - **Audit plans need the right distribution and an explicit detection target.** Ask: what error rate are we trying to detect, with what confidence?
    - **Tails matter more than means** for pricing multi-claim risk.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        For any count a junior models, ask for the variance-to-mean ratio first. It takes ten seconds and decides the model.

## 3. When to use which (Navigator)

!!! tip "What else could I try?"
    - **[Poisson](../../navigator/#N-DIST-POIS)** — Counts with variance ≈ mean (claims per policy, baskets per hour).
    - **[Negative Binomial](../../navigator/#N-DIST-NB)** — Over-dispersed counts (weekly SKU units, claims with heterogeneous risk).
    - **[Bernoulli / Binomial (Beta-Binomial if the rate varies across groups)](../../navigator/#N-DIST-BIN)** — Defaults in a vintage, conversions in an A/B test.
    - **[Hypergeometric (acceptance sampling)](../../navigator/#N-DIST-HYPER)** — Audit samples drawn without replacement from a finite file population.

## 5. Output anatomy — count fit

| Number | Meaning | Read as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dispersion 4.4 | variance ÷ mean | 1 = Poisson; > 1.5 usually needs NB |
| z ≈ (dispersion − 1)·√((n − 1)/2) | rough test of dispersion | > 2: over-dispersion is real |
| ΔAIC | fit difference | > 10: clear winner |
| zeros observed vs model | excess zeros? | many more zeros → zero-inflated / hurdle |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Pattern | Meaning | Next |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | variance ≈ mean | Poisson OK | proceed |
    | variance ≫ mean | over-dispersion | negative binomial |
    | many more zeros than predicted | zero inflation / intermittent | Croston, hurdle models (Day 7) |
    | group rates vary more than binomial | real between-group differences | beta-binomial / hierarchical |

=== "Leader"

    | Pattern | Action | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Safety stock built on Poisson | **Rebuild on NB or empirical variance** | Retail demand rarely Poisson |
    | Vintage rates inconsistent with one PD | **Segment PD by vintage/channel** | Monitor vintage curves monthly |
    | Audit sample chosen by habit | **Specify detection target**, size with hypergeometric | — |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R2-3 · Basic · Poisson or negative binomial?"
    **Business framing:** Store S001 needs a distribution for weekly Dairy units (safety stock, Day 7).

    **Deliverable:** Run `retail_fit_weekly_units.yaml`: mean, variance, dispersion, AIC of both families.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Mean 71.0, variance 310.2, dispersion 4.4; negative binomial wins clearly. Median dispersion across regular SKUs (chain): 7.1. *(tolerance: ± 0.1)*

        **Model answer:** Variance several times the mean → over-dispersed. A Poisson would understate week-to-week variability and lead to too little safety stock.

        **Common wrong answer:** Assuming Poisson because the data are counts.

    **Leader drill:** What is the business cost of using the Poisson here?

??? example "R2-4 · Applied · Waiting for a promotion basket"
    **Business framing:** How many baskets until a shopper's first basket containing a promoted item?

    **Deliverable:** Share of baskets with a promo item (p); geometric mean wait 1/p; P(at least one within 3 baskets).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** p = 49.4%; mean wait 2.02 baskets; P(within 3) = 87.1%. *(tolerance: ± 0.2)*

        **Model answer:** The geometric model assumes each basket independently has the same probability — reasonable as a first approximation; real shoppers differ (promo hunters), which a mixture would capture.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using 1 − p³ instead of 1 − (1 − p)³.

    **Leader drill:** Where would the independence assumption break for CRM targeting?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F2-3 · Basic · Are vintage defaults binomial?"
    **Business framing:** If every account had the same 4% default probability, vintage default counts would vary only by binomial chance.

    **Deliverable:** Default-rate range across vintages; chi-square test of equal rates for all vintages and for vintages before 2025Q2.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Rates 1.93%–10.95%; all vintages p ≈ 9.22e-114; excluding 2025Q2–Q3 p = 0.203. *(tolerance: ± 0.05)*

        **Model answer:** Older vintages look like one binomial process; the two newest do not — something changed (population drift, F11).

        **Common wrong answer:** Assuming one PD for all vintages.

    **Leader drill:** What does this imply for the PD used in provisioning?

??? example "F2-4 · Applied · Audit sampling"
    **Business framing:** Internal audit samples 50 of 200 loan files; 10 contain a documentation error.

    **Deliverable:** P(at least one error found) — hypergeometric vs binomial approximation; sample size for 95% detection.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Hypergeometric 94.8% vs binomial 92.3%; n = 51 files for 95%. *(tolerance: ± 0.1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Sampling a quarter of a small population without replacement makes detection more likely than the binomial suggests. Use the hypergeometric for audit plans on small populations.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using the binomial when sampling a large share of a finite population.

    **Leader drill:** Is 50 files enough? What error rate could it miss?

??? example "F2-5 · Stretch · Claims per policy"
    **Business framing:** Is claim frequency Poisson?

    **Deliverable:** Mean claims per policy; dispersion; observed vs Poisson share with 0 and with 2+ claims.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Mean 0.0865; dispersion 1.154; zero claims 92.26% vs 91.71%; 2+ claims 0.8% vs 0.353%. *(tolerance: ± 0.05)*

        **Model answer:** Mild over-dispersion, but the Poisson under-predicts multi-claim policies by more than half — the policies that matter most for pricing (F17).

        **Common wrong answer:** Accepting Poisson because the mean and the zero share look fine.

    **Leader drill:** How would mis-modelling multi-claim policies affect premiums?

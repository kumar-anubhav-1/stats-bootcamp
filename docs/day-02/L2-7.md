## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Data as vectors and matrices

    A table is a **matrix**: rows = observations, columns = variables. A column is a **vector** (one number per customer). A basket ×
    subcategory table is a matrix of 0/1 entries.

    ### 2.2 Dot product and cosine similarity

    The dot product sums element-wise products; dividing by the vectors' lengths gives the **cosine** of the angle between them (1 = same
    direction, 0 = unrelated). Over baskets: cos(Pasta, Pasta Sauce) = **0.575**; cos(Pasta, Shampoo) =
    0.113. Recommendation engines and embeddings (Day 9) are built on this.

    ### 2.3 Multiplication, transpose, inverse, determinant, rank

    | Idea | Plain meaning | Where it shows up |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Xβ** | each prediction = weighted sum of a row's values | every linear model |
    | **Transpose Xᵀ** | swap rows and columns | XᵀX = cross-products of variables |
    | **Inverse A⁻¹** | "undo" A; exists only if A is not singular | solving for coefficients |
    | **Determinant** | volume scaling; 0 = singular (columns redundant) | correlation matrix det = 0.0762 |
    | **Rank** | number of truly independent columns | duplicated information lowers rank |

    ### 2.4 Eigenvalues: a collinearity detector

    Correlation matrix of income, limit, bureau score, DTI and inquiries — eigenvalues **[1.96, 1.131, 1.009, 0.86, 0.04]**. They sum to the number of
    variables; one near zero means a combination of variables is almost constant. Its eigenvector has loadings income_annual 0.71 · limit_or_loan_amount -0.71 · bureau_score -0 · dti -0 · inquiries_6m 0:
    *income − limit* barely varies — the two are near duplicates. Ratio largest/smallest = 49.0; regression coefficients on
    both will be unstable (Day 5, VIF).

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="matrix_transform" data-caption="Slide through matrices: watch the grid deform, the determinant (area scale) and the eigenvectors (red). The near-singular case is what collinear features do to regression."></div>

    ### 2.5 Least squares as projection

    Linear regression chooses β so that Xβ is the closest point to y in the space spanned by X's columns: the **projection**. Residuals are
    then perpendicular (orthogonal) to every column: max |Xᵀ residual| = 6.6e-14 ≈ 0. Solving the normal equations (XᵀX)β = Xᵀy
    and a numerical solver give the same β = [2.221, -1.942, -0.167] — but solvers use QR/SVD because inverting XᵀX is unstable when columns are
    nearly collinear.

    ### 2.6 SVD and PCA

    Any matrix can be decomposed into directions ranked by how much variation they capture (SVD). On customers' category-spend shares,
    two directions capture **47.0%** of the variation — the idea behind PCA and segmentation (Day 8).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Vectors and matrices are just tables. Cosine = similarity. A near-zero eigenvalue = redundant variables → unstable coefficients.
        Regression = projection. SVD/PCA = summarise many correlated columns in a few directions.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Unstable coefficients are a symptom of redundancy**, not of a bad analyst. Ask for the eigenvalues or VIFs before arguing about signs.
    - **"Never invert matrices by hand"** is a code-review rule: use library solvers.
    - **Dimensionality reduction loses interpretability** — ask what each component means in business terms.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        When coefficient signs flip between model versions, ask the junior for the correlation matrix's smallest eigenvalue and its loadings.
        It usually names the culprit pair.

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R2-14 · Applied · Cosine similarity of products"
    **Business framing:** Which subcategories appear in the same baskets?

    **Deliverable:** Cosine similarity of basket vectors for Pasta vs Pasta Sauce and Pasta vs Shampoo.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Pasta–Sauce 0.575; Pasta–Shampoo 0.113. *(tolerance: ± 0.01)*

        **Model answer:** Each subcategory is a vector over baskets; cosine measures how often they point the same way. The planted association (R21) shows up — Day 8 turns this into market-basket rules.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using raw co-occurrence counts, which favour big categories.

    **Leader drill:** How would merchandising use this?

??? example "R2-15 · Stretch · SVD of shopping profiles"
    **Business framing:** Customers' category spend shares (customers with 3+ baskets).

    **Deliverable:** Share of variance captured by the first two singular vectors.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 47.0% from 2 components (520 customers × 12 categories). *(tolerance: ± 1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Two directions summarise almost half of the variation in shopping mixes — the basis for PCA and segmentation (Day 8).

        **Common wrong answer:** Running SVD on raw spend (dominated by big spenders) instead of shares.

    **Leader drill:** What would you name the first two directions after looking at their loadings?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F2-12 · Applied · Eigenvalues reveal collinearity"
    **Business framing:** Correlation matrix of income, limit, bureau score, DTI, inquiries.

    **Deliverable:** Eigenvalues; ratio largest/smallest; loadings of the smallest eigenvector; determinant.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Eigenvalues [1.96, 1.131, 1.009, 0.86, 0.04]; ratio 49.0; smallest-vector loadings income_annual 0.71 · limit_or_loan_amount -0.71 · bureau_score -0 · dti -0 · inquiries_6m 0; det 0.0762. *(tolerance: ± 0.01)*

        **Model answer:** One eigenvalue near zero means the matrix is nearly singular: income and limit (loadings ≈ ±0.71) almost duplicate each other. Regression coefficients on both will be unstable (F06, Day 5).

        **Common wrong answer:** Reading a small eigenvalue as an unimportant variable — it marks a redundant combination.

    **Leader drill:** Which variable would you drop or combine, and how would you explain it to the business?

??? example "F2-13 · Stretch · Least squares as a projection"
    **Business framing:** Fit log(units) on log(price ratio) and promo for one SKU in the price extract.

    **Deliverable:** Coefficients from the normal equations (XᵀX)β = Xᵀy vs a least-squares solver; largest |Xᵀ residual|.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** β = [2.221, -1.942, -0.167]; methods agree = True; max |Xᵀe| = 6.6e-14. *(tolerance: ± 0.01)*

        **Model answer:** Residuals are orthogonal to every column of X — the fitted values are the projection of y onto the space spanned by the predictors. Solvers use QR/SVD rather than inverting XᵀX because inversion is unstable when columns are nearly collinear.

        **Common wrong answer:** Computing (XᵀX)⁻¹ explicitly on collinear data.

    **Leader drill:** Why should analysts never hand-invert matrices in production code?

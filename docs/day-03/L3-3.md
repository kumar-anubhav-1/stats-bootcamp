## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The logic in five steps

    1. **State the claim to be tested as H0** ("no difference") and the alternative **H1** ("a difference", or "bigger" for a
       one-sided test chosen *in advance* for a business reason).
    2. **Choose α** (the false-alarm rate you accept, usually 0.05) and the test, **before** seeing results.
    3. **Compute a test statistic**: how many standard errors the observed difference is from H0.
    4. **p-value** = the probability of a result at least this extreme **if H0 were true**.
    5. **Decide and report the effect with its CI** — the p-value alone answers almost nothing a manager asks.

    ```mermaid
    flowchart LR
      H0[Step 1 · Null: the promo did nothing] --> S[Step 2 · Statistic: lift ÷ its standard error] --> N[Step 3 · How would it vary if H0 were true?] --> P[Step 4 · p-value: how surprising is what we saw?] --> D[Step 5 · Decide with effect size, CI and a practical threshold]
    ```


    ### 2.2 Three worked examples

    | Question | Result | p | 95% CI of difference | Reading |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Fri–Sat vs Mon–Thu basket (L3.4 data) | 49.98 vs 50.54 | 0.8308 | -5.76 to 4.63 | no evidence of a difference; a difference of ± 5 is still compatible |
    | Default 2025Q1 vs 2024Q4 vintage | 2.88% vs 3.0% | 0.803 | -1.01 to 0.78 pp | no evidence of a change |
    | Default 2025Q2 vs 2025Q1 vintage | 8.82% vs 2.88% | 4.6e-20 | 4.62 to 7.25 pp | strong evidence: default roughly tripled |

    ### 2.3 What a p-value is — and is not

    **Is:** how surprising the data would be if there were no effect.

    **Is not** (the classic misreadings, ASA statement 2016):

    1. the probability that H0 is true;
    2. the probability the result is due to chance;
    3. a measure of effect size or importance;
    4. proof of no effect when p > 0.05;
    5. evidence the result will replicate at the same strength;
    6. meaningful when the test was chosen after looking at the data.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="pvalue" data-caption="Drag the observed z: the p-value is the red area — how surprising the result would be if the promotion did nothing."></div>

    ### 2.4 Statistical vs practical significance

    Monthly transaction count: branch-acquired accounts 19.77 vs online 20.14, using
    **417,813** panel rows → difference -0.364 (95% CI -0.492 to -0.237),
    **p = 2.1e-08**, Cohen's d = -0.017. Two problems: the effect is a third of a transaction per month — irrelevant
    — and the rows are not independent (about 19 months from each of 22,286 accounts). Analysed at account level the
    p-value is **0.62**. Big n makes trivial differences significant; pseudo-replication makes them look even surer.

    ### 2.5 Errors and what they cost

    | | H0 true (no effect) | H0 false (real effect) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Reject H0 | **Type I error** (false positive), rate α | correct (power = 1 − β) |
    | Do not reject | correct | **Type II error** (false negative), rate β |

    **Seeing α in action (A/A test).** We split the 40 stores randomly into two halves 2,000 times — no treatment at
    all — and tested Snacks sales growth. **5.1%** of these A/A tests were "significant" at α = 0.05, as designed.
    The p-values were spread evenly from 0 to 1 (counts per 0.1 bin: 192, 203, 194, 207, 194, 225, 196, 189, 207, 193): **under H0, every p-value is
    equally likely.**

    | Business case | Type I cost | Type II cost | Lean |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | National rollout of a promo | spend on a dud | miss a winner | balanced (α 0.05, power 0.8) |
    | Fraud rule that blocks cards | angry genuine customers | fraud losses | depends on cost ratio (L6.6) |
    | Claim to a regulator | reputational, legal | missed improvement | small α (0.01) |

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        A p-value measures how surprising the data are under "no effect". It says nothing about size, importance or the probability
        that the effect is real. Report effect + CI; read "not significant" as "not established".

    **Judgement points**

    - **Demand the plan before the result.** H0, H1, unit, outcome, α and test written down first. Tests chosen after looking
      inflate false positives (L3.7).
    - **Translate the two errors into money** for each decision; that sets α and power, not convention.
    - **Watch for big-n theatre.** p = 2.1e-08 on 417,813 rows for a third of a transaction a month is a
      reporting failure, not a finding.
    - **"No significant change" in 2025Q1 is not "no change".** The CI (-1.01 to 0.78 pp) shows
      what the data can and cannot rule out.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ban the sentence "the result is significant" without three companions: the effect in business units, its CI, and the unit
        of analysis.

## 5. Output anatomy — a test result

| Line | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| H0 / H1 and sidedness | written before the data were seen? |
| Test and unit | right test (Navigator) at the independent unit? |
| Statistic, df | distance from H0 in SE units |
| p-value | surprise under H0 — not importance |
| Effect + 95% CI | **what the decision needs** |
| Assumption checks | independence first; normality matters least at large n |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Result | Effect size | Say |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | p < α | large | evidence of a meaningful effect |
    | p < α | trivial | statistically detectable, practically irrelevant |
    | p ≥ α | CI narrow around 0 | evidence the effect is small |
    | p ≥ α | CI wide | inconclusive — need more data (L3.6) |

=== "Leader"

    | Team says | You say | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | "p = 0.06, so no effect" | "inconclusive; what does the CI allow?" | absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence |
    | "p = 0.0001, huge effect" | "how big, in what units?" | p ≠ effect size |
    | "we tested one-sided after seeing the direction" | re-run two-sided | sidedness fixed in advance |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R3-6 · Basic · State the hypotheses first"
    **Business framing:** Store operations ask: are Friday–Saturday baskets bigger?

    **Deliverable:** H0, H1 (two-sided), unit, outcome, test and α written before testing; then the result with CI.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** H0: mean Fri–Sat basket = mean Mon–Thu basket; H1: they differ. Unit basket; Welch t (Mann-Whitney as robustness); α 0.05. Result 49.98 vs 50.54, p = 0.8308, CI -5.76 to 4.63. *(tolerance: p ± 0.02)*

        **Model answer:** No evidence that weekend baskets are bigger (the busier weekend comes from more baskets, not bigger ones). The CI shows differences of up to about ± 5 cannot be excluded.

        **Common wrong answer:** Concluding 'weekend has no effect on baskets' — not established at that precision.

    **Leader drill:** How would you phrase this finding so store operations don't over-read it?

??? example "R3-7 · Applied · An A/A test shows what α means"
    **Business framing:** The analytics lead wants to show the team why 1 in 20 tests is a false alarm.

    **Deliverable:** 2,000 random 20/20 splits of the 40 stores (no treatment); Welch test on Snacks growth (weeks 70–77 ÷ 54–69). False-positive rate and p-value histogram.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** False-positive rate 5.1%; p-values roughly uniform (bins: 192, 203, 194, 207, 194, 225, 196, 189, 207, 193). *(tolerance: ± 1.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** When nothing happens, p-values are uniformly distributed, so about 5% fall below 0.05 by construction. α is the false-alarm rate you chose.

        **Common wrong answer:** Expecting zero significant A/A tests.

    **Leader drill:** How would you use A/A tests to validate a new experimentation platform?

??? example "R3-8 · Stretch · The many-categories trap in A/A data"
    **Business framing:** The same analyst now checks all categories in every A/A split.

    **Deliverable:** Share of A/A splits where at least one of 11 categories is 'significant'; compare with 1 − 0.95^k.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 38.1% observed vs 43.1% for independent tests. *(tolerance: ± 3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Testing many outcomes guarantees false discoveries. The observed rate is slightly below the independent-test formula because category growths are correlated.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting the 'winning' category as a finding.

    **Leader drill:** What rule would you set for experiment readouts with many metrics?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F3-6 · Basic · Did default change? Two vintage comparisons"
    **Business framing:** Risk committee asks whether default changed between recent vintages.

    **Deliverable:** 2025Q1 vs 2024Q4 and 2025Q2 vs 2025Q1 — rates, difference with CI, p-value; plain-English reading for each.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Q1 vs Q4: 2.88% vs 3.0%, CI -1.01 to 0.78 pp, p = 0.803. Q2 vs Q1: 8.82% vs 2.88%, CI 4.62 to 7.25 pp, p = 4.6e-20. *(tolerance: ± 0.05 pp)*

        **Model answer:** No evidence of change into 2025Q1; a large, highly significant rise in 2025Q2 — roughly a tripling. Investigate what changed in origination (channel mix, verification — L3.4, L9.6).

        **Common wrong answer:** Saying 2025Q1 'proved' default was stable.

    **Leader drill:** What three questions do you ask before the risk committee meets?

??? example "F3-7 · Applied · Big n, tiny effect"
    **Business framing:** An MI report says online customers transact 'significantly more' than branch customers.

    **Deliverable:** Monthly transaction counts by channel on panel rows (difference, CI, p, Cohen's d) and on one row per account.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Panel rows (417,813): 19.77 vs 20.14, p = 2.1e-08, d = -0.017. Account level (22,286): p = 0.62. *(tolerance: p within an order of magnitude)*

        **Model answer:** The difference is a third of a transaction per month and the panel rows are pseudo-replicated. At the independent unit there is no evidence of a difference.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting 'highly significant' as if it meant 'important'.

    **Leader drill:** What reporting standard would prevent this in MI packs?

??? example "F3-8 · Stretch · Costing Type I and Type II errors"
    **Business framing:** A fraud rule will block cards automatically if a test shows it reduces fraud.

    **Deliverable:** Describe the Type I and Type II errors, their business costs, and propose α and power with a justification.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Type I: deploy a useless rule — customer friction and lost spend. Type II: miss a working rule — fraud losses. Choose α and power from the cost ratio, not convention. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** If blocking genuine customers is expensive (churn, complaints), use a smaller α and require a larger effect; if fraud losses dominate, accept higher α for more power. Document the trade-off.

        **Common wrong answer:** Defaulting to α = 0.05 without discussion.

    **Leader drill:** Who in the organisation should sign off α for this decision?

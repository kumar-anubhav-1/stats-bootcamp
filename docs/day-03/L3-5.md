# L3.5 · More than two groups and categorical tests

<p class="lesson-meta">Day 3 · Part B · Basic → Advanced 75 min · Leader 30 min · Prerequisites: L3.3, L3.4 · Recipe: <code>rcp_multi_group_test</code></p>

<div class="you-are-here">📍 <b>You are here:</b> Compare groups (is A different from B?) › No, or already checked › Continuous (amount, units, score) › More than 2 groups › <a href="../../navigator/#N-TEST-KG">One-way ANOVA (+ Levene); Welch ANOVA + Games-Howell if variances differ; Kruskal-Wallis + Dunn if non-normal</a> <code>N-TEST-KG</code></div>

**Objectives** — you can: compare three or more groups with ANOVA, Welch ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis; check variances with Levene; find
*which* groups differ with corrected post-hoc tests; test association between categorical variables with chi-square, Fisher's exact
and McNemar; read η², Cramér's V and standardised residuals; and judge which differences matter.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "Does basket value differ by region?"

=== "Finance"

    > "Is default associated with employment type? And did delinquency on the same accounts rise between month 6 and month 12?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Why not run t-tests on every pair?

    Four regions mean six pairs. Six tests at α = 0.05 give roughly a 26% chance of at least one false alarm. Test **once** for "any
    difference", then compare pairs with a correction.

    ### 2.2 Basket value by region

    | Region | Baskets | Mean | Median | SD |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | East | 657 | 48.38 | 39.23 | 44.6 |
    | North | 492 | 40.94 | 32.78 | 39.5 |
    | South | 1,030 | 52.14 | 40.5 | 84.4 |
    | West | 984 | 55.03 | 41.6 | 71.1 |
    

    | Test | Assumes | Result |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | One-way ANOVA | equal variances, roughly normal | F = 5.2, p = 0.0014 |
    | Levene | — (tests the equal-variance assumption) | p = 0.05: borderline, yet SDs range from 39.5 to 84.4 — do not rely on equal variances |
    | **Welch ANOVA** | unequal variances allowed | F = 9.29, p = 4.3e-06 |
    | **Kruskal-Wallis** | ranks — robust to skew | H = 28.7, p = 2.6e-06 |

    Baskets are heavily skewed, so the recipe picks **Kruskal-Wallis** as primary. All tests agree: regions differ.

    **Which regions?** Pairwise Mann-Whitney with Holm correction: significant pairs **East–North, North–South, North–West**. (Tukey's
    HSD on means finds North–South, North–West; Games-Howell is the unequal-variance version.) North has smaller
    baskets than everyone.

    **Does it matter?** η² = **0.0049**: region explains half a percent of the variation in basket value.

    **Why is North lower?** 57.3% of North's baskets come from small Express stores, against
    13.9% elsewhere. Compare like with like — North's median basket minus other regions' median, *within*
    format: Express -1.66 · Hyper 1.39 · Super 7.28. Within formats North is not consistently lower at all. The regional "difference" is
    **store mix** — a reason to look at North's store portfolio, not at North's shoppers.

    ### 2.3 Default by employment type — chi-square

    | Employment | Default rate |
    | --- | --- |
    | contract | 4.48% |
    | retired | 4.01% |
    | salaried | 3.79% |
    | self-employed | 3.39% |
    | student | 6.48% |
    

    χ² = 29.7, df = 4, p = 5.5e-06. Cramér's V = **0.034** — a very weak
    association overall. The **standardised residual** for students who defaulted is **+4.6** (|r| > 2
    marks a cell that drives the result): the association is mostly "students default more". Smallest expected count
    45.0 — chi-square is valid.

    ### 2.4 Small counts — Fisher's exact

    Branch BR18: 8 defaults out of 562
    vs 1.1% in the rest of region R4 (expected about 6.2).
    With expected counts this small, use **Fisher's exact test**: p = 0.5119. Compared with its own region the branch is
    unremarkable — its low rate reflects the region's customers.

    ### 2.5 Paired categories — McNemar

    The same 20,524 accounts were observed at month 6 and month 12 on book. 30+ days past due: 0.57% at
    month 6, 3.42% at month 12. A chi-square test would wrongly treat these as independent groups. **McNemar** uses only
    the accounts that changed: 701 became delinquent, 118 recovered → p = 6.1e-92. Delinquency
    builds with time on book (L8.7).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        One omnibus test, then corrected pairwise comparisons, then effect size. Welch or Kruskal when variances or shapes differ.
        Chi-square for associations (Fisher when counts are small, McNemar when paired). Read η² / Cramér's V before acting.

    **Judgement points**

    - **"Significant" across groups ≠ a strategy.** η² = 0.0049 for region; Cramér's V = 0.034 for employment.
      Ask what share of the outcome the grouping explains and what you would do differently per group.
    - **Group differences are often mix.** The low-default branch sits in a low-default region; North's small baskets are its Express-heavy
      store mix. Stratify before attributing (L2.4, L4.4).
    - **Using employment type in credit decisions** is lawful in most jurisdictions but check fairness and proxy effects (L9.5).

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        When a junior shows a significant ANOVA, ask *"which groups, after correction, by how much, and what explains it?"*

## 5. Output anatomy — `rcp_multi_group_test`

| Section | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| Group summary | n, mean, median, SD, skew — eyeball before testing |
| Overall tests | ANOVA / Welch / Kruskal / Levene; the primary is bold in the narrative |
| η² or Cramér's V | share of variation / strength of association |
| Standardised residuals (categorical) | cells with |r| > 2 drive the association |
| Pairwise (Holm) | which pairs differ after correction |
| Row accounting | rows → units (baskets); duplicates removed |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Outcome × groups | Test | Post-hoc |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | numeric, roughly normal, equal SDs | ANOVA | Tukey HSD |
    | numeric, unequal SDs | Welch ANOVA | Games-Howell |
    | numeric, skewed | Kruskal-Wallis | Dunn / pairwise Mann-Whitney + Holm |
    | categorical × categorical | chi-square + Cramér's V | residuals / pairwise + Holm |
    | small expected counts | Fisher's exact | — |
    | same units, two binary measurements | McNemar | — |

=== "Leader"

    | Pattern | Response | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | significant, tiny η² / V | note, don't build strategy on it | η² < 0.01 is negligible |
    | one group drives it (residuals) | focus there; check mix | — |
    | branch/region league table | Fisher or funnel plot vs peers | small units at extremes |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R3-9 · Basic · Basket value by region"
    **Business framing:** The regional director asks whether regions differ.

    **Deliverable:** Run `retail_basket_by_region.yaml`: summary by region, ANOVA, Welch ANOVA, Kruskal-Wallis, Levene, η².

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** ANOVA p = 0.0014; Welch p = 4.3e-06; Kruskal p = 2.6e-06; Levene p = 0.05; η² = 0.0049. *(tolerance: p within an order of magnitude; η² ± 0.001)*

        **Model answer:** All tests say regions differ; Kruskal-Wallis is primary because baskets are skewed. η² is tiny — region explains about half a percent of basket variation.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using classic ANOVA without checking variances or skew, or stopping at 'significant'.

    **Leader drill:** Would you change any regional plan on this evidence?

??? example "R3-10 · Applied · Which regions — and why?"
    **Business framing:** Follow-up — which regions differ, and what explains it?

    **Deliverable:** Pairwise Mann-Whitney with Holm; share of baskets from Express stores in North vs elsewhere; North's median gap within each format.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Significant pairs: East–North, North–South, North–West. Express share 57.3% (North) vs 13.9%. Gap within format: Express -1.66 · Hyper 1.39 · Super 7.28. *(tolerance: ± 1)*

        **Model answer:** North differs from every region, but within formats it is not consistently lower — the difference is store mix (Express-heavy), a Simpson-type pattern.

        **Common wrong answer:** Concluding North shoppers spend less.

    **Leader drill:** What would you tell the North regional manager?

??? example "R3-11 · Stretch · 780 store pairs"
    **Business framing:** A consultant offers a league table of 'significantly different' stores.

    **Deliverable:** Welch tests for all pairs of 40 stores on basket value; count significant raw and after Holm.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 780 pairs; 389 raw p < 0.05; 116 after Holm. *(tolerance: ± 10%)*

        **Model answer:** Stores genuinely differ (format, size), so many pairs survive, but correction removes a large share of the raw 'differences'. A league table should use intervals or a funnel plot, not 780 tests.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting 389 significant differences.

    **Leader drill:** How would you present store comparisons to the operations board instead?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F3-9 · Basic · Default by employment type"
    **Business framing:** Credit policy asks whether employment type matters.

    **Deliverable:** Run `finance_default_by_employment.yaml`: rates, chi-square, Cramér's V, largest standardised residual.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** χ² = 29.7 (df 4), p = 5.5e-06; V = 0.034; student residual +4.6. *(tolerance: χ² ± 0.5)*

    **Leader drill:** Would you add employment type to the scorecard? What else would you check?

??? example "F3-10 · Applied · Fisher's exact for a small branch"
    **Business framing:** Is branch BR18's low default rate unusual for its region?

    **Deliverable:** 2×2 table vs the rest of region R4; Fisher's exact p; expected defaults.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Table [[8, 554], [26, 2340]]; expected ≈ 6.2 defaults; Fisher p = 0.5119. *(tolerance: p ± 0.02)*

        **Model answer:** Compared with its own region the branch is unremarkable. Its low rate is regional mix.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using chi-square with expected counts below 5, or comparing with the whole bank.

    **Leader drill:** How would you set fair targets for branches in different regions?

??? example "F3-11 · Stretch · McNemar on delinquency"
    **Business framing:** Did delinquency rise between month 6 and month 12 on the same accounts?

    **Deliverable:** Paired 2×2 of 30+ DPD at months 6 and 12; McNemar test; explain why chi-square is wrong.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 20,524 accounts; 0.57% → 3.42%; discordant 701 newly delinquent vs 118 recovered; p = 6.1e-92. *(tolerance: ± 0.05 pp)*

        **Model answer:** The same accounts are measured twice; only those that changed carry information. Delinquency builds with months on book.

        **Common wrong answer:** Treating month-6 and month-12 as independent samples.

    **Leader drill:** How would you use this seasoning pattern when comparing young and old vintages?

**Objectives** — you can: report effects in business units and in standard units (Cohen's d, Hedges' g, odds ratio, relative risk,
risk difference, NNT, η², Cramér's V); explain power and the minimum detectable effect (MDE); size a store test and a champion /
challenger test from your own data; account for clustering with the design effect; and refuse "no effect" claims from under-powered
tests.

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Effect sizes — the number the decision needs

    | Measure | Use for | Example from our data |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Difference in business units | always | challenger cure rate 41.0% vs champion 35.0%: **+6.0 pp** (95% CI 2.4 – 9.7) |
    | Relative risk (RR) | rates | 1.173 (cure is 17% more likely) |
    | Odds ratio (OR) | logistic models, case-control | 1.294 — larger than RR when the outcome is common |
    | Number needed to treat (NNT) | operations | 17 accounts on the challenger per extra cure |
    | Cohen's d / Hedges' g | standardised mean difference | Hair Care test g = 1.06 (L3.4) |
    | η² / Cramér's V | k groups / tables | region η² = 0.0049; employment V = 0.034 (L3.5) |

    Cohen's benchmarks (d 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large) are context-free; a 3 pp cure-rate gain worth millions is "small" by
    Cohen and huge by the P&L.

    ### 2.2 Power — the chance of detecting a real effect

    Four quantities are linked: **α**, **power** (1 − β), **effect size** and **n**. Fix three and the fourth follows. Variability comes
    from your data: the store growth index has SD **0.126** (pooled within arms of the Hair Care test).

    **Stores per arm for 80% power, α = 0.05:**

    | Lift to detect | 5% | 8% | 10% | 12% | 15% | 20% |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Stores per arm | 101 | 41 | 27 | 19 | 13 | 8 |

    With the 20 + 20 stores we have, the **MDE is 11.2%**. Power for a true lift of 5% is only
    **24%**; for 12%, 85%. A 5% effect would usually be missed — and the chain has only
    40 stores, so a 5% test is impossible as designed. Options: longer test window, better baseline adjustment (CUPED, L4.2), or accept
    a larger MDE.

    **Halving the effect quadruples the sample** (n ∝ 1/effect²).

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="power" data-caption="Hover to read: how many customers per group to detect a small (d = 0.1) vs medium (d = 0.5) effect with 80% power."></div>

    ### 2.3 Proportions and unequal splits

    Champion cure rate ≈ 35.0%. To detect +3 pp (Cohen's h = 0.0623) at 80% power with an
    **80/20** champion/challenger split: **2,526 challenger + 10,102 champion =
    12,628 accounts**. A 50/50 split would need about 8,082 — unequal splits are safer
    for the business but cost sample.

    | Improvement to detect | +2 pp | +3 pp | +5 pp | +8 pp |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Accounts needed (80/20) | 28,249 | 12,628 | 4,594 | 1,819 |

    The actual test (F16) had 860 challenger and 3,335 champion accounts: power for a 3 pp effect was
    only **37%**. It found an effect because the true effect was about 6 pp — lucky, not well designed.

    ### 2.4 Clustering: the design effect

    If stores are randomised but you analyse store-weeks, the weeks within a store are highly alike. Intra-class correlation of weekly
    Hair Care units within stores: **ICC = 0.759**. Design effect for 16 weeks per store:

    $$\text{DEFF} = 1 + (m - 1) \times \text{ICC} = 12.4$$

    So 640 store-weeks (40 stores × 16) carry the information of about **52** independent observations. Plan and
    analyse at the store.

    ### 2.5 Under-powered "no effect"

    L3.4 found no significant default difference for high-net-worth customers (n = 241, Fisher p = 0.3753). Power to
    detect a true 6% vs 4% default rate with that n was **30%**. The test could not have found it; "HNI customers are
    no riskier" is not a supported conclusion. Report the MDE whenever a test is not significant.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Effect size is what the decision needs; power is the probability the design detects the effect you care about. Size tests
        *before* running them, on the unit that was randomised. A non-significant under-powered test is uninformative.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Set the MDE from economics.** "What is the smallest lift that pays for a rollout?" — then size the test for it. If the
      required sample is impossible, change the design or don't run the test.
    - **Never accept post-hoc "observed power".** It is a restatement of the p-value. Power is a design property.
    - **Unequal splits are a risk choice.** 80/20 protects the business but needs ~56%
      more accounts for the same power.
    - **Cluster designs need cluster thinking.** A vendor quoting "power from 25,000 transactions" for a 40-store test is wrong by a
      factor of the design effect.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Before approving any test, ask for: MDE, power, unit of randomisation, n per arm, test duration and the decision rule.

## 5. Output anatomy — `rcp_power`

| Section | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| Design line | α, sides, power, ratio and the **unit** |
| From data | mean/SD or baseline rate the plan rests on (check it is a pre-treatment or control estimate) |
| Sample-size table | units per arm and total for each effect |
| With the sample you have | MDE and achieved power for the real n |
| Power curve | how quickly power rises with n for each effect |

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Situation | Effect size to report | Planning tool |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | means, two groups | difference + d/g | t-test power |
    | rates | risk difference, RR, OR, NNT | proportions power (Cohen's h) |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R3-12 · Basic · Stores needed for the next test"
    **Business framing:** Category management wants to test a new fixture in Snacks next quarter.

    **Deliverable:** Run `retail_power_store_test.yaml`: stores per arm for 5–20% lifts and the MDE with 20 stores per arm.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Per arm: 5% 101, 10% 27, 12% 19, 15% 13. MDE at 20/arm 11.2%. *(tolerance: ± 1 store; MDE ± 0.3)*

        **Model answer:** With 40 stores the test can reliably detect lifts of about 11.2% or more. A 5% effect would need ~101 stores per arm — more than the chain has. Reduce variance (CUPED, longer windows) or accept a larger MDE.

        **Common wrong answer:** Sizing from transaction counts instead of stores.

    **Leader drill:** The fixture costs 2% of category sales to install. Is the test worth running?

??? example "R3-13 · Applied · Sizing the e-coupon test"
    **Business framing:** Digital marketing plans the next e-coupon A/B test (users randomised).

    **Deliverable:** Users per arm for +1.0 and +1.5 pp conversion from the current base rate (80% power, α 0.05).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Base 11.29%; +1.0 pp needs 16,325 per arm; +1.5 pp needs 7,385 per arm. *(tolerance: ± 3%)*

        **Model answer:** Small absolute lifts on an 11% base need tens of thousands of users. The last test had ~11,400 in total — under-powered for 1 pp.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using the same n as the last test without checking power.

    **Leader drill:** How long must the test run at your current app traffic?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F3-12 · Basic · Size the collections challenger"
    **Business framing:** Collections plans a new call script and wants to detect +3 pp in 3-month cure rate.

    **Deliverable:** Run `finance_power_challenger.yaml`: accounts for +2/+3/+5/+8 pp at 80/20; compare 50/50; months needed at 1,400 new 30+ DPD accounts a month.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** +3 pp: 12,628 accounts at 80/20 (8,082 at 50/50); curve 2 28,249 · 3 12,628 · 5 4,594 · 8 1,819; about 9.0 months. *(tolerance: ± 1%)*

        **Model answer:** A 3 pp target needs about nine months of intake at 80/20. Options: target a larger effect, use 50/50 for a shorter test, or use a variance-reducing covariate.

        **Common wrong answer:** Running for a month and calling a null result 'no effect'.

    **Leader drill:** Which option would you choose and why?

??? example "F3-13 · Applied · Effect sizes for the last challenger"
    **Business framing:** The last challenger test is being written up.

    **Deliverable:** Risk difference with CI, RR, OR, NNT; achieved power for the planned 3 pp effect.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** +6.0 pp (CI 2.4 – 9.7); RR 1.173; OR 1.294; NNT 17; power for 3 pp 37%. *(tolerance: ± 0.1)*

        **Model answer:** The effect is real and useful, but the test was under-powered for its own target; it succeeded because the true effect was about twice the planned one.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting OR as if it were RR ('29% more likely').

    **Leader drill:** What would you change in the next test plan?

Report effects in business units; size tests from data before running; MDE ∝ 1/√n; plan at the randomised unit; never read an

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The bootstrap: let the data simulate its own uncertainty

    1. Treat your sample as a stand-in for the population.
    2. Draw a new sample of the same size **with replacement** (some rows twice, some not at all).
    3. Compute the statistic (median, ratio, percentile, anything).
    4. Repeat thousands of times. The spread of those values is the sampling distribution; its 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles give a
       95% **percentile interval**. (BCa intervals adjust for bias and skew — use them when the recipe offers them.)

    | Statistic | Estimate | 95% bootstrap CI | Why bootstrap |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Median spend per customer (1,287 customers) | 79.84 | 75.48 – 87.46 | no simple SE formula for a median |
    | Margin rate (margin ÷ revenue) | 30.92% | 30.49 – 31.37% | ratio of two random totals |

    ### 2.2 Resample the right unit

    The bootstrap assumes the things you resample are independent. Lines from the same store share the store's prices, mix and
    customers. Mean revenue per line = 6.692:

    | Resampling unit | SE of mean line revenue |
    | --- | --- |
    | lines (wrong: treats lines as independent) | 0.07 |
    | stores (right: whole stores resampled — a *cluster* bootstrap) | 0.357 |

    The honest SE is **5.1× larger**. Any CI or test built on lines would be far too confident.

    ### 2.3 The permutation test: what if the labels didn't matter?

    H0: branch makes no difference to default. If that were true, shuffling the branch labels would not change anything systematic.

    1. Compute the observed difference: BR11 3.73% vs BR03 3.32% →
       **0.41 pp**.
    2. Pool the 2,991 accounts, shuffle the labels, recompute the difference. Repeat 10,000 times.
    3. p = share of shuffles at least as extreme as the observed difference = **0.5457**.

    The chi-square test gives p = 0.5465 — the two agree because n is large. The difference is noise.

    ### 2.4 When resampling beats formulas — and when it doesn't

    | Use resampling | Prefer a formula or a model |
    | --- | --- |
    | medians, ratios, percentiles, Gini, any custom metric | simple means and proportions with large n (same answer, faster) |
    | skewed data with modest n | very small n (< 15): the bootstrap cannot invent information |
    | small randomised experiments (permutation is exact) | clustered or time-series data unless you resample clusters/blocks |
    | checking a formula's assumptions | extreme quantiles (P99 of 300 points) |

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        The bootstrap resamples your data to measure uncertainty of any statistic. A permutation test shuffles labels to see how
        unusual the observed difference is under "no effect". Both depend on resampling the **independent unit**.

    **Judgement points**

    - **"We bootstrapped it" is not a quality stamp.** Ask *what* was resampled. Lines instead of stores understated uncertainty
      5.1-fold here.
    - **Permutation tests are ideal for small store tests.** L3.4 used one: with 20 v 20 stores the shuffle distribution is exact
      and assumption-light (p = 0.0019).
    - **Fix the seed and the number of resamples** so results reproduce — audit and model validation will ask.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"What did you resample, how many times, with what seed — and does the classical test agree?"* Disagreement is a clue to
        skew or clustering, not a reason to pick the smaller p-value.

## 5. Output anatomy — a bootstrap result

| Element | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| Resampling unit | must be the independent unit (customer, basket, store, account) |
| B (resamples), seed | ≥ 2,000 for intervals; seed for reproducibility |
| Bootstrap distribution plot | lumpy or multi-modal → statistic unstable (median of few values) |
| Percentile vs BCa interval | close → fine; far apart → skew/bias, prefer BCa |
| Permutation p | share of shuffles as extreme as observed; minimum is 1/(B + 1) |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Question | Method |
    | --- | --- |
    | CI for a median, ratio, percentile | bootstrap (percentile or BCa) |
    | Data clustered in stores/branches | cluster bootstrap (resample clusters) |
    | Small randomised test, any statistic | permutation test |
    | Large n, mean or proportion | classical test (resampling agrees) |

=== "Leader"

    | "bootstrap CI on 25,000 lines" | resampling unit | resample stores/customers |
    | "permutation p = 0.000" | number of shuffles | report p < 1/(B + 1) |
    | "branch X worse" | permutation/chi-square p and CI | most branch gaps are noise or mix |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R3-4 · Basic · Bootstrap the median"
    **Business framing:** Finance prefers the median basket because the mean is distorted by bulk buyers.

    **Deliverable:** Median basket value and median annual spend per customer, each with a 95% bootstrap CI (≥ 3,000 resamples, fixed seed).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Median basket 38.94 (CI 37.9 – 40.29); median customer spend 79.84 (CI 75.48 – 87.46). *(tolerance: ± 0.5)*

        **Model answer:** No simple formula exists for the SE of a median; the bootstrap resamples baskets (or customers) to measure it. The median is far below the mean — the typical basket is smaller than the average one.

        **Common wrong answer:** Resampling lines instead of baskets or customers.

    **Leader drill:** When would you report the median rather than the mean to the board?

??? example "R3-5 · Applied · Lines or stores? The resampling unit"
    **Business framing:** A dashboard shows mean revenue per line with a very tight interval.

    **Deliverable:** SE of mean revenue per line by resampling lines vs resampling whole stores; the ratio.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** SE 0.07 (lines) vs 0.357 (stores): 5.1× larger. *(tolerance: ratio ± 1)*

        **Model answer:** Lines in the same store share prices, mix and customers; they are not independent. The cluster bootstrap gives the honest uncertainty. Any test on lines would be far too confident.

        **Common wrong answer:** Trusting the line-level interval because n is large.

    **Leader drill:** Which of your team's dashboards might show over-confident intervals for this reason?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F3-4 · Basic · Permutation test between two branches"
    **Business framing:** Is branch BR11 riskier than BR03?

    **Deliverable:** Rates, difference, 10,000-shuffle permutation p; chi-square p for comparison.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** BR11 3.73% vs BR03 3.32% (+0.41 pp); permutation p = 0.5457; chi-square p = 0.5465. *(tolerance: p ± 0.02)*

        **Model answer:** No evidence of a difference; both tests agree because n is large.

        **Common wrong answer:** Calling a 0.4 pp gap a risk problem.

    **Leader drill:** When would you insist on a permutation test rather than chi-square?

??? example "F3-5 · Applied · Bootstrap a ratio"
    **Business framing:** Collections wants a CI for the cure-rate ratio challenger ÷ champion.

    **Deliverable:** Relative risk with a bootstrap 95% CI (resample accounts within each arm, ≥ 3,000 resamples).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** RR ≈ 1.173; CI excludes 1 (the risk-difference CI is 2.4 – 9.7 pp). *(tolerance: RR ± 0.01)*

        **Model answer:** Ratios have skewed sampling distributions; the bootstrap handles this without a log-transform formula. Resample within arms to keep group sizes fixed.

        **Common wrong answer:** Resampling the pooled data without arm labels.

    **Leader drill:** Which would you present to the collections head — RR or the pp difference? Why?

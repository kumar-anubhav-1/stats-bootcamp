## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The model

    $$\log(\text{units}) = \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\text{promo share} + \beta_2\,\text{stock-out share} + \beta_3\,\text{temperature} + \beta_4\,\text{holiday} + \text{store effects} + \varepsilon$$

    OLS picks the β's that minimise the sum of squared residuals (the projection of L2.7). Each β is the expected change in the
    outcome for a one-unit change in that variable **with the others held fixed** — here also comparing each store with itself
    (store fixed effects = one dummy per store, one store as reference).

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="reg3d" data-caption="Rotate the 3-D view: each dot is a store-week; the plane is the fitted regression. Steeper along price = more elastic."></div>

    ### 2.2 Reading the coefficients (4,160 store-weeks, R² = 0.886)

    | Driver | β | 95% CI | Business reading |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Temperature (°C) | 0.0202 | 0.0192 – 0.0211 | **+2.04% units per +1 °C** (planted truth +2.0%) |
    | Holiday week | 0.3712 | 0.3545 – 0.3879 | **+44.9%** in holiday weeks (truth +40%, before other effects) |
    | Promo SKU share (0–1) | 0.8929 | 0.7524 – 1.0334 | +10 pp of SKUs on promotion → **+9.3%** |
    | Stock-out SKU share (0–1) | -0.9829 | -1.2296 – -0.7362 | +10 pp of SKUs out of stock → **-9.4%** |

    With a **log outcome**, 100 × (e^β − 1) is the % change per unit. With **log outcome and log predictor**, β is an elasticity
    (Day 8). With a **raw outcome**, β is in units: in the same model on raw units (format dummies instead of store effects),
    +1 °C = 2.62 units and a holiday week = 56.9 units per store-week — averages that hide the fact
    that a Hyper store gains far more units than an Express store. Log models express effects as proportions, which travel across
    store sizes.

    ### 2.3 Dummies and reference categories

    `C(format)` creates indicators for Hyper and Super; Express is the reference. Each dummy coefficient is "compared with Express,
    other things equal". Changing the reference changes the numbers, not the model.

    ### 2.4 Fit statistics

    | Statistic | Meaning | Caveat |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | R² | share of outcome variation explained | rises with every added variable |
    | adjusted R² | penalises extra variables | still not a test of usefulness |
    | RMSE | typical prediction error, outcome units | compare with the outcome's SD |
    | F-test | do the predictors jointly explain anything? | almost always yes with big n |

    R² is high here mostly because store effects capture size. A model can have high R² and wrong coefficients (L5.2), or low R² and
    exactly the right causal effect (an A/B test).

    ### 2.5 Finance: how limits and prices are set

    - log(limit) on log(income) + product: elasticity **0.999** (R² 0.986) — limits are set proportional to
      income (personal loans 60.3% larger). This is the policy rule, recovered from data — and the reason income and
      limit are near-duplicates (L5.2).
    - Interest rate (%) on bureau score + product + DTI: **-3.97 pp per +100 bureau points**, personal loans
      -8.13 pp; DTI adds nothing once bureau score is in (p = 0.119). R² 0.942: pricing is
      risk-based on bureau score.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Regression coefficients are adjusted associations: the expected change in the outcome per unit of a driver, holding the
        others fixed. Log outcomes give % effects. R² measures fit, not truth or causality.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Ask for the business reading.** "+2% per degree, +45% in holiday weeks, −9% per 10 pp of SKUs out of stock" is actionable;
      a table of β's is not.
    - **"Holding constant" is a modelling claim.** Promotions and displays move together (L4.4); a coefficient for one is only as good
      as the DAG behind the model.
    - **Regression can reveal policy.** Limits ∝ income and rate ∝ bureau score are bank rules, not customer behaviour — don't
      present them as discoveries.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Read me each coefficient in one business sentence, with its interval — and tell me which ones you'd bet money on."*

## 5. Output anatomy — `rcp_regression`

| Section | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| Fit | R², adjusted R², AIC/BIC (OLS); AUC (logistic); dispersion (GLM) |
| Coefficients | effect in business units with CI; hidden fixed effects listed |
| Diagnostics | heteroscedasticity, skew, influence, VIF (L5.2) |
| `coefficients.csv` | raw β, SE, p and transformed effect |
| `diagnostics.png` | residuals vs fitted; Q-Q |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Outcome / driver | Specification | Coefficient reads as |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | positive, skewed outcome | log(y) ~ x | % change per unit of x |
    | log(y) ~ log(x) | log–log | elasticity (% per 1%) |
    | raw y ~ x | linear | units per unit of x |
    | category driver | C(x) dummies | difference vs reference level |
    | many units (stores) | fixed effects | within-unit comparison |

=== "Leader"

    | Claim | Question | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | "R² 0.9, so the model is right" | what's driving R²? | fit ≠ validity |
    | "temperature adds 2.6 units" | per which store type? | prefer % effects |
    | "the model shows DTI doesn't matter for pricing" | it's our policy | regressions describe the data-generating rule |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R5-1 · Basic · Drivers of Beverages sales"
    **Business framing:** The Beverages buyer wants to know what moves weekly sales per store.

    **Deliverable:** Run `retail_regression_beverages.yaml`; give every coefficient as a business sentence with its CI.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Temperature +2.04% per °C; holiday +44.9%; +10 pp promo share +9.3%; +10 pp stock-out share -9.4%; R² 0.886. *(tolerance: ± 0.2 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Each effect is a % change holding the others and the store fixed. Temperature and holiday effects match the planted truths closely.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reading the promo coefficient (~0.89) as '+89%'.

    **Leader drill:** Which of these effects can the buyer control, and which only forecast?

??? example "R5-2 · Applied · Raw units vs log units"
    **Business framing:** A junior fitted the same drivers on raw units with format dummies.

    **Deliverable:** Temperature and holiday coefficients from the raw-units model; explain why they differ from the log model.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Raw: +2.62 units per °C and +56.9 units in holiday weeks (mean 143.0 units/store-week). *(tolerance: ± 0.2)*

        **Model answer:** Raw effects are averages over stores of very different size; a Hyper store gains far more units than an Express store. Log effects are proportional and comparable.

        **Common wrong answer:** Applying +57 units to every store.

    **Leader drill:** Which model would you use to plan stock for an Express store in a heatwave?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F5-1 · Basic · How limits are set"
    **Business framing:** Risk asks for evidence of how credit limits relate to income.

    **Deliverable:** log(limit) on log(income) + product type; elasticity, product effect, R².

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Elasticity 0.999; personal loans +60.3%; R² 0.986. *(tolerance: ± 0.01)*

        **Model answer:** Limits are proportional to income (the policy rule). Near-perfect R² reflects a rule, not behaviour.

        **Common wrong answer:** Presenting the elasticity as a customer insight.

    **Leader drill:** What does this imply for using both income and limit in risk models?

??? example "F5-2 · Applied · Risk-based pricing"
    **Business framing:** Is pricing driven by risk?

    **Deliverable:** Interest rate (%) on bureau score + product + DTI; effect per 100 bureau points; DTI p; R².

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** -3.97 pp per +100 points; personal loans -8.13 pp; DTI p = 0.119; R² 0.942. *(tolerance: ± 0.05)*

        **Model answer:** Pricing follows bureau score; DTI adds nothing given the score.

        **Common wrong answer:** Concluding DTI 'doesn't matter for risk' — it matters for default (L5.3), just not for our pricing rule.

    **Leader drill:** Should DTI enter pricing?

## 1. Business question

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Non-linear effects: utilisation (F04)

    Credit-card default rate by utilisation band, observed vs a logistic model linear in utilisation:

    | Utilisation | Observed default | Linear-logit prediction |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | ≤50% | 2.7% | 2.5% |
    | 50–70% | 3.9% | 4.6% |
    | 70–80% | 5.1% | 5.7% |
    | 80–90% | 8.9% | 7.6% |
    | >90% | 16.8% | 13.5% |
    

    Risk is flat-ish below 50% and climbs steeply above 80%: the top band defaults **6.1×** the bottom. The linear
    term on the log-odds scale already bends upward on the probability scale, but under-predicts the >90% band and over-predicts the
    middle.

    | Specification | AIC | AUC |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | linear utilisation | 4,050 | 0.819 |
    | 5 bins (WoE-style, Day 8) | 4,059 | 0.819 |
    | **B-spline, 4 df** | **4,043** | **0.822** |

    The spline fits best (smooth, few parameters). Bins are popular in credit scoring because they are monotone, explainable and
    stable — at a small cost in fit. Ranking (AUC) barely changes; **calibration in the high-risk band** is what improves, and that is
    where limits and collections decisions sit.

    ### 2.2 Interactions: young × high DTI (F05)

    | Default rate | DTI ≤ 0.45 | DTI > 0.45 |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | age ≥ 25 | 3.51% | 4.53% |
    | age < 25 | 6.4% | **12.46%** |

    `y ~ young * hidti` adds the product term: interaction odds ratio **1.6** (95% CI 1.04 –
    2.49, p = 0.0345) — high DTI hurts young customers more than its separate effects predict. Only
    337 accounts sit in the young × high-DTI cell, so the CI is wide. Interpret interactions with a 2×2 table
    or predicted probabilities, never from the raw coefficient alone.

    ### 2.3 Mixed models: random store intercepts

    Basket value (log) with store format as a fixed effect and a **random intercept per store**:

    - Intra-class correlation **ICC = 0.027** — only ~3% of basket variation is
      between stores; most is between baskets.
    - **Shrinkage:** each store's estimated effect is pulled toward the format average by the factor k = σ²_store ÷ (σ²_store + σ²_e/n).
      The median store (56 baskets) keeps 60% of its raw
      deviation; store S017 (24 baskets in the sample) keeps only
      40%.

    Fixed effects (one dummy per store) estimate each store separately; random effects **borrow strength** across stores — better for
    small units, ranking stores, and when you want to generalise to new stores (L4.3 shrinkage, same idea).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Real effects bend (utilisation), combine (age × DTI) and cluster (stores). Splines/bins capture curves; interactions capture
        "it depends"; mixed models give honest store-level estimates with shrinkage.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Thresholds are where money is.** The utilisation curve matters for limit-management and collections triggers above 80%.
    - **"It depends" claims need the interaction test and the cell size.** 337 accounts is enough to flag the
      segment, not to price it precisely.
    - **Store league tables should be shrunk.** Raw store averages exaggerate small stores' highs and lows.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Did you plot the outcome against each key driver before fitting a straight line?"*

## 5. Output anatomy

| Element | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| `bs(x, df=k)` terms | spline pieces — interpret through a partial-effect plot, not coefficients |
| `a:b` interaction term | extra effect when both are present (odds ratio for logistic) |
| random-effect variance, ICC | share of variation between groups |
| BLUPs / store effects | shrunk group estimates |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Pattern | Tool |
    | --- | --- |
    | curve or threshold | splines / bins / polynomial |
    | effect depends on another variable | interaction term |
    | grouped data (stores, branches) | random intercepts (mixed model) or cluster SEs |
    | many groups, few rows each | mixed model (shrinkage) |
    | complex non-linear interactions | tree ensembles (Day 6) |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R5-11 · Basic · Store random effects"
    **Business framing:** How much of basket value is the store?

    **Deliverable:** Mixed model of log basket value (format fixed, store random); ICC; shrinkage factor for the smallest and median store.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** ICC 0.027; shrinkage 0.4 for S017 (24 baskets) and 0.6 for the median store (56). *(tolerance: ± 0.02)*

        **Model answer:** Most variation is between baskets. Small stores' raw averages are pulled strongly toward their format mean.

        **Common wrong answer:** Ranking stores on raw average basket.

    **Leader drill:** How would you use shrunk store effects in a store league table?

??? example "R5-12 · Applied · Is the temperature effect linear?"
    **Business framing:** The buyer suspects sales respond more on very hot days.

    **Deliverable:** Add a spline in temperature (3 df); compare AIC and plot the partial effect.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** The spline adds little: the planted effect is linear (+2% per °C), so AIC changes only slightly. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Check non-linearity with a spline and a plot; keep the linear term when the gain is negligible.

        **Common wrong answer:** Keeping a spline because one knot is 'significant'.

    **Leader drill:** When is extra flexibility worth the complexity?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F5-12 · Basic · The utilisation curve"
    **Business framing:** Collections triggers are set on utilisation.

    **Deliverable:** Observed default by utilisation band vs linear-logit prediction; AIC/AUC of linear, bins, spline.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Observed ≤50 2.7% · 50–70 3.9% · 70–80 5.1% · 80–90 8.9% · >90 16.8%; linear predicted ≤50 2.5% · 50–70 4.6% · 70–80 5.7% · 80–90 7.6% · >90 13.5%; AIC linear 4,050, bins 4,059, spline 4,043. *(tolerance: ± 0.3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Risk climbs steeply above 80%; the linear term under-predicts the top band. Splines fit best; bins are the explainable alternative.

        **Common wrong answer:** Judging only by AUC (nearly identical).

    **Leader drill:** Where would you set a collections trigger?

??? example "F5-13 · Applied · Young × high DTI"
    **Business framing:** Is high DTI worse for young customers?

    **Deliverable:** 2×2 default rates; interaction OR with CI; cell size.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Rates older_loDTI 3.51% · older_hiDTI 4.53% · young_loDTI 6.4% · young_hiDTI 12.46%; interaction OR 1.6 (CI 1.04–2.49), n = 337. *(tolerance: ± 0.1)*

        **Model answer:** Yes — the combination is worse than the sum of parts, but the estimate is imprecise.

        **Common wrong answer:** Interpreting main effects alone when an interaction is present.

    **Leader drill:** Would you add a policy rule or a model term?

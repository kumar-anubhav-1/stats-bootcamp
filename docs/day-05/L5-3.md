## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 From probability to log-odds

    $$\log\frac{p}{1-p} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_1 + \dots \qquad p = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\beta_0 + \beta_1 x_1 + \dots)}}$$

    The model is linear on the log-odds scale and always gives probabilities between 0 and 1. e^β is the **odds ratio** for a
    one-unit increase in x.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="logistic_threshold" data-caption="Drag the decline threshold: the trade-off between declining more customers and catching more defaulters."></div>

    ### 2.2 Default model (25,000 accounts; mean predicted PD 4.0%)

    | Driver | Odds ratio | 95% CI | Reading |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Bureau score (+50 points) | 0.376 | 0.35 – 0.404 | each +50 points cuts the odds of default by ~62% |
    | Utilisation (+10 pp) | 1.159 | 1.111 – 1.209 | odds +16% per 10 pp (linear on log-odds — L5.6 checks that) |
    | DTI (+1.0) | 3.828 | 2.446 – 5.991 | per +0.1 DTI: odds × 1.14 |
    | Income unverified | 1.389 | 1.197 – 1.612 | adjusted for the other drivers (cf. raw gap in L3.4) |
    | Partner channel (vs branch) | 2.465 | 2.082 – 2.918 | marginal effect ≈ **+3.14 pp** PD on average |
    | Online channel (vs branch) | 0.949 | 0.806 – 1.117 | no difference |

    Fit: **AUC 0.83** (ranking quality, L6.6), McFadden pseudo-R² 0.178 (0.2–0.4 is
    considered good for logistic models — not comparable with OLS R²).

    ### 2.3 Odds ratios are not risk ratios

    An OR of 2.465 means the **odds** are 2.465× higher. Because default is rare (~4%), odds ≈
    probability and OR ≈ RR here. For common outcomes (cure ≈ 35%, L3.6) OR overstates RR. For executives, translate to
    **probabilities or percentage points**: "a partner-channel customer's PD is about 3.14 pp higher than an
    otherwise similar branch customer's".

    ### 2.4 Returns (retail)

    Line-level model of return probability: Electronics OR **2.07** (1.16 – 3.7),
    price (per doubling of unit price: odds × 1.19), delivery/click-and-collect vs in-store OR
    1.093 (p = 0.324). AUC only **0.546**: real effects, weak prediction —
    returns are mostly unpredictable from these fields (R17 abuse is a customer pattern, L8.5).

    ### 2.5 Goodness of fit

    Deviance (lower is better; compare nested models with a likelihood-ratio test), AIC/BIC, AUC for ranking, and **calibration**
    (do predicted 5% customers default 5% of the time? — L6.6). Hosmer-Lemeshow is a legacy calibration test; prefer a calibration plot.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Logistic regression models log-odds; coefficients exponentiate to odds ratios. Translate into probabilities for decisions.
        Real effects can coexist with weak prediction (AUC near 0.5).

    **Judgement points**

    - **Ban "X times more likely" for odds ratios** unless the outcome is rare. Ask for pp differences.
    - **Statistical significance ≠ predictive power.** The returns model has significant drivers and almost no ability to rank
      lines (AUC 0.546).
    - **Logistic models remain the backbone of credit scoring** (Day 8) because they are stable, explainable and calibratable.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Give me the effect in percentage points for a typical customer, and show me the calibration plot."*

## 5. Output anatomy — logistic output

| Element | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| odds ratio | multiplicative change in odds per unit; < 1 protective |
| CI | excludes 1 → evidence of association |
| AUC | ranking: 0.5 random, 0.7–0.85 typical credit models |
| pseudo-R² | not OLS R²; 0.2–0.4 good |
| event rate | needed to translate odds into probabilities |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R5-5 · Basic · Who returns what?"
    **Business framing:** The returns team wants to know which lines are likelier to be returned.

    **Deliverable:** Logistic model of return_flag on Electronics, log unit price, non-store channel and loyalty; odds ratios and AUC.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Electronics OR 2.07 (CI 1.16–3.7); price OR 1.289 per log unit; channel OR 1.093 (p = 0.324); AUC 0.546. *(tolerance: ± 0.05)*

        **Model answer:** Electronics and pricier items are returned more, but the model barely ranks lines (AUC near 0.5) — returns are mostly unpredictable from line attributes.

        **Common wrong answer:** Calling it a good predictive model because drivers are significant.

    **Leader drill:** What data would improve return prediction?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F5-5 · Basic · Default drivers as odds ratios"
    **Business framing:** Run `finance_logit_default.yaml` (or the lesson model).

    **Deliverable:** ORs with CIs for bureau (+50), utilisation (+10 pp), DTI, unverified income, partner and online channels; AUC.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"

    **Deliverable:** Average marginal effect of the partner channel on PD.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"

        **Model answer:** Holding other drivers at their observed values, partner customers' PD is ~3 pp higher on average.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using the odds ratio as a pp effect.

    **Leader drill:** Is the partner premium priced in?

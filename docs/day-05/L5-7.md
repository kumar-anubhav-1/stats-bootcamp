**Objectives** — you can: take a vague business question to a defensible answer with the full workflow (data checks → descriptive
breakdown → decomposition → model → sensitivity → recommendation); separate *mix* from *rate* effects; save the analysis as a reusable
config; and, as a leader, review a junior's analysis live.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "Margin rate fell from 31.21% in 2025Q1 to 28.14% in 2025Q2. Why — and is it fixed?"

=== "Finance"

    > "Approval rates dropped from 87.3% to 69.1% in 2025Q2. Has underwriting become stricter?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The workflow

    1. **Restate** the question as a measurable comparison (metric, periods, population).
    2. **Data checks** (Day 1): duplicates, entry errors, definition changes between periods.
    3. **Decompose** the change: did the **mix** change (more of a low-margin or low-approval segment), or did the **rate** within
       segments change?
    4. **Model** the rate change with the drivers you can measure (regression, Day 5).
    5. **Sensitivity:** does the conclusion survive alternative definitions?
    6. **Recommend** with numbers and uncertainty; save the analysis as a config.

    ### 2.2 Retail: mix vs rate

    Segments = category × promotion status. Change in margin rate = mix effect + rate effect:

    $$\Delta m = \underbrace{\sum_s (w_{s,1} - w_{s,0})\,m_{s,0}}_{\text{mix}} + \underbrace{\sum_s w_{s,1}(m_{s,1} - m_{s,0})}_{\text{rate}}$$

    | Component | pp |
    | --- | --- |
    | Mix (shifts between segments) | 0.28 |
    | **Rate (margin within segments)** | **-3.34** |

    Promotion share barely moved (21.5% → 22.2% of lines). The fall is *within*
    segments, and the largest contributors are **regular-price** lines: Beverages|regular -0.99 pp · Fresh Produce|regular -0.78 pp · Dairy|regular -0.67 pp.

    **Why did regular margins fall?** Shelf price relative to list price dropped from 0.994 to
    0.973 while unit cost relative to list stayed at 0.666 → 0.667.
    Regular-line margin: 34.9% → 31.9%. By region (price index Q1 → Q2):
    East 0.979 → 0.992; North 0.979 → 0.967; South 0.996 → 0.975; West 1.007 → 0.964.
    Regional price indices were cut in North, South and West (competitive pricing, L8.4). Promoted lines also earned less
    (12.4% → 7.0%): a regression of promoted-line margin on discount depth and category
    shows each +1 pp of discount costs 1.65 pp of margin, and discounts deepened slightly
    (24.9% → 26.0%); after adjusting for depth the Q2 effect shrinks from
    -4.38 to -2.16 pp.

    **Is it fixed?** In 2025Q3 the regular price index returned to 0.993 and total margin to 30.39%.

    ### 2.3 Finance: a pure mix story

    | Approval rate | 2025Q1 | 2025Q2 |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Branch | 86.7% | 86.1% |
    | Online | 88.0% | 87.4% |
    | Partner (new from 2025Q2) | — | **59.8%** |
    | **All applications** | 87.3% | **69.1%** |

    Within the existing channels approval did not change (87.3% → 86.6%). A new
    digital partner arrived in 2025Q2 and supplied **65.5%** of applications, with weaker applicants and a ~60%
    approval rate. Underwriting is not stricter; the applicant population changed — the same shift that drives the default rise
    (L3.3) and the population drift of Day 9 (F11).

    ### 2.4 Save it as a config

    Ask Claude Code to turn the decomposition into a reusable `rcp_*` config (or a small new recipe) so next quarter's review is one
    command — and record it in `toolkit/catalogue.yaml`.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Most "why did X change?" questions are mix vs rate. Decompose first, then model the rate. Check the data definitions before
        believing either.

    **Live review checklist** (use it on a junior's capstone):

    1. Is the metric and comparison precisely defined (periods, population, exclusions)?
    2. Were data issues checked (duplicates, entry errors, definition changes)?
    3. Is mix separated from rate?
    4. Are drivers quantified with uncertainty?
    5. Does the recommendation follow, with a monitoring plan?
    6. Can I re-run it from the config?

    **Judgement points**

    - Retail: the margin dip was a **pricing decision** (regional price cuts) plus slightly deeper discounts — a question for the
      pricing committee, not a cost problem.
    - Finance: approvals fell because **who applies** changed. The real decision is whether the partner channel is worth it at its
      approval and default rates (Day 8 economics).

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask the junior: *"How much of the change is mix and how much is rate?"* If they can't answer, the analysis isn't done.

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Finding | Meaning | Next |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | mix ≫ rate | population/segment shift | ask why mix changed |
    | rate ≫ mix | within-segment change | model drivers of the rate |
    | both | combination | report both with numbers |
    | definition changed | artefact | restate with a consistent definition |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R5-13 · Basic · Mix vs rate decomposition"
    **Business framing:** Margin fell from 2025Q1 to 2025Q2.

    **Deliverable:** Margin rates by quarter; mix and rate effects over category × promotion segments; top three segment contributions.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 31.21% → 28.14%; mix 0.28 pp, rate -3.34 pp; top: Beverages|regular -0.99 pp · Fresh Produce|regular -0.78 pp · Dairy|regular -0.67 pp. *(tolerance: ± 0.05 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Almost all of the fall is within segments, led by regular-price lines — not a shift toward promotions.

        **Common wrong answer:** Blaming more promotions.

    **Leader drill:** Who owns the follow-up?

??? example "R5-14 · Applied · Why did regular margins fall?"
    **Business framing:** Explain the rate effect.

    **Deliverable:** Regular-line shelf price ÷ list and unit cost ÷ list by quarter and region; regular margin; Q3 recovery.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Price index 0.994 → 0.973 (Q3 0.993); cost index 0.666 → 0.667; regular margin 34.9% → 31.9%. *(tolerance: ± 0.005)*

        **Model answer:** Regional shelf prices were cut (North, South, West) while costs were flat; prices recovered in Q3 and so did margin.

        **Common wrong answer:** Blaming supplier costs.

    **Leader drill:** Was the price cut a good decision? What would you need to know (volume response, Day 8)?

??? example "R5-15 · Stretch · Promotion depth"
    **Business framing:** Promoted lines' margin also fell.

    **Deliverable:** Promoted-line margin by quarter; regression of margin rate on discount depth + category + quarter.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Promo margin 12.4% → 7.0%; each +1 pp discount costs 1.65 pp margin; Q2 effect -4.38 → -2.16 pp after adjusting for depth. *(tolerance: ± 0.3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Deeper discounts explain part of the promo-margin fall; the remainder reflects the same regional price cuts.

        **Common wrong answer:** Attributing the whole fall to discount depth.

    **Leader drill:** What guardrail would you put on promotion depth?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F5-14 · Basic · Why are approvals down?"
    **Business framing:** The sales director says underwriting got stricter.

    **Deliverable:** Approval rate by quarter and channel (booked + declined); partner share in 2025Q2.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 87.3% → 69.1%; non-partner 87.3% → 86.6%; partner 59.8% approval and 65.5% of applications. *(tolerance: ± 0.2 pp)*

        **Model answer:** A pure mix effect: a new, weaker applicant stream arrived; underwriting is unchanged.

        **Common wrong answer:** Comparing overall rates without the channel split.

    **Leader drill:** What is the right question for the sales director?

??? example "F5-15 · Applied · Link approvals to defaults"
    **Business framing:** Risk committee wants one story.

    **Deliverable:** Connect the approval drop to the default rise in 2025Q2–Q3 vintages (L3.3) and to channel default rates.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Partner customers default at ~13.49% vs ~3% elsewhere; their arrival explains both the approval drop and much of the default rise. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** One cause, two symptoms. Evaluate the partner channel's economics (approval, default, margin) rather than treating each metric separately.

        **Common wrong answer:** Two separate investigations.

    **Leader drill:** What decision does the committee actually need to take?

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "Can we flag returns abuse when only a few dozen customers do it?"

=== "Finance"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The accuracy paradox

    Predicting "not fraud" for everyone is **99.3% accurate** and catches nothing. With
    70 frauds among 10,000 test accounts, what matters is how many of the cases we
    investigate are fraud (precision) and how many frauds we catch (recall).

    ### 2.2 Fraud models (features: inquiries, new-to-bank, partner, digital share, bureau score, shared device/address counts)

    | Model | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Precision @50 | @100 | @200 | Recall @100 | Flagged at p ≥ 0.5 |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | logistic | 0.94 | 0.262 | 38.0% | 27.0% | 16.0% | 38.6% | 16 |
    | logistic_weighted | 0.94 | 0.276 | 40.0% | 27.0% | 16.5% | 38.6% | 1,497 |
    | gbm | 0.929 | 0.234 | 30.0% | 21.0% | 14.0% | 30.0% | 11 |
    

    - ROC-AUC looks excellent (~0.93); **PR-AUC (~0.25)** shows the real difficulty — it is measured against a 0.7%
      baseline.
    - At a capacity of 100 investigations, about one in four is fraud — a **39-fold**
      improvement on random checking, catching ~38.6% of fraud.
    - **Class weights don't change the ranking** (same precision@k) but inflate probabilities: the weighted model flags
      1,497 accounts at p ≥ 0.5 (mean predicted 19.6%
      vs a true 0.7%). Fine for ranking a queue; wrong as a probability unless recalibrated.
    - Accounts sharing a device with ≥ 5 others (41) are fraud 63.4% of the time — network
      features (F07, L8.6) carry much of the signal; the recipe config without them reaches lower precision.

    ### 2.3 The toolbox

    | Technique | Changes | Use when |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | threshold moving / top-k | decisions only | always — set by capacity or cost |
    | class weights | training emphasis; probabilities inflated | model ignores the minority class |
    | random over/under-sampling | same as weights; risk of overfitting duplicates | large data, quick experiments |
    | SMOTE (synthetic minority points) | same; can create unrealistic cases | numeric features, with CV *inside* the folds |
    | better features | everything | almost always the biggest win |

    Resampling must happen **inside** cross-validation folds (L6.4) — oversampling before splitting leaks copies into the test set.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Rare-event models are judged by precision and recall at the operating point, not accuracy or ROC-AUC alone. Weights and
        resampling change probabilities; thresholds change decisions; features change everything.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Size the queue first.** "We can work 100 cases" turns the problem into precision@100 — a number the fraud team understands.
    - **Never deploy weighted-model probabilities as risk estimates** without recalibration.
    - **Invest in features (device, network, velocity)** before algorithms.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"At our capacity, what share of alerts is fraud and what share of fraud do we catch?"*

## 5. Output anatomy — rare-event metrics

| Metric | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| PR-AUC | vs baseline = event rate |
| precision@k | share of the top-k queue that is positive |
| recall@k | share of all positives caught in the queue |
| flagged at a threshold | operational volume |
| mean predicted | calibration check (weights inflate it) |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Goal | Metric | Technique |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | fixed investigation capacity | precision@k | rank, take top k |
    | catch most events | recall at an acceptable precision | threshold moving |
    | probability for pricing/provisioning | calibration, Brier | no weights, or recalibrate |
    | minority ignored by model | PR-AUC | class weights |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R6-9 · Basic · Accuracy paradox for returns abuse"
    **Business framing:** 30 abusive customers among ~1,300.

    **Deliverable:** Accuracy of predicting 'not abusive' for everyone; which metrics to use instead.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** ≈ 97.7% accuracy with zero detection; use precision@k and PR-AUC. *(tolerance: ± 0.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** With rare positives accuracy is meaningless; size the review queue and measure precision within it.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting accuracy.

    **Leader drill:** How many accounts can loss prevention review per month?

??? example "R6-10 · Applied · Resampling inside folds"
    **Business framing:** A junior oversampled abusers before splitting.

    **Deliverable:** Explain the leak and the fix.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Duplicated minority rows appear in train and test → inflated scores. Resample inside each training fold only (pipeline). *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Any data-dependent preprocessing belongs inside the cross-validation loop.

        **Common wrong answer:** SMOTE on the whole dataset.

    **Leader drill:** Add this to your code-review checklist.

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F6-8 · Basic · Fraud queue of 100"
    **Business framing:** Investigators can work 100 accounts a month.

    **Deliverable:** Precision and recall at 100 for logistic, weighted logistic and GBM; ROC-AUC vs PR-AUC.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Logistic precision@100 27.0%, recall 38.6%; ROC-AUC 0.94, PR-AUC 0.262. *(tolerance: ± 5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** About a quarter of the queue is fraud — far above the 0.7% base rate.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting accuracy 99.3% of an all-zero model.

    **Leader drill:** Is a 27% hit rate good enough for the fraud team?

??? example "F6-9 · Applied · Class weights and probabilities"
    **Business framing:** The weighted model says many accounts are 'likely fraud'.

    **Deliverable:** Accounts flagged at p ≥ 0.5 and mean predicted probability, weighted vs unweighted.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Weighted flags 1,497 (mean 19.6%) vs 16 (mean 0.74%). *(tolerance: ± 10%)*

        **Model answer:** Weights inflate probabilities without improving ranking; recalibrate or rank.

        **Common wrong answer:** Telling customers they have a 40% fraud probability.

    **Leader drill:** Where could inflated probabilities cause harm?

??? example "F6-10 · Stretch · Network features"
    **Business framing:** Fraud rings share devices.

    **Deliverable:** Fraud rate among accounts sharing a device with ≥ 5 others.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 41 accounts; fraud rate 63.4%. *(tolerance: ± 3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Shared-device counts are the strongest fraud signal (F07); graph methods (L8.6) recover the rings.

        **Common wrong answer:** Ignoring the link table.

    **Leader drill:** What privacy controls apply to device data?

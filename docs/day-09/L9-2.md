## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "How long until a new customer comes back — and do loyalty members return faster?"

=== "Finance"

    > "When do customers close their accounts, and what drives it?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Censoring (F13)

    71.9% of accounts had **not** churned by the end of observation — we only know they lasted *at least* that long.
    Two tempting summaries are wrong:

    - "Churn rate is 28.1%" — ignores that accounts were observed for different lengths of time.
    - "Average time to churn is 10.6 months" — averages churners only, i.e. the people who left early.

    ### 2.2 Kaplan–Meier

    KM multiplies, month by month, the share of at-risk accounts that survive; censored accounts count while observed, then drop out.

    | Month | 6 | 12 | 24 |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Still open (KM) | 90.6% | 82.7% | 69.1% |

    The median is not reached (more than half survive 24 months), so report **restricted mean survival time**: over the first 24
    months, an account stays open **20.1 months** on average.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="km" data-caption="Kaplan–Meier curves keep every account, including the 72% still open."></div>

    ### 2.3 Comparing groups: log-rank

    | Group | Still open at 24 months |
    | --- | --- |
    | no complaints | 75.6% |
    | ≥ 1 complaint | 56.7% |

    Log-rank χ² = 934.0, p < 0.001.

    ### 2.4 Cox proportional hazards

    | Driver | Hazard ratio | 95% CI |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | complaints_12m | 1.677 | 1.633–1.722 |
    | digital_share_10pp | 0.892 | 0.883–0.902 |
    | interest_rate_pp | 1.011 | 1.006–1.016 |
    | tenure_years | 0.994 | 0.988–1.0 |
    

    Each complaint multiplies the monthly churn hazard by 1.677; each extra 10 points of digital share
    multiplies it by 0.892. **Proportional hazards check:** the complaints HR is 1.672
    in months 1–12 and 1.685 in months 13–24 — stable, so one HR is a fair summary.

    ### 2.5 Retail: time to repeat purchase

    Among customers seen in the last year, the median time from first to second visit is **230.0 days**
    (members 221.0, non-members 268.0); 55.3% had not yet
    returned (censored). Remember L9.1: members differ for many reasons.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Survival methods answer *when*, using everyone — including customers who haven't left yet.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Retention KPIs:** use KM survival at fixed horizons (6, 12, 24 months) by cohort, not "average lifetime of churners".
    - **Hazard ratios are associations**: complaints may signal a bad experience rather than cause churn — but they're an excellent
      trigger for a service-recovery call.
    - **Act where hazard is high**: complaints and low digital engagement identify accounts to save.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"How were customers who haven't churned yet handled?"*

## 5. Output anatomy

| Item | Read it as |
| --- | --- |
| KM step curve | share still active by time; ticks = censoring |
| median / RMST | typical survival; RMST when the median isn't reached |
| log-rank p | do curves differ overall |
| hazard ratio | multiplicative change in instantaneous risk |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Question | Method |
    | --- | --- |
    | survival curve with censoring | Kaplan–Meier |
    | compare two or more groups | log-rank |
    | several drivers | Cox PH |
    | hazards not proportional | stratified Cox, time-varying effects, or discrete-time logistic |
    | competing exits (churn vs default) | competing-risks models |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

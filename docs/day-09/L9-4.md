## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Frequency (F17)

    Claim frequency is **0.1387** claims per policy-year. Counts are over-dispersed (variance/mean
    1.154); with an exposure offset, negative binomial (α = 1.48) beats Poisson: AIC
    4754.5 vs 4799.7.

    ### 2.2 Severity

    Mean claim **4,856** vs median 2,413 — right-skewed (Gamma/lognormal). Three
    catastrophic claims are **29.5%** of all losses. Loss ratio 1.084 overall vs
    0.765 excluding them: price attritional losses with a GLM and add a **large-loss loading**
    (here ≈ 41.8% on attritional losses), or buy reinsurance.

    ### 2.3 Aggregate loss, VaR and ES

    Simulate 20,000 years: number of claims from the NB, severities resampled from actual claims (including the tail).

    | Measure (millions, one portfolio-year) | Value |
    | --- | --- |
    | Premium | 4.97 |
    | Expected loss | 5.39 |
    | VaR 99.5% (1-in-200 year) | 7.63 |
    | Expected shortfall 99.5% (average beyond VaR) | 7.94 |
    | Capital = VaR − expected loss | 2.24 |

    **VaR** answers "how bad at the 99.5th percentile"; **ES** answers "how bad on average when it's worse than that" — ES is never
    below VaR and sees the tail VaR ignores.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="var" data-caption="Drag the confidence level: VaR (red) is the threshold; expected shortfall (purple) is the average beyond it."></div>

    ### 2.4 Credit portfolio (one-factor Vasicek)

    Exposure 333.3M, PD 4.0%, LGD 60%, asset correlation 0.15. Defaults cluster when the economy (a common
    factor) is bad:

    | Millions | Base | Stress: PD × 2.5 (2025Q2 vintage behaviour) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Expected loss | 8.01 | 20.04 |
    | VaR 99.9% | 54.64 | 92.49 |
    | ES 99.9% | 60.38 | — |

    Capital (VaR − EL) is **46.63M** — about 5.8× expected loss, because
    correlation fattens the tail.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Expected loss is priced; unexpected loss is held as capital. Tails, not averages, sink portfolios.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Pricing:** the book runs at a loss ratio of 1.084 — premiums don't cover expected claims once catastrophes
      are included. Reprice, reinsure, or both.
    - **Risk appetite** is a statement like "we hold capital to survive a 1-in-200 year" — choose the percentile, then the measure (ES
      for tail sensitivity).
    - **Stress tests** ask "what if" (PD × 2.5) rather than "how likely" — essential when history lacks the scenario.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"What's the 1-in-200 loss, what's the average beyond it, and which assumption moves them most?"*

### Retail · NorthStar

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F9-9 · Basic · Frequency model"
    **Business framing:** Price the motor/home book.

    **Deliverable:** Poisson vs NB with exposure offset.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** AIC 4799.7 vs 4754.5; α 1.48; frequency 0.1387. *(tolerance: ± 1%)*

        **Model answer:** NB preferred.

        **Common wrong answer:** Ignoring exposure.

    **Leader drill:** Impact on pricing?

??? example "F9-10 · Applied · Large losses"
    **Business framing:** Three huge claims.

    **Deliverable:** Top-3 share; loss ratio with/without; loading.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 29.5%; LR 1.084 vs 0.765; loading 41.8%. *(tolerance: ± 1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Price attritional + loading or reinsure.

        **Common wrong answer:** Deleting catastrophic claims.

    **Leader drill:** Reinsure or retain?

??? example "F9-11 · Applied · Aggregate VaR/ES"
    **Business framing:** Capital for the insurance book.

    **Deliverable:** Monte Carlo EL, VaR99.5, ES99.5 vs premium.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** EL 5.39M; VaR 7.63M; ES 7.94M; premium 4.97M. *(tolerance: ± 5%)*

        **Model answer:** Premium below expected loss; capital ≈ 2.24M.

        **Common wrong answer:** Normal approximation of a skewed loss.

    **Leader drill:** Risk appetite statement?

??? example "F9-12 · Stretch · Credit stress"
    **Business framing:** Loan book downturn.

    **Deliverable:** Vasicek EL, VaR99.9, ES; stress PD × 2.5.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** EL 8.01M, VaR 54.64M, ES 60.38M; stress EL 20.04M, VaR 92.49M. *(tolerance: ± 5%)*

        **Model answer:** Correlation fattens the tail; capital ≫ EL.

        **Common wrong answer:** Summing independent defaults.

    **Leader drill:** Which assumption matters most (ρ, PD, LGD)?

**Objectives** — you can: estimate a difference-in-differences effect with store and week fixed effects and check parallel trends;
explain why adjusting for observed demographics does not remove self-selection; use within-unit (before/after joining) comparisons;
choose between DiD, matching, RDD, synthetic control and uplift models; and defend a causal claim to a sceptical executive.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "Did the Hair Care promotion in 20 test stores raise sales — and does the loyalty programme really make customers spend 38% more?"

=== "Finance"

    > "Would raising credit limits make customers more profitable, or just riskier? What can we claim without a randomised test?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Difference-in-differences (R09)

    Twenty test stores ran the Hair Care promotion in weeks 70–77; twenty control stores did not. Comparing post-period averages alone
    gives **+19.2%** with p = 0.4397 — stores differ so much in size that the noise swamps the
    effect. DiD compares each store with **itself** before the test and with the controls over the same weeks:

    | Estimator | Lift | 95% CI |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Post-period means only | +19.2% | not significant (p = 0.4397) |
    | Growth ratio vs controls (L4.5) | +13.6% | 5.5% to 21.7% |
    | Two-way fixed effects, log units (store + week) | **+14.5%** | 6.2% to 23.4% |

    Both DiD estimates cover the planted +12%.

    **Parallel trends.** DiD assumes test and control would have moved together without the promotion. Check the pre-period: the
    difference in weekly trend (weeks 54–69) is 0.1% per week, p = 0.861 — no evidence
    of diverging trends. An **event-study plot** (test–control gap by week, relative to week 69) should hover around zero before week
    70 and jump after; weekly points are noisy (largest pre-period gap ±12.6%), so judge the pattern, not single weeks.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="did_event" data-caption="Each point is the test–control gap in one week relative to week 69: flat before, up during the promotion."></div>

    ### 2.2 Self-selection: the loyalty programme (R20)

    | Comparison | "Effect" of loyalty on basket value |
    | --- | --- |
    | Members vs non-members (naive) | **+37.7%** |
    | … adjusted for age, household size, store, week | +33.5% |
    | Within customer: 265 customers who joined during the panel, before vs after joining, vs never-members (two-way FE) | **+1.6%** (CI -11.8% to 17.1%) |

    The planted causal effect is +5.0%. Demographic adjustment barely helps because people join **because** they
    already spend more: joiners' baskets were 31.0% bigger than never-members' **before** they joined. Only
    designs that remove each customer's fixed spending level (or a randomised offer) get close.

    ### 2.3 The causal toolbox

    | Design | Needs | Key assumption | Example |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Randomised test | ability to assign | none beyond execution (SRM, L4.1) | F16: challenger +6.0pp cure (CI 2.4 to 9.7) |
    | DiD / two-way FE | before & after, treated & control | parallel trends | R09 promotion, R20 joiners |
    | Matching / propensity scores | rich pre-treatment covariates | no unobserved confounders | loyalty if we had pre-join spend |
    | Regression discontinuity | a score cut-off | units just either side comparable | limit increase at a score threshold |
    | Synthetic control | one treated unit, many donors | weighted donors track pre-period | one region's new pricing |
    | Uplift models | randomised data | heterogeneity is stable | whom to target with a retention offer |

    **Uplift** (T-learner): fit outcome models on treated and control separately from a randomised test; target customers with the
    largest predicted difference — not those most likely to respond anyway ("sure things").

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Correlation in observational data mixes the effect with **who chose** the treatment. DiD and within-unit comparisons remove
        fixed differences; randomisation removes all of them.

    **Judgement points**

    - **"Members spend 38% more" is a selection story**, not a programme effect; the defensible figure is about +1.6%
      (wide CI) — or run a randomised enrolment incentive.
    - **Ask which assumption the design rests on** — parallel trends, no hidden confounders, comparability at the cut-off — and what
      evidence supports it.
    - **Prefer a test** whenever you can assign treatment; observational methods are for when you cannot.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Who chose to be treated, and what else is different about them? Show me the pre-period."*

## 5. Output anatomy

| Item | Read it as |
| --- | --- |
| interaction coefficient (treat × post) | the DiD effect on log scale → exp(b) − 1 |
| clustered SE | accounts for repeated weeks per store |
| pre-trend p-value / event plot | evidence for parallel trends |
| within-unit estimate | effect net of each unit's fixed level |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Situation | Design |
    | --- | --- |
    | can randomise | A/B test (L4) |
    | treated & control groups, pre and post | DiD |
    | people opt in, pre-period data available | within-unit FE / matching on pre-period |
    | eligibility by score cut-off | RDD |
    | one treated region/store | synthetic control |
    | decide whom to treat | uplift model on test data |

=== "Leader"

    | Claim | Ask for |
    | --- | --- |
    | "programme members do better" | pre-join comparison or randomised offer |
    | "since launch, sales rose" | control group over the same period |
    | "model-targeted customers responded" | random holdout within the targeted group |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R9-1 · Basic · Hair Care DiD"
    **Business framing:** Did the promotion work?

    **Deliverable:** Naive post-only comparison vs two-way FE DiD on log units, weeks 54–77.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Naive +19.2% (p = 0.4397); DiD +14.5% (CI 6.2 to 23.4). *(tolerance: ± 2 pp)*

        **Model answer:** DiD removes store size differences; covers the planted +12%.

        **Common wrong answer:** Concluding 'no effect' from the post-only test.

    **Leader drill:** Roll out chain-wide?

??? example "R9-2 · Applied · Parallel trends"
    **Business framing:** Can we trust the DiD?

    **Deliverable:** Pre-period trend difference and event-study plot.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Slope difference 0.1%/week, p = 0.861. *(tolerance: ± 0.5)*

        **Model answer:** No evidence of diverging pre-trends; weekly gaps noisy around zero.

        **Common wrong answer:** Judging by a single pre-period week.

    **Leader drill:** What would make you doubt it?

??? example "R9-3 · Applied · Placebo test"
    **Business framing:** Is the method finding effects that aren't there?

    **Deliverable:** Run the same DiD for a category without a promotion (Personal Care).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Estimate near 0 with CI covering 0. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Placebos validate the design.

        **Common wrong answer:** Skipping falsification checks.

    **Leader drill:** Which placebo would convince the CFO?

??? example "R9-4 · Basic · Loyalty selection"
    **Business framing:** Marketing: members spend 38% more.

    **Deliverable:** Naive and demographics-adjusted gaps; joiners' pre-join spend vs never-members.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Naive +37.7%; adjusted +33.5%; pre-join gap +31.0%. *(tolerance: ± 2 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Members were bigger spenders before joining — selection.

        **Common wrong answer:** Crediting the programme with the whole gap.

    **Leader drill:** Reword the marketing claim.

??? example "R9-5 · Stretch · Within-customer effect"
    **Business framing:** What does joining actually do?

    **Deliverable:** Two-way FE (customer + week) for joiners vs never-members.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** +1.6% (CI -11.8 to 17.1); 265 joiners. *(tolerance: ± 3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Close to the true +5%; wide CI → test with randomised incentives.

        **Common wrong answer:** Matching only on demographics.

    **Leader drill:** Design a randomised enrolment test.

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F9-1 · Basic · Gold standard"
    **Business framing:** Collections strategy effect.

    **Deliverable:** Quote the randomised F16 estimate.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** +6.0pp (CI 2.4 to 9.7). *(tolerance: ± 0.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Randomisation removes confounding.

        **Common wrong answer:** Comparing collectors' historical results.

    **Leader drill:** Roll out?

??? example "F9-2 · Applied · Limit increase RDD"
    **Business framing:** Limit increases are given above a score of 650.

    **Deliverable:** Design a regression discontinuity.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Compare spend/default just above vs below 650 (bandwidth), check no manipulation of scores and covariate balance at the cut-off. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Local effect at the threshold.

        **Common wrong answer:** Comparing all increased vs not increased.

    **Leader drill:** What does the effect at 650 tell you about 600?

??? example "F9-3 · Stretch · Uplift targeting"
    **Business framing:** Retention offers.

    **Deliverable:** Describe a T-learner uplift model and evaluation.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Randomised offer; separate models for treated/control; target highest predicted difference; evaluate with Qini/uplift curves. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Target persuadables, not sure things.

        **Common wrong answer:** Targeting highest churn risk.

    **Leader drill:** Holdout size?

## 1. The two briefs (pick one; Leaders review both)

=== "Retail"

    > **"Did the Q4 promotion plan work?"** — Trading Director, NorthStar

    The plan for 4 November–31 December 2025 aimed to cut discount spend while protecting sales. Compare with the same weeks of
    2024, decide whether the plan worked, and recommend next year's plan.

=== "Finance"

    > **"Why did default rise in the latest vintage — and what do we do?"** — Chief Risk Officer, Meridian

    The 2025Q3 vintage is defaulting far faster than 2025Q1. Explain why, how much is mix vs deterioration, and what to change.

## 2. What the data show (verify every number yourself)

=== "Retail"

    | Nov 4 – Dec 31 | 2024 | 2025 |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Revenue (panel sample) | 14,701 | 15,752 (+7.2%) |
    | Margin rate | 30.88% | 31.86% |
    | Promo share of revenue | 13.0% | 13.3% |
    | Discount cost | 1,030 | 990 (-3.9%) |

    In 2025, promoted lines earned a 15.7% margin vs 34.3% for full-price lines. By
    mechanic:

    | Mechanic | Revenue | Discount cost | Cost per unit revenue | Margin rate |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | TPR | 1,247 | 268 | 0.21 | 20.0% |
    | multibuy | 473 | 128 | 0.27 | 15.6% |
    | coupon | 218 | 38 | 0.18 | 20.0% |
    | BOGO | 151 | 142 | 0.94 | -25.2% |
    

    **What the data cannot show by themselves:** whether the +7.2% revenue is *because of* the plan — the year-on-year
    comparison has no control (traffic, prices, the online channel doubling — R19). Bring in causal evidence: the Hair Care test
    (+14.5%, L9.1), Snacks promo ROI 0.61 and cannibalisation (L8.4), the Q2 margin investigation (L5.7).

=== "Finance"

    Default rate: **2.88%** (2025Q1) → **10.95%** (2025Q3), +8.07pp.

    | Channel | 2025Q1 | 2025Q3 | Share of 2025Q3 |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | branch | 3.1% | 5.46% | 23.7% |
    | online | 2.62% | 7.17% | 19.1% |
    | partner | — | 14.48% | 57.2% |
    

    **Mix vs rate decomposition.** At the 2025Q1 channel mix (no partner), 2025Q3's within-channel rates would give
    6.24%. So of the +8.07pp rise, **4.71pp (58%) is the
    partner mix** and 3.36pp is deterioration **within** existing channels — consistent with the steeper
    utilisation slope (concept drift, L9.6). Partner applicants' median bureau score is 658.0 vs
    699.0 for other channels.

    Bring in: vintage curves (L8.7), PSI 0.269 and calibration (4.97% predicted vs
    9.92% actual, L9.6), the collections test (+6.0pp cures, L4.5), fairness of any cut-off change (L9.5).

    This whole lesson is a hands-on lab: every step is done by directing Claude Code on real data.

## 3. Deliverables

1. A one-page readout (pyramid, L9.7) with the decision you want.
2. An appendix: methods, data checks, every number with its source file and config.
3. Committed configs that reproduce your numbers.
4. Five minutes presenting + ten minutes of challenge.

## 4. Challenge panel (Leaders sit on it)

| Panel question | What a strong answer includes |
| --- | --- |
| "How much of this is real vs mix/seasonality?" | decomposition or control group with numbers |
| "How sure are you?" | CIs, sensitivity, what would change your mind |
| "What exactly should we do Monday?" | action, owner, metric, test |
| "What did you check in the data?" | quality checks, anomalies found |
| "Who could this harm?" | fairness / customer impact (L9.5) |

## 5. Rubric

Same five criteria as the transfer test (correctness, assumptions, interpretation, action, clarity & reuse), 1–5 each; pass ≥ 3 on all
and ≥ 18 in total.

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R10-6 · Basic · Q4 headline numbers"
    **Business framing:** Did the Q4 plan work?

    **Deliverable:** Revenue, margin rate, promo share, discount cost Nov 4–Dec 31, 2025 vs 2024.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Revenue +7.2%; margin 30.88% → 31.86%; promo share 13.0% → 13.3%; discount cost -3.9%. *(tolerance: ± 0.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Less discount spend, higher margin rate, revenue up.

        **Common wrong answer:** Stopping here and declaring success.

    **Leader drill:** What else changed between the two years?

??? example "R10-7 · Applied · Mechanic economics"
    **Business framing:** Which mechanics to keep?

    **Deliverable:** Revenue, discount cost per unit revenue and margin by promo type (2025).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** TPR cost 0.21/rev, margin 20.0%; multibuy cost 0.27/rev, margin 15.6%; coupon cost 0.18/rev, margin 20.0%; BOGO cost 0.94/rev, margin -25.2%;  *(tolerance: ± 0.02)*

        **Model answer:** Rank mechanics by margin and cost; combine with uplift evidence.

        **Common wrong answer:** Ranking by revenue only.

    **Leader drill:** Which mechanic would you test cutting?

??? example "R10-8 · Stretch · Attribution"
    **Business framing:** How much of the growth is the plan?

    **Deliverable:** Explain why YoY can't attribute; assemble causal evidence.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** No control; online share doubled; use R09 DiD (+14.5%), Snacks ROI 0.61, cannibalisation; propose a store-level holdout for 2026. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Triangulate and design the test.

        **Common wrong answer:** Attributing all growth to the plan.

    **Leader drill:** Size the 2026 holdout (rcp_power).

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F10-3 · Basic · Default rise"
    **Business framing:** CRO question.

    **Deliverable:** Default rate 2025Q1 vs 2025Q3; by channel.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 2.88% → 10.95%; branch 5.46%; online 7.17%; partner 14.48%;  *(tolerance: ± 0.2 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Partner channel defaults far higher.

        **Common wrong answer:** Calendar-month delinquency only.

    **Leader drill:** What's the first question to the partner manager?

??? example "F10-4 · Applied · Mix vs rate"
    **Business framing:** How much is mix?

    **Deliverable:** Counterfactual 2025Q3 rate at 2025Q1 mix; split of the rise.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Counterfactual 6.24%; mix 4.71pp (58%), within-channel 3.36pp. *(tolerance: ± 0.3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Both mix and deterioration — two different fixes.

        **Common wrong answer:** Blaming the partner channel for everything.

    **Leader drill:** Which fix first?

??? example "F10-5 · Applied · Partner quality"
    **Business framing:** Are partner applicants riskier?

    **Deliverable:** Median bureau scores by channel group.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 2025Q1 700 · 2025Q3 partner 658 · 2025Q3 other 699. *(tolerance: ± 3)*

        **Model answer:** Lower bureau scores via partner.

        **Common wrong answer:** Assuming same risk because the score 'covers it'.

    **Leader drill:** Cut-off or pricing change?

??? example "F10-6 · Stretch · Readout"
    **Business framing:** CRO, one page.

    **Deliverable:** Pyramid readout with decision, three supports, risks, next steps.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Tighten partner cut-off and recalibrate; mix 58% of rise; calibration 4.97% vs 9.92%; collections +6.0pp; fairness check on any cut-off. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Answer first, quantified, owners.

        **Common wrong answer:** Method-first narrative.

    **Leader drill:** What's your ask of the CRO?

??? example "F10-7 · Stretch · Fairness of the fix"
    **Business framing:** Tightening the partner cut-off.

    **Deliverable:** Check approval-rate ratio by group before/after the change.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Simulate the cut-off; report DI by group and region; document justification. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Policy changes need fairness review (L9.5).

        **Common wrong answer:** Assuming a risk-based cut-off is automatically fair.

    **Leader drill:** Who approves?

**Objectives** — you can: draw a causal diagram (DAG) for a business question; identify confounders, mediators and colliders; find
backdoor paths and the adjustment set that closes them; explain why adjusting for the wrong variable creates bias; and answer
"what else could explain this?" before anyone acts on an observational comparison.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "How much do promotions — and displays — really lift units per line?"

=== "Finance"

    > "In our booked accounts, past delinquency barely relates to bureau score. Does delinquency history matter less than we thought?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Drawing the diagram

    Nodes are variables; arrows mean "directly causes". Promotions example:

    ```mermaid
    graph LR
      F[Store format] --> P[Promotion]
      F --> Q[Units per line]
      P --> D[Display]
      P --> Q
      D --> Q
    ```

    - **Confounder** (format): causes both treatment and outcome → opens a *backdoor* path P ← F → Q. **Adjust for it.**
    - **Mediator** (display): lies on the path P → D → Q. Adjust for it only if you want the *direct* effect of promotion excluding
      the display it brings.
    - **Collider**: a variable caused by two others (see 2.3). **Never adjust for it** — doing so opens a spurious path.

    ### 2.2 Promotions: the estimate depends on what you adjust for

    Displays accompany 92.6% of promoted lines vs 3.0% of others; 62.8%
    of Express lines are promoted. Effect of promotion on units per line (log-linear model, % change):

    | Adjustment set | Promotion effect | 95% CI | What it estimates |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | none | **-11.9%** | -13.5 to -10.3 | biased by format (Simpson, L2.4) |
    | format | 21.7% | 19.2 to 24.3 | total effect: promotion + the display it brings |
    | format + display | **16.6%** | 12.2 to 21.1 | direct effect of promotion |

    The planted truth is +15% for promotion and +10% for display. The display effect
    estimate is 4.9% (CI 1.2 to 8.7) — imprecise, because only
    354 promoted lines had no display and 576 non-promoted lines had one. Observational
    data can barely separate the two levers; a test that varies them independently can (R15).

    ### 2.3 Colliders and selection: approved-only data

    ```mermaid
    graph LR
      B[Bureau score] --> A[Approved]
      H[Delinquency history] --> A
      B --> Y[Default]
      H --> Y
    ```

    Approval depends on both bureau score and delinquency history, so **approval is a collider**. Looking only at booked accounts
    *conditions on* it.

    | Measure | All 30,240 applicants | Booked accounts only |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Correlation: bureau score vs delinquency count | **-0.289** | **-0.104** |
    | Correlation: inquiries vs delinquency | 0.101 | 0.011 |
    | Share with any delinquency, bureau ≤ 600 | 48.5% | 30.7% |

    Selection removed the applicants who had *both* low scores and delinquencies, flattening the relationship. A model trained on
    booked accounts will under-state how much delinquency history matters for the population that applies (reject inference, L8.7,
    F18).

    ### 2.4 The backdoor criterion in practice

    1. Draw the DAG with domain experts (Claude Code can draft it; you correct it).
    2. List every path from treatment to outcome.
    3. Block backdoor paths (arrows *into* the treatment) by adjusting for confounders.
    4. Do not adjust for mediators (unless you want a direct effect) or colliders, or for anything caused by the treatment.
    5. If an important confounder is unmeasured, observational adjustment cannot give a causal answer — design a test (Day 4) or use
       a quasi-experimental method (Day 9).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Causal effects need the right comparison. A DAG makes assumptions explicit: adjust for confounders, not mediators or
        colliders. Selected data (approved, retained, surviving) condition on a collider and distort relationships.

    **Judgement points**

    - **"What else could explain this?"** is the leader's question. Format explains the "negative" promotion effect; the display
      explains part of the "positive" one.
    - **"Controlling for everything" is not safe.** Adjusting for a mediator removes part of the effect you want; adjusting for a
      collider manufactures bias.
    - **Booked-only analyses need a warning label.** Any credit relationship estimated on approved accounts is filtered by the
      approval rule.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask the junior to draw the DAG on the whiteboard before running a regression, and to point at each adjusted variable and say
        whether it is a confounder, mediator or collider.

## 5. Output anatomy — a DAG-based analysis plan

| Element | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Causal question | effect of promotion on units per line (total) |
| DAG | Mermaid diagram with sources for each arrow |
| Adjustment set | {format} for total effect; {format, display} for direct effect |
| Excluded on purpose | basket size (mediator/consequence), post-promotion variables |
| Unmeasured confounders | competitor activity → sensitivity analysis |
| Design recommendation | test promotions with and without displays |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R4-10 · Basic · Promotion effect under three adjustment sets"
    **Business framing:** The promotions team asks how much promotions lift units per line.

    **Deliverable:** Log-linear models of units per line on promotion with (a) no controls, (b) format, (c) format + display.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** (a) -11.9%; (b) 21.7%; (c) 16.6% (CI 12.2 to 21.1). *(tolerance: ± 1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Unadjusted is biased by format (promotions concentrate in small Express baskets). Adjusting for format gives the total effect (promotion + display); adding display gives the direct effect ≈ planted +15%.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting −12%, or adding every available variable without a DAG.

    **Leader drill:** Which number answers 'should we run more promotions?' and why?

??? example "R4-11 · Applied · Can we separate display from promotion?"
    **Business framing:** Merchandising wants separate ROI for displays.

    **Deliverable:** Counts of promoted lines without display and non-promoted lines with display; display effect with CI.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 354 and 576 lines; display +4.9% (CI 1.2 to 8.7). *(tolerance: ± 1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Very few lines vary one lever without the other, so the display effect is imprecise (truth +10%). Recommend a factorial test: promotion × display.

        **Common wrong answer:** Quoting the display coefficient as precise ROI.

    **Leader drill:** Sketch the 2×2 test design and its unit of randomisation.

??? example "R4-12 · Stretch · Draw the loyalty DAG"
    **Business framing:** Marketing claims loyalty members spend 37.7% more because of the programme.

    **Deliverable:** A DAG (Mermaid) with membership, spend, prior spend, household size, store format, tenure; the adjustment set; what cannot be fixed by adjustment.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Confounders: prior spend, household size, tenure, home-store format. Unmeasured: shopping enthusiasm → need pre/post (DiD) or matching on pre-period spend (Day 9). *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Self-selection: big spenders join. Adjust for pre-programme spend at minimum; the planted true effect (+5%) is far below the naive gap.

        **Common wrong answer:** Adjusting for post-joining visits (a mediator).

    **Leader drill:** What evidence would convince you the programme pays for itself?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F4-10 · Basic · Collider bias in booked accounts"
    **Business framing:** A junior finds delinquency history 'barely relates' to bureau score in booked accounts.

    **Deliverable:** Correlation of bureau score with delinquency count among all applicants vs booked; share with any delinquency at bureau ≤ 600.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** -0.289 (all) vs -0.104 (booked); 48.5% vs 30.7% with delinquency at bureau ≤ 600. *(tolerance: ± 0.01)*

        **Model answer:** Approval depends on both variables — a collider. Conditioning on approval removes low-score, delinquent applicants and weakens the observed relationship.

        **Common wrong answer:** Concluding delinquency history doesn't matter.

    **Leader drill:** Where else in the bank do analyses condition on a collider?

??? example "F4-11 · Applied · DAG for credit-limit increases"
    **Business framing:** Accounts that received limit increases default less. Did the increases cause it?

    **Deliverable:** A DAG with limit increase, default, bureau score, utilisation, payment history, income; adjustment set; what is unmeasured.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Increases are granted to good payers: payment history and bureau score confound. Adjust for pre-increase risk; unmeasured income shocks remain → quasi-experiment (RDD at the eligibility cut-off, Day 9). *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Naive comparison credits the policy with the selection of good customers.

        **Common wrong answer:** Adjusting for post-increase utilisation (a mediator).

    **Leader drill:** What design would give a credible answer?

??? example "F4-12 · Stretch · Mediator trap"
    **Business framing:** An analyst adds 'months in collections' to a model of the challenger's effect on recovery.

    **Deliverable:** Explain why this biases the estimate and what to report instead.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Months in collections is affected by the script (a mediator); adjusting removes part of the effect. Report the total effect from the randomised comparison. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** In a randomised test, the simple comparison is the causal effect; adjusting for post-treatment variables can only hurt.

        **Common wrong answer:** Adding all available variables 'to be safe'.

    **Leader drill:** What rule would you set for covariates in experiment readouts?

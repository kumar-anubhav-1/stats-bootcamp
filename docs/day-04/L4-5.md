**Objectives** — you can: run a complete experiment workflow from design to decision memo; analyse a cluster-randomised test at the
right unit; check balance and sample ratio; read the primary metric with and without CUPED; check guardrails; and write a one-page
decision memo that a leader can defend.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "Should we roll out the Hair Care in-store feature to all stores?"

=== "Finance"

    > "Should the challenger collections script replace the champion?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The workflow

    | Step | Retail — Hair Care (R09) | Finance — collections (F16) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | 1. Design | 20 v 20 stores, stratified by format; weeks 70–77; baseline 54–69 | 30+ DPD accounts, 80/20 champion/challenger |
    | 2. Power (L3.6) | MDE 11.2% at 80% power | power 37% for +3 pp (under-powered) |
    | 3. Sample ratio | 20 : 20 ✓ | 20.5% challenger vs 20% planned, p = 0.418 ✓ |
    | 4. Balance | pre-period units p = 0.94 | standardised differences ≤ 0.046 on bureau, utilisation, balance, DTI |
    | 5. Primary | growth index +13.6% (CI 5.5 – 21.7); CUPED +17.3% (CI 9.2 – 25.5) | cure +6.0 pp (CI 2.4 – 9.7), p = 0.00099 |
    | 6. Secondary | Hair Care revenue +11.6% (CI 4.5 – 18.8) | recovery amount +411 (CI -251 to 1,073) |
    | 7. Guardrails | other categories' revenue -1.1% (CI -4.2 – 1.9) — no cannibalisation detected | complaints 0.655 vs 0.685 per account (p = 0.357) |
    | 8. Decision | roll out; the 10% threshold lies inside the CI → monitor | replace champion; CI lower bound 2.4 pp ≥ 2 pp threshold |

    ### 2.2 Cluster tests: analyse at the cluster

    Stores were randomised, so the store is the unit. Line-level panel data had only 28 and
    32 Hair Care lines in the test window and treat lines as independent — wrong on both counts (L3.4). CUPED
    on store-level pre-period units shrinks the SE 6.1-fold (L4.2).

    ### 2.3 The decision memo (one page)

    1. **Decision requested** and recommendation.
    2. **What we tested**, unit, dates, sample sizes, pre-registered primary metric.
    3. **Hygiene:** SRM, balance, data issues found.
    4. **Result:** effect in business units with CI; probability of exceeding the threshold (L4.3).
    5. **Guardrails and risks.**
    6. **Money:** expected annual value with a range (Monte Carlo, L2.5).
    7. **Next steps:** rollout plan, holdout, monitoring metric and review date.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        A test is only as good as its weakest step: design, hygiene, analysis unit, guardrails, decision rule. Your job is to check
        the chain and make the call — including when the evidence is good but not decisive.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Hair Care:** effect is real (CI excludes 0) and probably above the 10% rollout threshold, but not certainly. Roll out with a
      **holdout of a few stores** to keep measuring; the cost of being wrong is modest and reversible.
    - **Collections:** the effect is solid despite the under-powered design (the true effect was larger than planned). Replace the
      champion, keep a 10% holdout of the old script for a quarter to confirm, and size the next test properly.
    - **Guardrails can veto.** Had complaints risen significantly, the cure-rate gain would not justify the switch without a
      conversation with conduct risk.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Have the junior present the memo in five minutes; your questions: *SRM? unit? CI vs threshold? guardrails? holdout?*

## 5. Output anatomy — the decision memo

| Section | Must contain |
| --- | --- |
| Recommendation | one sentence, first line |
| Evidence | effect + CI + unit + n |
| Hygiene | SRM p, balance, data caveats |
| Guardrails | each with CI; any breach explained |
| Value | annualised impact range |
| Plan | rollout, holdout, monitoring, review date |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Result pattern | Recommendation |
    | --- | --- |
    | CI above threshold, guardrails fine | ship (holdout optional) |
    | CI above 0, threshold inside CI | ship with holdout / extend |
    | CI includes 0, MDE large | inconclusive — redesign (CUPED, more units) |
    | guardrail breach | don't ship; investigate |
    | SRM | stop; fix; re-run |

=== "Leader"

    | Pressure | Response | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | "just roll it out everywhere" | keep a holdout | measure post-launch effect |
    | "the test was small, so ignore it" | read CI and MDE | evidence, not size alone |
    | "complaints are noise" | show CI | guardrail pre-agreed |

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R4-13 · Basic · Hair Care guardrail — cannibalisation?"
    **Business framing:** Category directors worry the Hair Care feature stole sales from other categories.

    **Deliverable:** Growth index of revenue in all other categories per store (weeks 70–77 vs 54–69), test vs control.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** -1.1% (CI -4.2 to 1.9), p = 0.453. *(tolerance: ± 0.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** No detectable effect on other categories; a loss larger than ~4% can be ruled out.

        **Common wrong answer:** Checking only Hair Care.

    **Leader drill:** What size of cannibalisation would change your decision?

??? example "R4-14 · Applied · The rollout decision"
    **Business framing:** The trading director asks for a go/no-go on the Hair Care feature.

    **Deliverable:** Primary (growth index and CUPED) with CIs, secondary (revenue), guardrail, decision vs 10% threshold.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Units +13.6% (CI 5.5 – 21.7); CUPED +17.3%; revenue +11.6%; other categories -1.1% (n.s.). *(tolerance: ± 0.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Go, with a holdout of ~4 stores and a 12-week review: the effect is real; it is probably but not certainly above 10%.

        **Common wrong answer:** No-go because the CI includes values below 10%.

    **Leader drill:** Present the decision in three sentences.

??? example "R4-15 · Stretch · The decision memo"
    **Business framing:** Write the memo for the trading committee.

    **Deliverable:** One page following the 7-part template, with a Monte Carlo range for annual value (assume 5,000 annual Hair Care units per store before the feature, margin 2.10 per unit, feature cost 400 per store per year).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Effect range from the CI; annual value ≈ 40 stores × 5,000 × lift × 2.10 − 16,000; P(value < 0) from simulating the lift from its sampling distribution. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** With the point estimate (~13.6%) value ≈ 40 × 5,000 × 0.136 × 2.10 − 16,000 ≈ 41,000 a year; the lower CI bound still covers the cost. Recommend rollout with a holdout.

        **Common wrong answer:** A memo with only a p-value.

    **Leader drill:** What would make you reverse the decision at the 12-week review?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F4-13 · Basic · Balance and guardrails"
    **Business framing:** Validate the challenger test before the decision.

    **Deliverable:** Standardised mean differences on bureau score, utilisation, balance and DTI; complaints guardrail with CI.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** SMDs bureau_score -0.021 · utilisation 0.01 · balance_current 0.042 · dti -0.046 (all < 0.1); complaints 0.655 vs 0.685 (CI -0.095 to 0.034), p = 0.357. *(tolerance: ± 0.005)*

        **Model answer:** Arms are balanced; complaints do not rise (an increase above ~0.03 per account can be ruled out).

        **Common wrong answer:** Testing balance with p-values on 4,000 accounts instead of SMDs.

    **Leader drill:** Which balance threshold would you set?

??? example "F4-14 · Applied · Primary and secondary results"
    **Business framing:** Prepare the results table for the decision memo.

    **Deliverable:** Cure rate difference with CI and p; recovery amount difference with CI; decision vs a 2 pp threshold.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Cure +6.0 pp (CI 2.4–9.7), p = 0.00099; recovery +411 (CI -251 to 1,073, n.s.). *(tolerance: ± 0.1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** The cure gain clears the 2 pp threshold; recovery is too noisy to conclude. Switch, with a holdout to keep measuring recovery.

        **Common wrong answer:** Claiming higher recovery.

    **Leader drill:** Which secondary metric would you power the next test for?

??? example "F4-15 · Stretch · The decision memo"
    **Business framing:** Write the memo for the Head of Collections.

    **Deliverable:** One page, 7-part template, including annualised value (assume 1,400 new 30+ DPD accounts a month and 600 saved per extra cure).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Extra cures per year ≈ 1,400 × 12 × 0.06 ≈ 1,000 → ≈ 600,000 a year at the point estimate; lower CI bound (2.4 pp) ≈ 240,000. *(tolerance: ± 10%)*

        **Model answer:** Recommend switching to the challenger, keeping a 10% holdout for one quarter, with complaints as the veto guardrail.

        **Common wrong answer:** Quoting only the point estimate.

    **Leader drill:** What would trigger reverting to the champion?

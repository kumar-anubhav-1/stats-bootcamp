## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The chart menu

    | Chart | Shows | Best for | Watch out |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Histogram** | counts in bins | shape of one variable | bin width changes the story |
    | **Density (KDE)** | smoothed histogram | comparing shapes | smooths away spikes; invents values below 0 |
    | **Box plot** | median, IQR, whiskers, points beyond | comparing many groups | hides bimodality |
    | **Violin** | box + density | groups with different shapes | needs enough data |
    | **ECDF** | share of values ≤ x | comparing distributions exactly; reading percentiles | less familiar to audiences |
    | **Q-Q plot** | data quantiles vs a theoretical distribution | checking normality / fit | points on the line = good fit |
    | **Bar** (categories) / **line** (time) / **scatter** (two numerics) / **heatmap** (two categories) | — | — | — |

    ### 2.2 Bins and scales

    For basket value, Sturges' rule suggests **13** bins and Freedman-Diaconis **308** — on raw,
    heavy-tailed data neither works: either everything sits in the first bar or hundreds of bins are empty. On a **log scale** the skew
    falls from 10.54 to -0.5 and the shape becomes readable. Always label log axes clearly.

    Logs cannot show zeros: **9.9%** of bank balances are exactly zero — report them separately when you plot on a log scale.

    ### 2.3 Reading an ECDF

    The ECDF at x is the share of values at or below x. Two groups: the curve **further left** has lower values. For bureau scores, the
    share below 650 is 19.9% for earlier vintages and **33.5%** for 2025Q2–Q3;
    the median falls from 699.0 to 675.0. The late curve sits left everywhere: the whole
    population shifted.

    ### 2.4 Reading a Q-Q plot

    | Shape of the points | Meaning |
    | --- | --- |
    | On the straight line | distribution matches the reference |
    | Curving up at the right end | right skew / heavy right tail |
    | S-shape | heavier (or lighter) tails than normal on both sides |
    | Steps | discrete or rounded values |

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Histogram for shape, box/violin for many groups, ECDF to compare distributions honestly, Q-Q to check a distributional assumption.
        Log scales for skewed amounts — and say what the log hides (zeros).

    **Misleading-chart checklist** (apply to every deck you approve):

    - Truncated y-axis on a bar chart (exaggerates differences).
    - Dual axes with arbitrary scaling (manufactures correlation).
    - Bins or smoothing chosen to hide or create a bump.
    - Averages without spread; two overlapping histograms with different bin widths.
    - Log axis unlabeled; zeros silently dropped.
    - Percentages without the base n.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask for two charts for any amount: a log-scale histogram **and** an ECDF. Then ask the junior to read three numbers off the ECDF
        (median, P90, share below a business threshold). It trains precise reading.

## 3. When to use, when not

| Goal | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare 2–3 groups' distributions | ECDF, violin | stacked histograms |
| Compare 10+ groups | box plots sorted by median | 10 densities on one axis |
| Check normality before a test | Q-Q plot | relying only on a normality test p-value |
| Show a skewed amount | log histogram + ECDF | raw histogram with one giant bar |

## 9. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R1-11 · Applied · How many bins?"
    **Business framing:** Plot the basket-value histogram.

    **Deliverable:** Bin counts suggested by Sturges and Freedman-Diaconis; skewness on the log scale.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Sturges 13 bins vs Freedman-Diaconis 308; log-scale skew -0.5 (raw 10.54). *(tolerance: ± 1 bin; ± 0.05)*

        **Model answer:** On heavy-tailed data both rules misbehave on the raw scale — one hides the shape, the other creates hundreds of near-empty bins because of the long tail. Plot on a log scale (or clip the axis and say so) and add an ECDF.

        **Common wrong answer:** Accepting the default histogram where everything sits in the first bar.

    **Leader drill:** What two charts would you require for any spend distribution?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F1-10 · Basic · ECDFs compare vintages at a glance"
    **Business framing:** Have recent applicants got weaker bureau scores?

    **Deliverable:** Median, P25 and share below 650 for early vs late (2025Q2–Q3) vintages; ECDF plot.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Median 699.0 → 675.0; P25 660.0 → 635.0; below 650: 19.9% → 33.5%. *(tolerance: ± 1)*

        **Model answer:** The late ECDF sits to the left everywhere — the whole distribution shifted, not just the tail. This previews population drift (F11, Day 9).

        **Common wrong answer:** Comparing two overlapping histograms with different bin widths.

    **Leader drill:** Which single number from the ECDF would you monitor monthly?

??? example "F1-15 · Stretch · A spike at zero"
    **Business framing:** Plot the distribution of current balances.

    **Deliverable:** Share of balances at exactly zero; linear vs log-scale histogram; what the spike means.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 9.9% at zero. *(tolerance: ± 0.2 pp)*

        **Model answer:** A log axis drops zeros silently, so report them separately. The spike mixes closed, paid-off and charged-off accounts — three different stories that should not be averaged together.

        **Common wrong answer:** Plotting log(balance) and losing a tenth of accounts without comment.

    **Leader drill:** How should zero balances appear in the monthly portfolio pack?

**Objectives** — you can: choose the right summary of centre and spread for the shape of the data; compute and interpret mean,
median, mode, trimmed and geometric means, SD, IQR, MAD, percentiles, coefficient of variation, skewness and kurtosis; and measure
concentration (top-share, Lorenz curve, Gini).

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "What is a **typical** basket worth, and how much do baskets vary?"

=== "Finance"

    > "How are balances distributed across the book — and how concentrated is our exposure?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Centre

    | Measure | Definition | Basket value (clean sales) | Use when |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Mean** | sum ÷ n | 50.52 | you need totals (mean × baskets = revenue); data roughly symmetric |
    | **Median** | middle value | 38.94 | "typical" for skewed data |
    | **Trimmed mean (10%)** | mean after removing top and bottom 10% | 41.79 | compromise: robust but uses more data |
    | **Geometric mean** | exp(mean of logs) | 34.93 | multiplicative data (growth rates, log-normal amounts) |
    | **Mode** | most frequent value | — | categories; rarely useful for continuous amounts |

    The mean is above **64.4%** of baskets: a few very large baskets pull it up.

    ### 2.2 Spread

    | Measure | Definition | Basket value |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Standard deviation** | typical distance from the mean (√ of average squared deviation, n − 1) | 67.58 |
    | **IQR** | Q3 − Q1 (middle 50%) | 39.42 (21.98 to 61.41) |
    | **MAD** | median of absolute deviations from the median | 19.45 |
    | **Percentiles** | P90, P99 | 90.99, 226.85 (max 1651.39) |
    | **Coefficient of variation** | SD ÷ mean | 1.34 |

    Here SD > mean: "mean ± 2 SD" would give a *negative* lower bound — a sure sign the data are skewed and SD is the wrong language.

    ### 2.3 Shape

    - **Skewness** — asymmetry. Basket value: **10.54** (long right tail). Rule of thumb: |skew| > 1 is strongly skewed.
    - **Kurtosis** (excess) — heaviness of the tails relative to a normal. Basket value: **174.4** — extreme values far more
      common than a normal distribution would predict. Heavy tails make means and SDs unstable.

    <div class="sb-chart" data-chart="basket_skew" data-caption="Toggle linear/log axis: on the log axis the long right tail becomes a readable hump."></div>

    ### 2.4 Concentration

    The top 25 customers produce **14.7%** of panel revenue; the top 10% produce
    **37.2%**. The **Lorenz curve** plots cumulative share of revenue against cumulative share of customers; the
    **Gini coefficient** (0 = everyone equal, 1 = one customer has everything) summarises it: **0.507**.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Skewed amounts (spend, income, balances, claims) → median + IQR/percentiles for "typical", mean only for totals. SD and plain
        z-scores mislead on heavy tails. Concentration (top-share, Gini) is often the real management question.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Which number goes on the slide?** For "typical customer": median with IQR. For revenue planning: mean (it multiplies to the total).
      Put both in the appendix, and never mean ± SD for a skewed amount.
    - **Balances** have mean 8,854 and median 6,600, skewness 13.53; the top 1% hold
      **14.2%**, and 9.9% are exactly zero. Exposure management is a concentration question.
    - **Comparing variability across groups** needs a relative measure (CV) or robust spread (IQR/median), not raw SDs.
    - **Heavy tails change methods downstream**: tests on means need more data (Day 3), models need log targets or robust losses (Day 5).

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask the junior to state the median *and* what share of customers sit below the mean. When they realise two-thirds are below
        "average", they rarely use the mean as "typical" again.

## 3. When to use which

| Data shape | Centre | Spread | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Symmetric, light tails | mean | SD | histogram |
| Skewed or heavy-tailed | median (+ mean for totals) | IQR, percentiles, MAD | log-scale histogram, ECDF |
| Comparing scales | — | CV, IQR/median | side-by-side box plots |
| Concentration question | top-share | Gini | Lorenz curve |

## 5. Output anatomy

| Number | Meaning | Typical range | Suspicious when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mean / median = 1.3 | skew indicator | ≈ 1 symmetric; 1.2–1.5 common for spend | > 2: a few extreme units dominate — check errors |
| CV 1.34 | relative variability | spend often 0.8–1.5 | > 3: mixed populations or errors |
| skew 10.54, kurtosis 174.4 | tail heaviness | skew 1–5 for spend | very high: inspect the top 1% |
| Gini 0.507 | concentration | customer revenue often 0.4–0.7 (rule of thumb) | sudden change: segment shift or data issue |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Pattern | Meaning | Next step |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Mean ≫ median | Right skew | Report median; inspect the tail |
    | SD > mean for a positive amount | Heavy tail | Use IQR/percentiles; log scale |
    | Median moves, mean stable | Shift in typical customer | Investigate the bulk, not the tail |
    | Mean moves, median stable | Change in the tail | Look at a few large units |

=== "Leader"

    | Pattern | Likely causes | Action — why / when / when not | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | KPI "average basket" jumps | A few bulk orders, data errors | **Ask for the median and P99**; do not celebrate until the median moves | Report median for "typical" |
    | High concentration (top 1% hold > 10–15%) | Large accounts, bulk buyers | **Manage the tail separately** (limits, key-account CRM) | Concentration limits in risk appetite |
    | Different CV across segments | Different mixes | Compare relative spread; do not compare SDs | — |

## 9. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R1-9 · Basic · Describe basket value"
    **Business framing:** The commercial director wants one number for the 'typical basket'.

    **Deliverable:** Mean, median, SD, IQR, MAD, P90, P99, skewness, CV of basket revenue (clean sales).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Mean 50.52, median 38.94, SD 67.58, IQR 39.42, MAD 19.45, P90 90.99, P99 226.85, skew 10.54, CV 1.34. *(tolerance: ± 0.1)*

        **Model answer:** The mean sits above 64.4% of baskets, so it is not 'typical'. Quote the median with the IQR; use the mean only when you need totals (mean × number of baskets).

        **Common wrong answer:** Quoting mean ± SD (a negative lower bound is a giveaway).

    **Leader drill:** Which number goes on the slide, and which in the footnote?

??? example "R1-10 · Applied · Who drives revenue?"
    **Business framing:** How concentrated is spend across customers?

    **Deliverable:** Revenue share of top 25 customers and top 10%; Gini of customer revenue.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Top 25: 14.7%; top 10%: 37.2%; Gini 0.507. *(tolerance: ± 0.3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** A small group carries a large share; any average is sensitive to them. These are the bulk buyers (R07) — a separate segment, not 'outliers to delete'.

        **Common wrong answer:** Removing the top customers to 'clean' the data.

    **Leader drill:** Decide how CRM should treat the top 25.

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F1-8 · Basic · Describe balances"
    **Business framing:** Portfolio reporting.

    **Deliverable:** Mean, median, skewness, share held by top 1%, Gini, share at zero.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Mean 8,854, median 6,600, skew 13.53, top 1% hold 14.2%, Gini 0.512, 9.9% at zero. *(tolerance: ± 0.2)*

        **Model answer:** Heavy tail plus a spike at zero (closed, paid-off or charged-off accounts). Describe the two parts separately — 'share at zero' and the distribution of positive balances.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting the mean balance as a typical customer's.

    **Leader drill:** Which concentration figure belongs in a risk-appetite statement?

??? example "F1-9 · Applied · Comparing spread across groups"
    **Business framing:** Is income more variable among self-employed customers?

    **Deliverable:** Median income and coefficient of variation by employment type.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Medians — contract 41,500 · retired 28,700 · salaried 52,500 · self-employed 57,600 · student 13,600. CVs — contract 1.26 · retired 1.46 · salaried 1.48 · self-employed 1.58 · student 1.31. *(tolerance: ± 0.02)*

        **Model answer:** CV compares spread relative to the mean, so groups with very different incomes can be compared. All groups are highly variable because of the heavy tail; robust spread (IQR/median) is a useful companion.

        **Common wrong answer:** Comparing raw SDs across groups with different scales.

    **Leader drill:** Would you set different verification rules by employment type based on this?

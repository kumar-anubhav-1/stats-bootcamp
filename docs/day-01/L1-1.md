## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    Two simple questions — and both come out wrong if you compute them at the wrong grain.

=== "Finance"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Data types and measurement scales

    | Type | Scale | Examples in the course data | What you may compute |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Categorical | **Nominal** (no order) | `region`, `channel`, `brand` | counts, %, mode |
    | Categorical | **Ordinal** (ordered) | `age_band`, `loyalty_segment`, `delinquency_bucket` | + median, percentiles |
    | Numeric | **Interval** (no true zero) | `temperature_c` | + differences, mean |
    | Numeric | **Ratio** (true zero) | `quantity`, `revenue`, `income_annual` | + ratios ("twice as much") |
    | Numeric discrete | counts | `inquiries_6m`, `quantity` | count models (Day 5) |
    | Binary | yes/no | `default_12m`, `promo_flag` | proportions |

    **Traps:** codes that look numeric (`city_tier` 1–3, `store_id`) are categories — never average them. Flags stored as text
    ("Y"/"N") must be converted before you count them.

    ### 2.2 Grain — what one row represents

    | Table | Grain | Key |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `transactions` | one line on a till receipt | `line_id` (should be unique) |
    | `customers` | one shopper | `customer_id` |
    | `weekly_store_category` | one store × category × week | (`store_id`, `category`, `week`) |
    | `accounts` | one booked account | `account_id` |
    | `performance_monthly` | one account × month on book | (`account_id`, `month_on_book`) |

    To answer a basket question, first aggregate lines → baskets. Here a basket has **7.56 lines** on
    average, so the mean *line* revenue (7.35) is far below the mean *basket* revenue (50.52).

    ### 2.3 Tidy data

    One row per observation, one column per variable, one table per type of observational unit. Untidy signs: months as columns
    (`Jan_sales`, `Feb_sales`), two facts in one cell ("Hyper-North"), the same customer attribute repeated on every line (fine for
    analysis, dangerous for counting customers).

    ### 2.4 Joins and the join explosion

    Joining two tables multiplies rows whenever the key is not unique on one side. Joining transactions to the weekly table on
    store and category — **forgetting week** — turns 25,000 rows into **2,600,000**
    (104.0×). The code runs without error; every sum afterwards is wrong.

    **Rule:** state the grain of both tables, the join key, the expected row count, and check it after the join.

    ### 2.5 The EDA workflow

    1. **Question** — what decision, what metric, at what grain?
    2. **Profile** — types, nulls, uniques, ranges (`rcp_profile`).
    3. **Validate** — rules, duplicates, reconciliation (L1.2).
    4. **Visualise** — distributions, then relationships (L1.5, L1.7).
    5. **Hypothesise** — write down what you expect *before* testing (Day 3).

    ```mermaid
    flowchart LR
      Q[Business question] --> G[Grain and keys] --> P[Profile: counts, nulls, ranges] --> QC[Quality rules] --> V[Visualise] --> H[Hypotheses] --> T[Test or model] --> D[Decision]
      QC -.->|issue found| P
    ```


=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Types and scales decide which summaries are legal. Grain is the single most common source of wrong numbers. Joins on
        non-unique keys silently multiply rows. EDA = question → profile → validate → visualise → hypothesise.

    **Where judgement is needed**

    - **Metric definitions are grain decisions.** "Average balance" at account-snapshot grain is 8,854;
      averaged over account-months it is 10,254. Neither team is wrong — the definition was missing.
      Publish the grain with every KPI.
    - **Joins are the highest-risk step in any pipeline.** Ask for row counts before and after every join in every review.
    - **EDA before hypotheses, hypotheses before tests.** Juniors who "explore until something is significant" are p-hacking (L3.7).

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Read me the first row of the table in business words."* ("Customer C00170 bought 3 × 750 g FarmFresh fruit at S013
        on 6 Nov 2025 in basket B001548.") If they cannot, they do not know the grain yet.

## 3. When to use, when not

| Step | Use | Do not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grain check | Before every aggregation and join | Assume from the file name |
| Tidy reshaping | Before plotting or modelling | Reshape the source file in place |
| Joins | With a declared key and expected row count | Join on "whatever matches" |

## 5. Output anatomy — a grain check

| Number | Meaning | Good / suspicious |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rows 25,000, unique `line_id` 24850 | duplicates exist | any gap means duplicates or wrong grain |
| 7.56 lines per basket | aggregation factor | grocery baskets of a few lines to dozens are plausible |
| 2.46 baskets per customer | panel depth | low: customer-level statistics will be noisy |
| join result 2,600,000 rows | explosion | any join that increases the left table's rows needs a reason |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Pattern | Meaning | Next step |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Rows increase after a join | Key not unique on the right | Add the missing key column or aggregate the right table first |
    | Key not unique | Duplicates or finer grain than expected | Investigate before de-duplicating |
    | Two teams' KPIs differ | Different grain or window | Write a shared definition |

=== "Leader"

    | Pattern | Likely cause | Action — when / when not | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | KPI jumps after a pipeline change | New join, changed grain | **Hold the KPI** until row counts are reconciled; not when the jump is explained by business events | Reconcile to an independent total every release |
    | Two departments disagree | Undefined metric | **Publish a definition** (grain, filters, window); do not pick a winner | One owner per KPI |
    | Junior's deck has no row counts | Skipped validation | **Send back**; accept only with row accounting | Row accounting in every analysis |

## 7. Alternatives

!!! tip "What else could I try?"
    - **[Data-quality rules (validity, duplicates, reconciliation)](../../navigator/#N-DESC-QUALITY)** — Profiling flagged duplicates, impossible values or unit errors.
    - **[Counts, percentages, mode; bar chart](../../navigator/#N-DESC-CAT)** — One categorical variable (region, product type).
    - **[Median, IQR, percentiles (P90/P99), MAD; log-scale histogram, ECDF; Lorenz/Pareto](../../navigator/#N-DESC-SKEW)** — Spend, income, balances, claim amounts — skewed or heavy-tailed.

## 8. Planted trap and reuse drill

!!! warning "Trap"
    `transactions` repeats customer attributes on every line. Counting `age_band` values on lines counts *lines*, not customers —
    frequent shoppers are over-weighted. Count customers from `customers`, or de-duplicate by `customer_id` first.

**Reuse drill:** profile `data/retail/variants/transactions_weekly.parquet` (store × SKU × week grain) with `rcp_profile`. What

## 9. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R1-1 · Basic · What does one row represent?"
    **Business framing:** The category team wants "average basket value" and "baskets per customer".

    **Deliverable:** Grain of `transactions`; lines per basket; baskets per customer (sales lines, de-duplicated).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** One row = one transaction line. 7.56 lines per basket; 2.46 baskets per customer. *(tolerance: ± 0.05)*

        **Model answer:** Basket and customer metrics need aggregation from line grain first. Averaging line revenue (7.35) instead of basket revenue (50.52) understates basket value several-fold.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reporting the mean of `revenue` as average basket value.

    **Leader drill:** A dashboard tile shows 'average basket' ≈ 7.35. What question exposes the error in one sentence?

??? example "R1-2 · Applied · The join explosion"
    **Business framing:** An analyst adds weekly store-category sales to each transaction line, joining on store and category only.

    **Deliverable:** Row count after the join on (store_id, category) vs (store_id, category, week).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 2,600,000 rows vs 25,000 — a 104.0× explosion. *(tolerance: exact)*

        **Model answer:** The weekly table has one row per store-category-week, so a key without `week` matches every week (104 rows per line). Any sum after this join is wildly inflated. Always state the grain of both tables and check row counts before and after a join.

        **Common wrong answer:** Not noticing because the code ran without error.

    **Leader drill:** Write the one-line review rule that would have caught this.

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F1-1 · Basic · The grain of the performance panel"
    **Business framing:** Risk wants monthly delinquency figures.

    **Deliverable:** Is (account_id, month_on_book) unique? Months observed per account on average.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Unique = True; 18.2 months per account. *(tolerance: ± 0.1)*

        **Model answer:** One row per account per month. Counting rows counts account-months, not accounts.

        **Common wrong answer:** Counting rows in the panel as 'number of accounts'.

    **Leader drill:** Which KPIs on the risk dashboard are at account grain and which at account-month?

??? example "F1-2 · Applied · Two 'average balances'"
    **Business framing:** Two teams report different average balances.

    **Deliverable:** Mean balance from `accounts` (snapshot) vs `performance_monthly` (all months).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Accounts: 8,854; panel: 10,254. *(tolerance: ± 50)*

        **Model answer:** Different grain and time window — snapshot per account vs an average over account-months (which weights long-lived accounts more and includes balances before pay-down or charge-off). Both are 'right'; the question decides which.

        **Common wrong answer:** Declaring one team wrong.

    **Leader drill:** Write the definition you would publish for 'average balance'.

## 11. Recap

- Name the type and scale before choosing a summary.
- State and test the grain; aggregate before answering questions at a coarser grain.
- Check row counts around every join.

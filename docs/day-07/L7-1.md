**Objectives** — you can: decompose a series into trend, seasonality and remainder (STL); tell additive from multiplicative patterns;
test stationarity (ADF, KPSS) and difference a series; read ACF/PACF plots; and connect seasonal patterns to business calendars.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "What are the weekly, seasonal and holiday patterns in chain sales?"

=== "Finance"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The components

    **Series = trend + seasonality + remainder** (additive) or **× each other** (multiplicative — seasonal swings grow with the level,
    as in retail; log the series to make it additive). STL (Seasonal-Trend decomposition using Loess) estimates the parts robustly.

    Chain weekly units (104 weeks, mean 43,320):

    | Pattern | Measured | Planted (R11) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Seasonal strength (STL) | 1.0 (0–1) | annual cycle |
    | Trend strength | 0.89 | — |
    | Fri–Sat vs Mon–Thu baskets | ×1.347 | +30% |
    | Q4 (non-holiday) vs other weeks | ×1.228 | +25% |
    | Holiday weeks vs others | ×1.442 | +40% |

    The peak week (week 8, 73,212 units) is a holiday week. With only two years of data, the
    seasonal component is estimated from two cycles — enough to see it, not enough to model every holiday precisely.

    ### 2.2 Stationarity

    A **stationary** series has constant mean and variance over time — the assumption behind ARIMA models.

    | Series | ADF p (H0: non-stationary) | KPSS p (H0: stationary) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Chain weekly units | 0.006 | 0.1 |
    | Monthly delinquency rate | 0.925 | — |

    Chain units fluctuate around a stable level with seasonality (both tests agree it is stationary around its seasonal pattern). The
    delinquency rate climbs from 0.58% to 5.4% over 42 months — **not stationary**.
    Differencing (month-to-month changes) is the ARIMA remedy, but the real story is a **regime shift**: a single change-point splits
    it at 2025-12 (1.63% → 4.4%), the arrival of the partner vintages seasoning on
    book (L5.7, L7.5).

    ### 2.3 ACF and PACF

    The **autocorrelation function** correlates the series with its own past: lag-1 ACF 0.34 (this week resembles last
    week) and **lag-52 ACF 0.48** (this week resembles the same week last year — annual seasonality). The PACF shows the
    direct effect of each lag after shorter lags are accounted for; it guides AR orders in ARIMA (a sharp PACF cut-off after lag p →
    AR(p)).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Decompose before modelling: trend, seasonality, holidays, remainder. Non-stationarity often signals a change in the business
        (mix, policy) rather than a statistical nuisance.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Year-on-year comparisons must align calendars** (holiday weeks, Fri–Sat counts, Q4) — otherwise "growth" is just the calendar.
    - **Rising delinquency is a population story** until proven otherwise: check vintage and channel mix before blaming the economy.
    - **Two years is a short history** for annual seasonality — expect uncertainty around holiday effects.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Show me the decomposition and the calendar effects before any forecast."*

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R7-1 · Basic · Decompose chain sales"
    **Business framing:** Planning wants the seasonal pattern of chain units.

    **Deliverable:** STL (period 52) of weekly chain units; seasonal and trend strength; ACF at lags 1 and 52.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Seasonal strength 1.0, trend strength 0.89; ACF1 0.34, ACF52 0.48. *(tolerance: ± 0.05)*

        **Model answer:** Annual seasonality dominates; same-week-last-year is highly informative.

        **Common wrong answer:** Fitting a trend line through seasonal data.

    **Leader drill:** How should year-on-year reporting handle holidays?

??? example "R7-2 · Applied · Calendar effects"
    **Business framing:** Trading asks how much weekends, Q4 and holidays matter.

    **Deliverable:** Fri–Sat vs Mon–Thu basket ratio; Q4 non-holiday vs other weeks; holiday vs non-holiday weeks.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** ×1.347, ×1.228, ×1.442 (planted +30%, +25%, +40%). *(tolerance: ± 0.03)*

        **Model answer:** Calendar effects are large; comparisons must align them.

        **Common wrong answer:** Comparing a holiday week with a normal week.

    **Leader drill:** Which KPI comparisons in your packs ignore the calendar?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F7-1 · Basic · Delinquency series"
    **Business framing:** Is delinquency stable?

    **Deliverable:** Monthly 30+ DPD rate; first and last value; ADF p-value.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 0.58% → 5.4% over 42 months; ADF p 0.925. *(tolerance: ± 0.1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Non-stationary — rising with portfolio seasoning and the partner vintages.

        **Common wrong answer:** Fitting a stationary model.

    **Leader drill:** What explains the trend?

??? example "F7-2 · Applied · Seasoning vs calendar"
    **Business framing:** Separate vintage effects from calendar effects.

    **Deliverable:** Describe a vintage × months-on-book view and why calendar-time series mix them.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Calendar series mixes accounts at different ages and from different channels; vintage curves compare like with like (Day 8). *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Delinquency depends on age; a growing young book lowers early rates, seasoning raises them later.

        **Common wrong answer:** Reading calendar trends as economic.

    **Leader drill:** Which view goes to the risk committee?

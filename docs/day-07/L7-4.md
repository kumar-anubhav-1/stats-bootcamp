**Objectives** — you can: classify items with ADI and CV² (smooth, erratic, intermittent, lumpy); forecast intermittent demand with
Croston and SBA; explain why MAE-based metrics mislead for sparse items; and reconcile forecasts across a hierarchy (bottom-up,

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "A quarter of our SKUs sell in fewer than three weeks out of four. How do we forecast them — and should we forecast by region or for the chain?"

=== "Finance"

    > "Branch-level forecasts don't add up to the regional plan. Which is right?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Classify demand (Syntetos–Boylan)

    **ADI** = average interval between non-zero periods; **CV²** = squared coefficient of variation of non-zero demand sizes.

    | Class | Rule | Share of 420 SKUs |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | smooth | ADI ≤ 1.32, CV² ≤ 0.49 | 75.0% |
    | erratic | ADI ≤ 1.32, CV² > 0.49 | 0.0% |
    | intermittent | ADI > 1.32, CV² ≤ 0.49 | 25.0% |
    | lumpy | ADI > 1.32, CV² > 0.49 | 0.0% |
    

    R22 planted the long tail: **25.0%** of SKUs have ADI > 1.32.

    ### 2.2 Croston and SBA

    Croston forecasts **size** and **interval** separately and divides them; it is biased upward, which the Syntetos–Boylan
    approximation (SBA) corrects by × (1 − α/2). Over the 105 intermittent SKUs (weeks 79–104):

    | Method | Mean MASE | Total bias |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Simple exponential smoothing (α = 0.1) | 0.918 | 2.4% |
    | Croston | 0.947 | 12.1% |
    | SBA | 0.933 | 6.5% |
    | **Forecast zero** | **0.678** | −100% |

    **Forecasting zero wins on MASE** — MAE rewards the median, and the median of sparse demand is zero. A zero forecast would empty
    the shelves. For intermittent items judge by **bias and service level** (does stock cover demand?), not MAE-based scores. Here SBA
    removes much of Croston's upward bias; with a low smoothing constant, SES is competitive in this data.

    ### 2.3 Hierarchies: regions → chain (Beverages, weeks 79–104)

    | Approach | Chain WAPE | Average region WAPE |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Bottom-up (forecast each region, add) | 6.5% | 12.0% |
    | Top-down (forecast chain, split by historic shares) | 6.5% | **8.6%** |

    Both give the same chain accuracy; top-down is **better at region level** here because regional series are noisy and regional
    shares are stable. With different regional patterns (e.g. a store opening), bottom-up or **optimal reconciliation (MinT)** — which
    combines forecasts at all levels so they add up — does better. Whatever the method, forecasts used by different teams must be
    **coherent** (add up).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Classify items before forecasting; use Croston/SBA or SES for sparse items and judge them by bias and service. Choose a
        hierarchy method by testing; insist forecasts are coherent across levels.

    **Judgement points**

    - **The long tail is a range decision as much as a forecasting one** — CZ/lumpy items may be better delisted or ordered to demand.
    - **A "most accurate" model that forecasts zero for slow items is a stock-out generator.**
    - **One number for planning:** finance, supply and stores must work from reconciled forecasts.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"What share of SKUs is intermittent, how are they forecast, and how is accuracy measured for them?"*

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R7-9 · Basic · Classify demand"
    **Business framing:** How much of the range is intermittent?

    **Deliverable:** ADI/CV² classes for all SKUs.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** smooth 75% · erratic 0% · intermittent 25% · lumpy 0%; ADI > 1.32 for 25.0%. *(tolerance: ± 1 pp)*

        **Model answer:** A quarter of SKUs need intermittent methods.

        **Common wrong answer:** Forecasting every SKU with ETS.

    **Leader drill:** Which of these SKUs should be delisted?

??? example "R7-10 · Applied · The zero-forecast trap"
    **Business framing:** A vendor reports excellent MASE on slow movers.

    **Deliverable:** Mean MASE and total bias of SES, Croston, SBA and zero on intermittent SKUs.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** MASE ses 0.918 · croston 0.947 · sba 0.933 · zero 0.678; bias ses 2.4% · croston 12.1% · sba 6.5%. *(tolerance: ± 0.02)*

        **Model answer:** Zero 'wins' MASE because MAE rewards the median; judge by bias and service level.

        **Common wrong answer:** Buying the vendor's zero forecasts.

    **Leader drill:** Which acceptance test would you set for the vendor?

??? example "R7-11 · Stretch · Hierarchy"
    **Business framing:** Regional planners and the chain plan disagree.

    **Deliverable:** Bottom-up vs top-down WAPE at chain and region level.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Chain 6.5% vs 6.5%; regions 12.0% vs 8.6%. *(tolerance: ± 0.3 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Top-down wins at region level here (noisy regions, stable shares); test before choosing; keep forecasts coherent.

        **Common wrong answer:** Each team forecasting independently.

    **Leader drill:** Who owns the reconciled number?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F7-9 · Basic · Intermittent events"

    **Deliverable:** Classify and suggest a method.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"

        **Model answer:** Pool information across sparse units.

        **Common wrong answer:** ETS per branch.

    **Leader drill:** How would you set branch fraud budgets?

??? example "F7-10 · Applied · Reconciling plans"

    **Deliverable:** Propose a reconciliation approach.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"

        **Model answer:** Coherent forecasts across levels.

        **Common wrong answer:** Letting each level plan independently.

    **Leader drill:** Who arbitrates?

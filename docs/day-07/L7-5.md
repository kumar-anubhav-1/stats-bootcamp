# L7.5 · Change-points and censored demand

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Finding the break (R08)

    S017's revenue as a share of the other 39 stores (removing chain seasonality): binary segmentation finds a break at **week
    61**: share 1.16% → 0.74% (level ratio 0.635, i.e.
    −36%). On the raw revenue series the drop is harder to see, because holiday and seasonal swings are as large as the shift.

    **CUSUM monitor** (baseline weeks 1–40, k = 0.5, h = 8.0): first alarm in **week 61**. With a
    tighter threshold (h = 5) the monitor alarms in week 60 — before the break: a false alarm. The threshold
    trades speed against false alarms.

    **Finance.** The monthly 30+ DPD rate breaks at 2025-12 (1.63% → 4.4%). Models
    fitted across a break average two regimes and fit neither — forecast from the new regime, or model the cause (vintage mix).

    ### 2.2 Censored demand (R12)

    When a SKU is out of stock, sales = stock, not demand. 3.0% of SKU-store-weeks had stock-outs.

    | Total demand estimate (all SKU-weeks) | vs true demand |
    | --- | --- |
    | Observed sales (naive) | **-2.9%** |
    | Uncensored: scale sales by 40 ÷ stores in stock | 1.3% |

    Uncensoring raises the demand estimate by 4.2%. The simple fix over-corrects slightly: it assumes
    out-of-stock stores sold nothing that week, but they sold until the shelf emptied. Better methods estimate lost sales within the
    week or use in-stock store-days. Forecasts and safety stock trained on censored history are **biased low and
    self-reinforcing** — less stock → more stock-outs → lower "demand".

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Breaks change the baseline; find them, explain them, and don't let models average across them. Stock-outs hide demand; fix
        the history before planning from it.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Every break needs a business explanation** (refit, competitor, data feed change) before it's modelled.
    - **Monitoring thresholds are a policy choice** — how many false alarms per year will operations tolerate?
    - **Lost sales are invisible in sales data.** Ask supply chain for in-stock measures before accepting forecast "accuracy".

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"What's the change-point date, what caused it, and how was demand corrected for stock-outs?"*

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R7-12 · Basic · Store S017 break"
    **Business framing:** Operations asks when S017's decline started.

    **Deliverable:** Run `retail_changepoint_s017.yaml`: break week, level ratio, CUSUM alarm (h = 8 and h = 5).

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Week 61, ratio 0.635; CUSUM alarm week 61 (h = 8), week 60 (h = 5, false alarm). *(tolerance: ± 1 week)*

        **Model answer:** Share-of-total isolates the store-specific shift; the threshold trades speed for false alarms.

        **Common wrong answer:** Looking for the break in raw revenue.

    **Leader drill:** What would you ask the store manager?

??? example "R7-13 · Applied · Uncensor demand"
    **Business framing:** Are stock-outs depressing forecasts?

    **Deliverable:** Total observed vs uncensored demand vs truth.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Observed -2.9% vs truth; uncensored 1.3%; uplift 4.2%. *(tolerance: ± 0.5 pp)*

        **Model answer:** Sales understate demand by about 3%; simple scaling slightly over-corrects.

        **Common wrong answer:** Training forecasts on raw sales.

    **Leader drill:** What in-stock data should supply chain provide?

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"

        **Common wrong answer:** Alerting on every monthly rise.

    **Leader drill:** How many false alarms per year will you accept?

??? example "F7-13 · Stretch · Censoring in finance"

    **Deliverable:** Explain how capped utilisation censors 'demand for credit'.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"

        **Model answer:** Same logic as stock-outs.

        **Common wrong answer:** Treating balances at the limit as true demand.

    **Leader drill:** How would you test limit increases?

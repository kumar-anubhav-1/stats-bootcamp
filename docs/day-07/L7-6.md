**Objectives** — you can: classify items by value (ABC) and variability (XYZ); compute safety stock and reorder points for a target
cycle service level; explain the steep cost of high service levels; use the real demand variance rather than a Poisson assumption;
size a single-period order with the newsvendor model; and translate the same logic to capacity buffers in finance.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "How much safety stock do our top SKUs need, and what does moving from 95% to 99% service cost?"

=== "Finance"

    > "How many collections agents do we need to handle monthly 30+ DPD entries 95% of the time?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 ABC × XYZ

    ABC by revenue (A = first 80% of value, B = next 15%, C = rest): A 166 · B 83 · C 171 SKUs — A items are
    39.5% of the range. XYZ by coefficient of variation of weekly demand (X ≤ 0.5, Y ≤ 1, Z > 1). There are
    **166 AX items** (valuable, steady → automate tightly) and **132 CZ items** (low value, erratic → question ranging,
    order to demand). The CZ group is essentially the intermittent long tail (L7.4).

    ### 2.2 Safety stock and reorder point

    $$\text{SS} = z_{\text{service}} \times \sigma_{\text{weekly}} \times \sqrt{L}, \qquad \text{ROP} = \mu \times L + \text{SS}$$

    Top SKU SKU0002 (GreenLeaf Vegetables 750g): chain weekly demand μ = 406.3, σ = 92.4, lead time
    L = 2 weeks.

    | Cycle service level | 90% | 95% | 98% | 99% | 99.5% |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Safety stock (units) | 168 | 215 | 269 | 304 | 337 |

    Reorder point at 95%: **1,028** units. Moving from 95% to 99% adds **41%**
    more safety stock — the last points of service are the most expensive. (Cycle service level = probability of no stock-out per
    cycle; **fill rate** = share of demand served from stock, usually higher for the same stock.)

    **Variance matters.** A Poisson assumption (σ² = μ) gives a 95% safety stock of only **47** units vs
    215 with the real variance — demand is over-dispersed (L2.2), so Poisson-based policies under-stock.

    ### 2.3 Newsvendor (single period)

    Fresh bakery item, price 3.00, cost 1.20, end-of-day salvage 0.30. Order the demand quantile at the **critical ratio**
    (p − c) ÷ (p − s) = **0.667**. Store S001 weekly Bakery demand (μ 51.2, σ 14.8) as an
    illustration: empirical quantile **52**, normal approximation 58 — the normal ignores the actual
    shape of demand.

    ### 2.4 Finance: the same logic for capacity

    New 30+ DPD entries per month (last 12 months): mean 129, SD 21. Capacity to handle a month at
    the 95th percentile ≈ mean + 1.645 SD = **164** cases (normal approximation). Divide by cases per agent per
    month to size the team — and revisit as the partner vintages season.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Inventory is a service-vs-cost trade-off set by policy: segment items (ABC/XYZ), choose service levels by segment, use real
        variability, and correct censored history first.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Differentiate service by segment**: 99% for AX staples, 90% (or delist) for CZ — a single blanket target wastes stock.
    - **The cost of the last few points of service** should be shown in money, not units, before the target is set.
    - **Buffers are everywhere**: agent capacity, branch cash, liquidity — the same quantile logic applies.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"What service level, by segment, at what stock cost — and which demand variance did you use?"*

## 8. Exercises

### Retail · NorthStar

??? example "R7-14 · Basic · Safety stock for the top SKU"
    **Business framing:** Set safety stock for SKU SKU0002.

    **Deliverable:** Safety stock at 90–99.5% service, lead time 2 weeks; reorder point at 95%; Poisson comparison.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** SS 90 168 · 95 215 · 98 269 · 99 304 · 99.5 337; ROP 1,028; Poisson SS(95%) 47. *(tolerance: ± 2 units)*

        **Model answer:** 99% needs ~40% more safety stock than 95%; Poisson badly under-states variability.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using σ = √μ.

    **Leader drill:** Is 99% worth it for this SKU?

??? example "R7-15 · Applied · ABC/XYZ policy"
    **Business framing:** Propose differentiated service levels.

    **Deliverable:** Run `retail_inventory_skus.yaml`; propose service levels by segment and estimate saving vs blanket 99%.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** 166 AX items, 132 CZ items; e.g. AX 99%, B 95%, C 90% (CZ order to demand). *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Segment-based targets cut stock substantially with little service loss on revenue.

        **Common wrong answer:** One target for all SKUs.

    **Leader drill:** Which segment would you delist first?

### Finance · Meridian

??? example "F7-14 · Basic · Collections capacity"
    **Business framing:** Size the collections team.

    **Deliverable:** Monthly 30+ DPD entries (last 12 months): mean, SD, 95% capacity.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Mean 129, SD 21; capacity 164 cases. *(tolerance: ± 5)*

        **Model answer:** Same quantile logic as safety stock.

        **Common wrong answer:** Staffing to the mean.

    **Leader drill:** What service level for collections calls?

??? example "F7-15 · Applied · Branch cash buffer"
    **Business framing:** Branch cash must cover withdrawals 99% of days.

    **Deliverable:** Describe the calculation and its data needs.

    ??? success "Expected finding and model answer"
        **Expected:** Buffer = mean + z₀.₉₉ × SD of daily net withdrawals over the replenishment interval (√L); use empirical quantiles if skewed. *(tolerance: —)*

        **Model answer:** Inventory logic applies to cash.

        **Common wrong answer:** Using the average withdrawal.

    **Leader drill:** What is the cost of idle cash vs a stock-out of cash?

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Censoring (F13)

    {{ a.d9_censored_pct }}% of accounts had **not** churned by the end of observation — we only know they lasted *at least* that long.
    Two tempting summaries are wrong:

    - "Churn rate is {{ a.d9_naive_churn_rate }}%" — ignores that accounts were observed for different lengths of time.
    - "Average time to churn is {{ a.d9_naive_mean_churners }} months" — averages churners only, i.e. the people who left early.

    ### 2.2 Kaplan–Meier

    KM multiplies, month by month, the share of at-risk accounts that survive; censored accounts count while observed, then drop out.

    | Month | 6 | 12 | 24 |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Still open (KM) | {{ a.d9_km_s6 }}% | {{ a.d9_km_s12 }}% | {{ a.d9_km_s24 }}% |

    The median is not reached (more than half survive 24 months), so report **restricted mean survival time**: over the first 24
    months, an account stays open **{{ a.d9_rmst24 }} months** on average.

    {{ chart("km", "Kaplan–Meier curves keep every account, including the 72% still open.") }}

    ### 2.3 Comparing groups: log-rank

    | Group | Still open at 24 months |
    | --- | --- |
    | no complaints | {{ a.d9_km_s24_compl_none }}% |
    | ≥ 1 complaint | {{ a.d9_km_s24_compl_any }}% |

    Log-rank χ² = {{ a.d9_logrank_chi2 }}, p < 0.001.

    ### 2.4 Cox proportional hazards

    | Driver | Hazard ratio | 95% CI |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    {% for k, v in a.d9_cox.items() %}| {{ k }} | {{ v.hr }} | {{ v.ci[0] }}–{{ v.ci[1] }} |
    {% endfor %}

    Each complaint multiplies the monthly churn hazard by {{ a.d9_cox.complaints_12m.hr }}; each extra 10 points of digital share
    multiplies it by {{ a.d9_cox.digital_share_10pp.hr }}. **Proportional hazards check:** the complaints HR is {{ a.d9_ph_hr_early }}
    in months 1–12 and {{ a.d9_ph_hr_late }} in months 13–24 — stable, so one HR is a fair summary.

    ### 2.5 Retail: time to repeat purchase

    Among customers seen in the last year, the median time from first to second visit is **{{ a.d9_repeat_median_days }} days**
    (members {{ a.d9_repeat_median_member }}, non-members {{ a.d9_repeat_median_non }}); {{ a.d9_repeat_censored }}% had not yet
    returned (censored). Remember L9.1: members differ for many reasons.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Survival methods answer *when*, using everyone — including customers who haven't left yet.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Retention KPIs:** use KM survival at fixed horizons (6, 12, 24 months) by cohort, not "average lifetime of churners".
    - **Hazard ratios are associations**: complaints may signal a bad experience rather than cause churn — but they're an excellent
      trigger for a service-recovery call.
    - **Act where hazard is high**: complaints and low digital engagement identify accounts to save.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"How were customers who haven't churned yet handled?"*

## 5. Output anatomy

| Item | Read it as |
| --- | --- |
| KM step curve | share still active by time; ticks = censoring |
| median / RMST | typical survival; RMST when the median isn't reached |
| log-rank p | do curves differ overall |
| hazard ratio | multiplicative change in instantaneous risk |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Question | Method |
    | --- | --- |
    | survival curve with censoring | Kaplan–Meier |
    | compare two or more groups | log-rank |
    | several drivers | Cox PH |
    | hazards not proportional | stratified Cox, time-varying effects, or discrete-time logistic |
    | competing exits (churn vs default) | competing-risks models |

=== "Leader"

    | Use | Metric |
    | --- | --- |
    | retention target | KM survival at 12 months by cohort |
    | save programme | hazard drivers → triggers |
    | lifetime value | RMST × margin (L8.2) |

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The chart menu

    | Chart | Shows | Best for | Watch out |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Histogram** | counts in bins | shape of one variable | bin width changes the story |
    | **Density (KDE)** | smoothed histogram | comparing shapes | smooths away spikes; invents values below 0 |
    | **Box plot** | median, IQR, whiskers, points beyond | comparing many groups | hides bimodality |
    | **Violin** | box + density | groups with different shapes | needs enough data |
    | **ECDF** | share of values ≤ x | comparing distributions exactly; reading percentiles | less familiar to audiences |
    | **Q-Q plot** | data quantiles vs a theoretical distribution | checking normality / fit | points on the line = good fit |
    | **Bar** (categories) / **line** (time) / **scatter** (two numerics) / **heatmap** (two categories) | — | — | — |

    ### 2.2 Bins and scales

    For basket value, Sturges' rule suggests **{{ a.d1_sturges_bins }}** bins and Freedman-Diaconis **{{ a.d1_fd_bins }}** — on raw,
    heavy-tailed data neither works: either everything sits in the first bar or hundreds of bins are empty. On a **log scale** the skew
    falls from {{ a.d1_b_skew }} to {{ a.d1_logb_skew }} and the shape becomes readable. Always label log axes clearly.

    Logs cannot show zeros: **{{ a.d1_bal_zero_pct }}%** of bank balances are exactly zero — report them separately when you plot on a log scale.

    ### 2.3 Reading an ECDF

    The ECDF at x is the share of values at or below x. Two groups: the curve **further left** has lower values. For bureau scores, the
    share below 650 is {{ a.d1_share_below_650_early }}% for earlier vintages and **{{ a.d1_share_below_650_late }}%** for 2025Q2–Q3;
    the median falls from {{ a.d1_bureau_med_early }} to {{ a.d1_bureau_med_late }}. The late curve sits left everywhere: the whole
    population shifted.

    ### 2.4 Reading a Q-Q plot

    | Shape of the points | Meaning |
    | --- | --- |
    | On the straight line | distribution matches the reference |
    | Curving up at the right end | right skew / heavy right tail |
    | S-shape | heavier (or lighter) tails than normal on both sides |
    | Steps | discrete or rounded values |

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Histogram for shape, box/violin for many groups, ECDF to compare distributions honestly, Q-Q to check a distributional assumption.
        Log scales for skewed amounts — and say what the log hides (zeros).

    **Misleading-chart checklist** (apply to every deck you approve):

    - Truncated y-axis on a bar chart (exaggerates differences).
    - Dual axes with arbitrary scaling (manufactures correlation).
    - Bins or smoothing chosen to hide or create a bump.
    - Averages without spread; two overlapping histograms with different bin widths.
    - Log axis unlabeled; zeros silently dropped.
    - Percentages without the base n.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask for two charts for any amount: a log-scale histogram **and** an ECDF. Then ask the junior to read three numbers off the ECDF
        (median, P90, share below a business threshold). It trains precise reading.

## 3. When to use, when not

| Goal | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare 2–3 groups' distributions | ECDF, violin | stacked histograms |
| Compare 10+ groups | box plots sorted by median | 10 densities on one axis |
| Check normality before a test | Q-Q plot | relying only on a normality test p-value |
| Show a skewed amount | log histogram + ECDF | raw histogram with one giant bar |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | What you see | Meaning | Next |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | ECDFs parallel, one shifted | location shift | compare medians |
    | ECDFs cross | different shapes | compare percentiles, not just centre |
    | Q-Q curves upward at right | heavy right tail | log scale, robust methods |
    | Histogram with a spike | rounding, a default value, zeros | investigate the spike's source |

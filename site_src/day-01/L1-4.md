# L1.4 · Centre, spread and shape

<p class="lesson-meta">Day 1 · Part A · Basic → Advanced 60 min · Leader 25 min · Prerequisites: L1.1–L1.2 · Recipe: <code>rcp_profile</code>, <code>rcp_outliers</code></p>

**Objectives** — you can: choose the right summary of centre and spread for the shape of the data; compute and interpret mean,
median, mode, trimmed and geometric means, SD, IQR, MAD, percentiles, coefficient of variation, skewness and kurtosis; and measure
concentration (top-share, Lorenz curve, Gini).

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "What is a **typical** basket worth, and how much do baskets vary?"

=== "Finance"

    > "How are balances distributed across the book — and how concentrated is our exposure?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Centre

    | Measure | Definition | Basket value (clean sales) | Use when |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Mean** | sum ÷ n | {{ a.d1_b_mean }} | you need totals (mean × baskets = revenue); data roughly symmetric |
    | **Median** | middle value | {{ a.d1_b_median }} | "typical" for skewed data |
    | **Trimmed mean (10%)** | mean after removing top and bottom 10% | {{ a.d1_b_trim10 }} | compromise: robust but uses more data |
    | **Geometric mean** | exp(mean of logs) | {{ a.d1_b_geo }} | multiplicative data (growth rates, log-normal amounts) |
    | **Mode** | most frequent value | — | categories; rarely useful for continuous amounts |

    The mean is above **{{ a.d1_b_share_below_mean }}%** of baskets: a few very large baskets pull it up.

    ### 2.2 Spread

    | Measure | Definition | Basket value |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Standard deviation** | typical distance from the mean (√ of average squared deviation, n − 1) | {{ a.d1_b_sd }} |
    | **IQR** | Q3 − Q1 (middle 50%) | {{ a.d1_b_iqr }} ({{ a.d1_b_q1 }} to {{ a.d1_b_q3 }}) |
    | **MAD** | median of absolute deviations from the median | {{ a.d1_b_mad }} |
    | **Percentiles** | P90, P99 | {{ a.d1_b_p90 }}, {{ a.d1_b_p99 }} (max {{ a.d1_b_max }}) |
    | **Coefficient of variation** | SD ÷ mean | {{ a.d1_b_cv }} |

    Here SD > mean: "mean ± 2 SD" would give a *negative* lower bound — a sure sign the data are skewed and SD is the wrong language.

    ### 2.3 Shape

    - **Skewness** — asymmetry. Basket value: **{{ a.d1_b_skew }}** (long right tail). Rule of thumb: |skew| > 1 is strongly skewed.
    - **Kurtosis** (excess) — heaviness of the tails relative to a normal. Basket value: **{{ a.d1_b_kurt }}** — extreme values far more
      common than a normal distribution would predict. Heavy tails make means and SDs unstable.

    {{ chart("basket_skew", "Toggle linear/log axis: on the log axis the long right tail becomes a readable hump.") }}

    ### 2.4 Concentration

    The top 25 customers produce **{{ a.d1_top25_share_pct }}%** of panel revenue; the top 10% produce
    **{{ a.d1_top10pct_share_pct }}%**. The **Lorenz curve** plots cumulative share of revenue against cumulative share of customers; the
    **Gini coefficient** (0 = everyone equal, 1 = one customer has everything) summarises it: **{{ a.d1_cust_gini }}**.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Skewed amounts (spend, income, balances, claims) → median + IQR/percentiles for "typical", mean only for totals. SD and plain
        z-scores mislead on heavy tails. Concentration (top-share, Gini) is often the real management question.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Which number goes on the slide?** For "typical customer": median with IQR. For revenue planning: mean (it multiplies to the total).
      Put both in the appendix, and never mean ± SD for a skewed amount.
    - **Balances** have mean {{ a.d1_bal_mean | n }} and median {{ a.d1_bal_median | n }}, skewness {{ a.d1_bal_skew }}; the top 1% hold
      **{{ a.d1_bal_top1_pct }}%**, and {{ a.d1_bal_zero_pct }}% are exactly zero. Exposure management is a concentration question.
    - **Comparing variability across groups** needs a relative measure (CV) or robust spread (IQR/median), not raw SDs.
    - **Heavy tails change methods downstream**: tests on means need more data (Day 3), models need log targets or robust losses (Day 5).

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask the junior to state the median *and* what share of customers sit below the mean. When they realise two-thirds are below
        "average", they rarely use the mean as "typical" again.

## 3. When to use which

| Data shape | Centre | Spread | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Symmetric, light tails | mean | SD | histogram |
| Skewed or heavy-tailed | median (+ mean for totals) | IQR, percentiles, MAD | log-scale histogram, ECDF |
| Comparing scales | — | CV, IQR/median | side-by-side box plots |
| Concentration question | top-share | Gini | Lorenz curve |

## 5. Output anatomy

| Number | Meaning | Typical range | Suspicious when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mean / median = {{ (a.d1_b_mean / a.d1_b_median) | round(2) }} | skew indicator | ≈ 1 symmetric; 1.2–1.5 common for spend | > 2: a few extreme units dominate — check errors |
| CV {{ a.d1_b_cv }} | relative variability | spend often 0.8–1.5 | > 3: mixed populations or errors |
| skew {{ a.d1_b_skew }}, kurtosis {{ a.d1_b_kurt }} | tail heaviness | skew 1–5 for spend | very high: inspect the top 1% |
| Gini {{ a.d1_cust_gini }} | concentration | customer revenue often 0.4–0.7 (rule of thumb) | sudden change: segment shift or data issue |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Pattern | Meaning | Next step |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Mean ≫ median | Right skew | Report median; inspect the tail |
    | SD > mean for a positive amount | Heavy tail | Use IQR/percentiles; log scale |
    | Median moves, mean stable | Shift in typical customer | Investigate the bulk, not the tail |
    | Mean moves, median stable | Change in the tail | Look at a few large units |

=== "Leader"

    | Pattern | Likely causes | Action — why / when / when not | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | KPI "average basket" jumps | A few bulk orders, data errors | **Ask for the median and P99**; do not celebrate until the median moves | Report median for "typical" |
    | High concentration (top 1% hold > 10–15%) | Large accounts, bulk buyers | **Manage the tail separately** (limits, key-account CRM) | Concentration limits in risk appetite |
    | Different CV across segments | Different mixes | Compare relative spread; do not compare SDs | — |

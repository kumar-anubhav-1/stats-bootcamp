## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Three classic learners

    | Learner | Intuition | Good at | Watch |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | SVM | find the boundary with the widest margin; kernels bend it | small/medium data, text, clear margins | scaling, slow on large n, probabilities need calibration |
    | KNN | predict from the k most similar cases | recommendations, similarity search | scaling, curse of dimensionality, slow at prediction |
    | Naive Bayes | combine per-feature likelihoods assuming independence | text (spam, complaint routing), speed | poorly calibrated probabilities |

    On structured credit data the logistic baseline wins. SVM and KNN suffer from rare events and mixed feature types; Naive Bayes
    ranks reasonably but its probabilities are badly calibrated (Brier {{ a.d6_bake.naive_bayes.brier }}). Averaging ranks of three
    models doesn't beat the best single model here.

    ### 2.3 Ensembling

    - **Bagging:** average models trained on bootstrap samples (random forest).
    - **Boosting:** sequential correction (GBM).
    - **Stacking:** a meta-model learns how to combine base models' out-of-fold predictions.
    - Gains are largest when base models are **good and different**; tiny when one model dominates.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        SVM, KNN and Naive Bayes have niches (text, similarity, speed); on tabular credit data logistic and GBM usually dominate.
        Ensembles help when strong models disagree; otherwise they add cost.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Vendor claims need a same-data, same-split bake-off** against your baseline.
    - **Diminishing returns:** after a good baseline and a GBM, more learners rarely justify the complexity.
    - **Text and similarity problems** are where these learners (and embeddings, L9.3) earn their place.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Same test set? Same features? What does the best model add over logistic, in money?"*

    ### 2.3 Calibration

    | Decile of predicted PD | Predicted | Observed |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    {% for c in a.d6_calib %}| {{ loop.index }} | {{ c[0] }}% | {{ c[1] }}% |

    ### 2.4 Thresholds from costs

    A missed default costs LGD × EAD ≈ **{{ a.d6_avg_cost_fn | n }}** on average; declining a good applicant forgoes ≈
    **{{ a.d6_avg_cost_fp | n }}** of margin. Expected cost on the test set (millions):

    | Decline if PD ≥ | Declined | Recall of defaults | Precision | Cost |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    {% for r in a.d6_thr %}| {{ r.t }} | {{ r.decline_pct }}% | {{ r.recall }}% | {{ r.precision }}% | {{ r.cost }} |
    {% endfor %}| no model (approve all) | 0% | 0% | — | {{ a.d6_thr_cost_none }} |

    Lowest cost at **PD ≥ {{ a.d6_thr_best.t }}** (decline {{ a.d6_thr_best.decline_pct }}%). The default 0.5 threshold would decline
    almost nobody — "accuracy" at 0.5 is {{ a.d6_acc_at_05 }}%, a meaningless number. Theory: decline when p > C_FP ÷ (C_FP + C_FN).

    ### 2.5 Confusion matrix vocabulary

    Precision = TP ÷ (TP + FP); recall (sensitivity) = TP ÷ (TP + FN); specificity = TN ÷ (TN + FP); F1 = harmonic mean of precision
    and recall (useful when costs are unknown, but costs are rarely unknown in business).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        AUC/Gini/KS rank; lift tells marketing and credit what the top deciles deliver; calibration makes scores usable as
        probabilities; thresholds come from costs or capacity, never 0.5.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Set the cut-off from economics, then sanity-check approval rate and portfolio mix.** The cost curve is flat near its minimum
      — choose within that band with strategy (growth vs risk appetite).
    - **Calibration is a control for finance:** provisions (IFRS 9 / CECL) and pricing rely on PD levels, not ranks.
    - **Norms help spot nonsense:** a Gini of 0.95 in credit means leakage; 0.25 means a weak model.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Show me the cost curve and the calibration table — then tell me where you'd put the cut-off and why."*

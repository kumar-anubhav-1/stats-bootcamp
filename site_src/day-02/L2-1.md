## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Rules you will use every day

    | Rule | Formula | Example |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Complement | P(not A) = 1 − P(A) | P(no fraud) = 1 − {{ a.d2_fraud_base_pct }}% |
    | Addition | P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B) | promo or display |
    | Conditional | P(A \| B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B) | return rate within Electronics |
    | Multiplication | P(A and B) = P(A \| B) × P(B) | — |
    | Independence | P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B) | testable from data |
    | Law of total probability | P(A) = Σ P(A \| Bᵢ) P(Bᵢ) | overall default = weighted channel defaults |
    | Expected value | E[X] = Σ x · P(x) | expected loss = PD × LGD × EAD |

    **Conditioning changes everything.** The return rate is {{ a.d2_p_return }}% overall, **{{ a.d2_p_return_elec }}%** for
    Electronics and {{ a.d2_p_return_food }}% for fresh food.

    **Independence is an empirical question.** Promotions run on {{ a.d2_p_promo }}% of sales lines and displays on
    {{ a.d2_p_display }}%. If independent, both would occur on {{ a.d2_p_both_if_indep }}% of lines; they actually co-occur on
    **{{ a.d2_p_both }}%** — P(display | promo) = {{ a.d2_p_disp_given_promo }}%.

    ### 2.2 Bayes' rule

    \[ P(\text{fraud} \mid \text{alert}) = \frac{P(\text{alert} \mid \text{fraud})\,P(\text{fraud})}{P(\text{alert} \mid \text{fraud})\,P(\text{fraud}) + P(\text{alert} \mid \text{no fraud})\,P(\text{no fraud})} \]

    In plain English: *of all alerts, what share are real?* It depends heavily on how rare fraud is.

    For the alert `{{ a.d2_alert_rule }}`: sensitivity {{ a.d2_alert_sens_pct }}%, false-positive rate {{ a.d2_alert_fpr_pct }}%,
    base rate {{ a.d2_fraud_base_pct }}% → **P(fraud | alert) = {{ a.d2_alert_ppv_bayes_pct }}%**. Counting directly in the data
    gives the same answer ({{ a.d2_alert_ppv_direct_pct }}%).

    ### 2.3 Natural frequencies — the way to explain it

    Of **10,000** accounts, about **{{ a.d2_nf_fraud }}** are fraudulent; the alert catches **{{ a.d2_nf_caught }}** of them — and
    also fires on **{{ a.d2_nf_false }}** genuine customers. So most alerts are false, even though the alert raises the fraud rate
    {{ a.d2_alert_lift }}-fold.

    ### 2.4 Confusing the inverse

    P(partner | default) = {{ a.d2_p_partner_given_default }}%, but P(default | partner) = **{{ a.d2_pd_partner_pct }}%**. Swapping them
    is one of the most common errors in business reporting.

    ### 2.5 Odds and log-odds

    Odds = p ÷ (1 − p). Partner default odds {{ a.d2_odds_partner }} vs branch {{ a.d2_odds_branch }}; log-odds of partner default
    = {{ a.d2_logodds_partner }}. Logistic regression (Day 5) works on the log-odds scale; odds ratios compare groups.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Condition before you compare. Independence is testable. Bayes: the share of alerts that are real depends on the base rate as
        much as on the alert's accuracy. Always translate into natural frequencies. P(A | B) ≠ P(B | A).

    **Judgement points**

    - **Every alert, screen or test is a Bayes problem.** Ask for sensitivity, false-positive rate *and* base rate — then the cases per
      10,000. An alert with {{ a.d2_alert_sens_pct }}% sensitivity produces about {{ (a.d2_nf_false / a.d2_nf_caught) | round(0) | int }}
      false alarms per fraud caught here.
    - **Capacity is the real constraint.** {{ a.d2_alert_rate_pct }}% of accounts alerting means {{ (a.d2_alert_rate_pct * 100) | round(0) | int }}
      investigations per 10,000 accounts. Is that affordable, and what is a caught fraud worth?
    - **Policy statements often swap conditionals.** "Most defaulters came through partners" does not mean "partner customers mostly default".
    - **Dependence between levers** (promo and display) means their effects cannot be separated from observational data.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        When a junior quotes an accuracy, sensitivity or "% of X who are Y", ask them to restate it as "out of 10,000 customers, how many …".
        Errors in conditioning become visible immediately.

## 3. When to use, pitfalls

| Situation | Do | Pitfall |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screening / alerts | Bayes with base rate, natural frequencies | quoting sensitivity as precision |
| Comparing groups | conditional rates per group | overall rates hiding group differences |
| Combining levers | test independence | assuming independence |
| Decisions under risk | expected value + spread | expected value alone (see L2.5) |

## 5. Output anatomy — an alert evaluation

| Number | Meaning | Good / suspicious |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base rate {{ a.d2_fraud_base_pct }}% | how rare the event is | decisive for precision |
| Sensitivity {{ a.d2_alert_sens_pct }}% | share of frauds caught | higher is better, at a cost in alerts |
| False-positive rate {{ a.d2_alert_fpr_pct }}% | share of genuine customers alerted | small % × huge population = many alerts |
| Precision (PPV) {{ a.d2_alert_ppv_bayes_pct }}% | share of alerts that are fraud | usually low for rare events — normal, not a bug |
| Lift {{ a.d2_alert_lift }}× | precision ÷ base rate | how much better than random |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Pattern | Meaning | Next |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | High sensitivity, low precision | rare event | check capacity; add a second-stage filter |
    | P(B \| A) quoted as P(A \| B) | confusion of the inverse | recompute with Bayes |
    | Joint probability ≫ product of marginals | dependence | effects can't be separated |

=== "Leader"

    | Pattern | Action — why / when / when not | Norm (rule of thumb) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Alert precision {{ a.d2_alert_ppv_bayes_pct }}% | **Deploy as a triage queue** if investigation cost < expected recovery per alert; *not* as an automatic block | Fraud alert precision is often single-digit % |
    | Vendor quotes 95% accuracy on a rare event | **Ask for precision at your base rate** and cases per 10,000 | Accuracy is meaningless when events are rare |
    | Two levers always co-occur | **Design a test** that varies them separately before allocating budget | — |

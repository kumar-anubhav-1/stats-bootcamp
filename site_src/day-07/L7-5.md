## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Finding the break (R08)

    S017's revenue as a share of the other 39 stores (removing chain seasonality): binary segmentation finds a break at **week
    {{ a.d7_cp_week }}**: share {{ a.d7_s17_share_before }}% → {{ a.d7_s17_share_after }}% (level ratio {{ a.d7_cp_ratio }}, i.e.
    −{{ ((1 - a.d7_cp_ratio) * 100) | round(0) | int }}%). On the raw revenue series the drop is harder to see, because holiday and seasonal swings are as large as the shift.

    **CUSUM monitor** (baseline weeks 1–40, k = 0.5, h = {{ a.d7_cusum_h }}): first alarm in **week {{ a.d7_cusum_alarm_week }}**. With a
    tighter threshold (h = 5) the monitor alarms in week {{ a.d7_cusum_alarm_h5 }} — before the break: a false alarm. The threshold
    trades speed against false alarms.

    **Finance.** The monthly 30+ DPD rate breaks at {{ a.d7_dq_break_month }} ({{ a.d7_dq_before }}% → {{ a.d7_dq_after }}%). Models
    fitted across a break average two regimes and fit neither — forecast from the new regime, or model the cause (vintage mix).

    ### 2.2 Censored demand (R12)

    When a SKU is out of stock, sales = stock, not demand. {{ a.d7_stockout_cell_pct }}% of SKU-store-weeks had stock-outs.

    | Total demand estimate (all SKU-weeks) | vs true demand |
    | --- | --- |
    | Observed sales (naive) | **{{ a.d7_naive_vs_true_pct }}%** |
    | Uncensored: scale sales by 40 ÷ stores in stock | {{ a.d7_uncens_vs_true_pct }}% |

    Uncensoring raises the demand estimate by {{ a.d7_uncensor_uplift_pct }}%. The simple fix over-corrects slightly: it assumes
    out-of-stock stores sold nothing that week, but they sold until the shelf emptied. Better methods estimate lost sales within the
    week or use in-stock store-days. Forecasts and safety stock trained on censored history are **biased low and
    self-reinforcing** — less stock → more stock-outs → lower "demand".

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Breaks change the baseline; find them, explain them, and don't let models average across them. Stock-outs hide demand; fix
        the history before planning from it.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Every break needs a business explanation** (refit, competitor, data feed change) before it's modelled.
    - **Monitoring thresholds are a policy choice** — how many false alarms per year will operations tolerate?
    - **Lost sales are invisible in sales data.** Ask supply chain for in-stock measures before accepting forecast "accuracy".

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"What's the change-point date, what caused it, and how was demand corrected for stock-outs?"*

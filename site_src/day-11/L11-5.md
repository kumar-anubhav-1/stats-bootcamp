# L11.5 · Computer vision in production: labels, drift, privacy and the business case

<p class="lesson-meta">Day 11 · Optional module · Basic → Advanced 45 min · Leader 45 min · Prerequisites: L9.5, L9.6, L11.1–L11.4 · Recipe: <code>rcp_drift</code> (on image statistics)</p>

**Objectives** — you can: measure how consistently people label images (agreement and **Cohen's kappa**); explain the effect of
label noise; monitor image-quality drift per camera or scanner; handle privacy and fairness in vision (faces, CCTV, documents);
and build a simple business case that weighs model errors in money.

!!! info "In plain words"
    A vision model is only as good as its labels, its cameras and the process around it. Most failures in industry come from
    those three, not from the network.

## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

=== "Finance"

    > *"Before we automate document checks, convince the risk committee it is accurate, fair, private and monitored."*

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Label quality: do two people agree?

    Two annotators labelled the same {{ a.v_n_test_windows | n }} windows. They agree on **{{ a.v_agree_pct }}%** — sounds high,
    but because most windows are obviously "product", they would agree about {{ a.v_kappa_expected_chance }}% of the time by chance
    alone. **Cohen's kappa** corrects for that: κ = (observed agreement − chance agreement) ÷ (1 − chance agreement) =
    **{{ a.v_kappa }}** (rule of thumb: above 0.8 is strong, 0.6–0.8 moderate). Annotator B is mislabelling some windows (planted V3).

    Fixes: clear labelling guide with examples, double-label a sample, measure kappa per annotator, retrain or replace weak
    labellers, and resolve disagreements with a third person.

    **Does a little label noise ruin the model?** Trained on labels with 8% random flips, our pixel model still scores
    {{ a.v_noisy_train_test_acc }}% on clean test labels — random noise mostly averages out. **Systematic** errors (one annotator
    always calling dark packs "gaps") do not average out; they teach the model the wrong rule.

    ### 2.2 Drift: watch the cameras, not just the model

    Track per camera, per day: average brightness, sharpness, colour balance, share of images rejected, and the model's average
    confidence. Use the PSI bands from L9.6 on these statistics. In our data the dim camera (S07) and the blurry one (S03) would be
    flagged in the first week — before any wrong OSA report reached a manager.

    ### 2.3 Privacy and fairness

    | Risk | Example | Control |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | People in images | shoppers or staff in shelf photos | point cameras at shelves, blur faces automatically, short retention |
    | Biometric use | face recognition for "loss prevention" | high legal risk in many countries; avoid unless clearly lawful and approved |
    | Sensitive documents | ID cards, cheques | secure storage, access logs, redact before analytics |
    | Unequal accuracy | handwriting styles, older documents, darker skin tones in ID photos | measure accuracy by group (L9.5) before launch |

    ### 2.4 The business case in money

    For shelf gaps, a missed gap costs lost sales; a false alarm costs a wasted staff walk. Choose the threshold that minimises
    *(missed gaps × lost margin) + (false alarms × cost of a walk)* — the cost-based threshold from L6.6. Add cameras, labelling
    (often the biggest cost), cloud compute, monitoring and the manual audit sample.

    ### 2.5 The lifecycle

    ```mermaid
    flowchart LR
      Q[Business question and KPI] --> C[Cameras / scanners and image quality] --> LB[Labelling guide, double-labelling, kappa]
      LB --> M[Model: pretrained + fine-tune + augmentation] --> E[Evaluate by store/camera: recall, precision, IoU]
      E --> P[Pilot with audits] --> R[Rollout] --> MON[Monitor quality, confidence, audited KPI]
      MON -.->|drift| LB
    ```

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Labels, cameras and process decide success. Measure agreement, monitor image quality, protect privacy, and price the errors.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Budget for labelling** — it is usually the largest and most underestimated cost.
    - **Require a per-camera quality dashboard** and an audited KPI before scaling.
    - **No face recognition** without explicit legal approval; design cameras so people aren't the subject.
    - **Fairness applies to vision**: test document and ID models across groups and document types.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"What's the kappa between our labellers, how do we know when a camera degrades, and who signed off on privacy?"*

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Symptom | Cause | Fix |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | kappa < 0.6 | unclear guide, weak labeller | examples, training, adjudication |
    | model confidence falling in one store | camera drift | quality check, recalibrate, augment |
    | accuracy differs by document type | unbalanced training data | collect more, measure by group |

=== "Leader"

    | Decision | Evidence needed | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | scale to all stores | pilot audit, cost per gap found, monitoring plan | audited KPI |
    | automate ID checks | accuracy by group, privacy assessment, fallback | human review for failures |

## 7. Planted traps and reuse drill

!!! warning "Traps"
    V3 noisy annotator (kappa well below raw agreement); V1 and V2 camera drift; privacy risk from people in shelf images.

**Reuse drill:** make annotator B's errors systematic (call every dark product window a gap) instead of random — retrain and compare
precision on the test stores.

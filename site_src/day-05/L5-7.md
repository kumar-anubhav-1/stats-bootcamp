## 1. Business question

=== "Retail"

    > "Margin rate fell from {{ a.d5_cap_m_q1 }}% in 2025Q1 to {{ a.d5_cap_m_q2 }}% in 2025Q2. Why — and is it fixed?"

=== "Finance"

    > "Approval rates dropped from {{ a.d5_appr_q1 }}% to {{ a.d5_appr_q2 }}% in 2025Q2. Has underwriting become stricter?"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The workflow

    1. **Restate** the question as a measurable comparison (metric, periods, population).
    2. **Data checks** (Day 1): duplicates, entry errors, definition changes between periods.
    3. **Decompose** the change: did the **mix** change (more of a low-margin or low-approval segment), or did the **rate** within
       segments change?
    4. **Model** the rate change with the drivers you can measure (regression, Day 5).
    5. **Sensitivity:** does the conclusion survive alternative definitions?
    6. **Recommend** with numbers and uncertainty; save the analysis as a config.

    ### 2.2 Retail: mix vs rate

    Segments = category × promotion status. Change in margin rate = mix effect + rate effect:

    $$\Delta m = \underbrace{\sum_s (w_{s,1} - w_{s,0})\,m_{s,0}}_{\text{mix}} + \underbrace{\sum_s w_{s,1}(m_{s,1} - m_{s,0})}_{\text{rate}}$$

    | Component | pp |
    | --- | --- |
    | Mix (shifts between segments) | {{ a.d5_cap_mix_pp }} |
    | **Rate (margin within segments)** | **{{ a.d5_cap_rate_pp }}** |

    Promotion share barely moved ({{ a.d5_cap_promo_share_q1 }}% → {{ a.d5_cap_promo_share_q2 }}% of lines). The fall is *within*
    segments, and the largest contributors are **regular-price** lines: {{ a.d5_cap_top_rate | kv(" pp") }}.

    **Why did regular margins fall?** Shelf price relative to list price dropped from {{ a.d5_cap_reg_pidx_q1 }} to
    {{ a.d5_cap_reg_pidx_q2 }} while unit cost relative to list stayed at {{ a.d5_cap_reg_cidx_q1 }} → {{ a.d5_cap_reg_cidx_q2 }}.
    Regular-line margin: {{ a.d5_cap_reg_m_q1 }}% → {{ a.d5_cap_reg_m_q2 }}%. By region (price index Q1 → Q2):
    {% for r, v in a.d5_cap_reg_pidx_by_region.items() %}{{ r }} {{ v[0] }} → {{ v[1] }}{{ "; " if not loop.last else "." }}{% endfor %}
    Regional price indices were cut in North, South and West (competitive pricing, L8.4). Promoted lines also earned less
    ({{ a.d5_cap_promo_m_q1 }}% → {{ a.d5_cap_promo_m_q2 }}%): a regression of promoted-line margin on discount depth and category
    shows each +1 pp of discount costs {{ a.d5_cap_mr_per_disc_pp | abs }} pp of margin, and discounts deepened slightly
    ({{ a.d5_cap_disc_q1 }}% → {{ a.d5_cap_disc_q2 }}%); after adjusting for depth the Q2 effect shrinks from
    {{ a.d5_cap_q2_effect_raw_pp }} to {{ a.d5_cap_q2_effect_after_disc_pp }} pp.

    **Is it fixed?** In 2025Q3 the regular price index returned to {{ a.d5_cap_reg_pidx_q3 }} and total margin to {{ a.d5_cap_m_q3 }}%.

    ### 2.3 Finance: a pure mix story

    | Approval rate | 2025Q1 | 2025Q2 |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Branch | {{ a.d5_appr_by_ch['2025Q1_branch'] }}% | {{ a.d5_appr_by_ch['2025Q2_branch'] }}% |
    | Online | {{ a.d5_appr_by_ch['2025Q1_online'] }}% | {{ a.d5_appr_by_ch['2025Q2_online'] }}% |
    | Partner (new from {{ a.d5_partner_first_q }}) | — | **{{ a.d5_appr_by_ch['2025Q2_partner'] }}%** |
    | **All applications** | {{ a.d5_appr_q1 }}% | **{{ a.d5_appr_q2 }}%** |

    Within the existing channels approval did not change ({{ a.d5_appr_nonpartner_q1 }}% → {{ a.d5_appr_nonpartner_q2 }}%). A new
    digital partner arrived in 2025Q2 and supplied **{{ a.d5_partner_share_q2 }}%** of applications, with weaker applicants and a ~60%
    approval rate. Underwriting is not stricter; the applicant population changed — the same shift that drives the default rise
    (L3.3) and the population drift of Day 9 (F11).

    ### 2.4 Save it as a config

    Ask Claude Code to turn the decomposition into a reusable `rcp_*` config (or a small new recipe) so next quarter's review is one
    command — and record it in `toolkit/catalogue.yaml`.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Most "why did X change?" questions are mix vs rate. Decompose first, then model the rate. Check the data definitions before
        believing either.

    **Live review checklist** (use it on a junior's capstone):

    1. Is the metric and comparison precisely defined (periods, population, exclusions)?
    2. Were data issues checked (duplicates, entry errors, definition changes)?
    3. Is mix separated from rate?
    4. Are drivers quantified with uncertainty?
    5. Does the recommendation follow, with a monitoring plan?
    6. Can I re-run it from the config?

    **Judgement points**

    - Retail: the margin dip was a **pricing decision** (regional price cuts) plus slightly deeper discounts — a question for the
      pricing committee, not a cost problem.
    - Finance: approvals fell because **who applies** changed. The real decision is whether the partner channel is worth it at its
      approval and default rates (Day 8 economics).

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask the junior: *"How much of the change is mix and how much is rate?"* If they can't answer, the analysis isn't done.

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    | Finding | Meaning | Next |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | mix ≫ rate | population/segment shift | ask why mix changed |
    | rate ≫ mix | within-segment change | model drivers of the rate |
    | both | combination | report both with numbers |
    | definition changed | artefact | restate with a consistent definition |

=== "Leader"

    | Team says | You ask | Norm |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | "approvals fell, underwriting tightened" | by channel? | decompose first |
    | "margin fell because of promotions" | promo share changed? | evidence per driver |
    | "fixed now" | which metric shows it? | monitoring plan |

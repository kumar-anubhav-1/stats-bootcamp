## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 The GLM recipe

    | Outcome | Family | Link | Coefficients read as |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | counts | Poisson / negative binomial | log | rate ratios (% change) |
    | positive, right-skewed amounts | Gamma | log | % change in the mean |
    | zeros + positive amounts (spend incl. non-buyers, pure premium) | Tweedie | log | % change |
    | yes/no | binomial | logit | odds ratios (L5.3) |

    **Exposure offset:** a policy observed for half a year has half the chance of a claim. Add log(exposure) as an offset so the model
    describes the claim *rate* per policy-year.

    ### 2.2 Over-dispersion in counts

    Beverages units per store-week (store effects, temperature, holiday, promo):

    | Model | Pearson dispersion | AIC | Temperature effect | SE |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Poisson | **{{ a.d5_pois_disp }}** | {{ a.d5_pois_aic | n }} | +{{ a.d5_pois_temp_pct }}% | {{ a.d5_pois_temp_se }} |
    | Negative binomial (α = {{ a.d5_nb_alpha }}) | — | **{{ a.d5_nb_aic | n }}** | +{{ a.d5_nb_temp_pct }}% | {{ a.d5_nb_temp_se }} |

    Variance is {{ a.d5_pois_disp }}× what Poisson assumes: the Poisson SEs are too small by roughly √{{ a.d5_pois_disp }}. Negative
    binomial fixes it (much lower AIC, honest SEs); the effects barely move. Rule: **check dispersion; > 1.5 → NB or robust SEs.**

    ### 2.3 Claim frequency (motor, {{ a.d5_freq_n | n }} policies, {{ a.d5_freq_claims }} claims)

    Overall {{ a.d5_freq_rate }} claims per policy-year. Negative binomial GLM with log(exposure) offset — rate ratios vs drivers aged
    {{ a.d5_freq_base_band }}:

    | Driver age | Rate ratio |
    | --- | --- |
    {% for k, v in a.d5_freq_rr.items() %}| {{ k }} | {{ v }} |
    {% endfor %}

    Each vehicle group step: ×{{ a.d5_freq_rr_vg }}. Poisson dispersion here is only {{ a.d5_freq_pois_disp }}, but NB still fits better
    (AIC {{ a.d5_freq_nb_aic | n }} vs {{ a.d5_freq_pois_aic | n }}): policyholders differ in risk beyond the rating factors (F17).

    ### 2.4 Severity: Gamma, and the large-loss problem

    Mean claim {{ a.d5_sev_mean | n }} across {{ a.d5_sev_n }} claims; by type: {{ a.d5_sev_by_type | kv }}. A Gamma GLM (log link)
    models the mean by claim type. But the catastrophic claims (F17) dominate: capping at the 99th percentile ({{ a.d5_sev_cap | n }})
    gives a mean of {{ a.d5_sev_mean_capped | n }} — the uncapped mean is **{{ a.d5_large_loading_pct }}% higher**. Standard practice:
    model attritional (capped) severity with Gamma and add a **large-loss loading** priced separately.

    ### 2.5 Pure premium and loss ratio

    Pure premium = frequency × severity. Motor: **{{ a.d5_pure_premium_motor | n }} per policy-year** of claims. Loss ratios (claims ÷
    premium): {{ a.d5_loss_ratio | kv }}. **Motor pays out more than it collects.** Re-rating should start with the large-loss load and the youngest drivers, whose
    claim rate is about {{ (1 / a.d5_freq_rr['25-34']) | round(1) }}× that of drivers aged 25–34.

    ### 2.6 Two more tools

    - **Tweedie**: one model for "zero or positive" outcomes (spend including non-buyers, pure premium per policy) — power parameter
      1 < p < 2 between Poisson and Gamma.
    - **Quantile regression**: models a percentile (median, P90) instead of the mean — robust to outliers and useful when the tail is
      the decision (P90 basket for staffing, P95 claim for reserving).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        GLMs match the model to the outcome: counts (Poisson/NB with exposure), positive amounts (Gamma), zero-inflated amounts
        (Tweedie), percentiles (quantile regression). Check over-dispersion; treat large losses separately.

    **Judgement points**

    - **A loss ratio above 1 is a pricing decision, not a modelling detail.** Motor needs re-rating (young drivers, vehicle groups)
      and an explicit large-loss load.
    - **Poisson SEs on over-dispersed data overstate certainty** — e.g. a promotion "significant" only because SEs are 2× too small.
    - **Ask which quantile matters.** Staffing, reserving and stock decisions often depend on P90/P95, not the mean.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Which family and link, why, what's the dispersion, and how are large losses handled?"*

## 5. Output anatomy — GLM output in `rcp_regression`

| Element | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| % change per unit | exp(β) − 1 for log links |
| Pearson dispersion | ≈ 1 fine; > 1.5 over-dispersed (Poisson) |
| deviance, AIC | compare families on the same data |
| exposure | offset — rates per unit of exposure |

## 6. Scenario matrix

=== "Basic → Advanced"

## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 WoE and IV

    For each bin: WoE = ln(% of goods ÷ % of bads); IV = Σ (%good − %bad) × WoE.

    | Variable | IV | Rule of thumb |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    {% for k, v in a.d8_iv.items() %}| {{ k }} | {{ v }} | {{ "very strong (bureau scores legitimately; otherwise suspect leakage)" if v > 0.5 else "strong" if v > 0.3 else "medium" if v > 0.1 else "weak" if v > 0.02 else "not useful" }} |
    {% endfor %}

    Utilisation WoE by band: {{ a.d8_util_woe | kv }} — monotone, matching the F04 curve. **Missing income** gets its own bin: bad rate
    {{ a.d8_income_missing_bad }}% (WoE {{ a.d8_income_missing_woe }}) — missingness is informative (F02). Never impute it away.

    ### 2.2 Points

    Logistic regression on WoE, scaled so **600 points = odds 50:1** and **20 points double the odds** (factor {{ a.d8_sc_factor }},
    offset {{ a.d8_sc_offset }}). Bureau score bins earn {{ a.d8_points_bureau | kv(" pts") }}. Average score {{ a.d8_score_mean | n }}.
    Gini **{{ a.d8_sc_gini }}**, KS **{{ a.d8_sc_ks }}** — within application-scorecard norms (Gini 0.4–0.7).

    ### 2.3 Expected loss

    **EL = PD × LGD × EAD.** E.g. PD 4%, LGD 60%, EAD 6,000 → EL 144 per account. PD from the scorecard (calibrated, L6.6), LGD from
    recoveries, EAD from limit × credit conversion factor. IFRS 9 / CECL extend this to lifetime losses.

    ### 2.4 Vintage curves — cumulative 90+ DPD rate by months on book

    | Vintage | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    {% for v, c in a.d8_vintage_curves.items() %}| {{ v }} | {{ c[3] }}% | {{ c[6] }}% | {{ c[9] }}% | {{ c[12] }}% |
    {% endfor %}

    Vintages compare like with like (same age). 2025Q2 runs at roughly **{{ (a.d8_vintage_curves['2025Q2'][9] / a.d8_vintage_curves['2024Q1'][9]) | round(1) }}×**
    earlier vintages at month 9 — the partner channel (L5.7). Calendar-time delinquency mixes ages and hides this.

    ### 2.5 Roll rates (last 12 months)

    | From → to | current | 1–29 | 30–59 | 60–89 | 90+ |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    {% for fr, row in a.d8_roll.items() %}| {{ fr }} | {{ row['current'] }}% | {{ row['1-29'] }}% | {{ row['30-59'] }}% | {{ row['60-89'] }}% | {{ row['90+'] }}% |
    {% endfor %}

    {{ a.d8_roll_30_60 }}% of 30–59 accounts roll to 60–89 next month (only {{ a.d8_roll_cure_30 }}% cure); {{ a.d8_roll_60_90 }}% of 60–89
    roll to 90+. Collections effort is most valuable at 30–59.

    ### 2.6 Reject inference (F18)

    We only observe performance for approved applicants. Declined applicants (median bureau {{ a.d8_dec_bureau_median }} vs
    {{ a.d8_acc_bureau_median }} for booked; {{ a.d8_dec_below600 }}% below 600 vs {{ a.d8_acc_below600 }}%) are missing exactly where
    risk is highest. Booked low-score accounts already default at {{ a.d8_booked_low_default }}%. Models built on booked data alone
    understate risk at the cut-off (collider, L4.4); remedies: small controlled approvals below the cut-off, bureau performance on
    declined applicants, parcelling/augmentation (with care).

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Scorecards are binned, monotone, points-based logistic models — explainable and stable. Vintages and roll rates monitor the
        book. Booked-only data hides the rejects.

    **Judgement points**

    - **IV > 0.5 needs a story** — bureau score yes; anything else, suspect leakage.
    - **Vintage curves are the early-warning system** — 2025Q2 is already at 2–3× prior vintages; act on channel and cut-off.
    - **Expected loss drives pricing and provisions** — PD calibration matters (L6.4/L6.6).

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"IV table, monotone WoE, points, Gini/KS out of time — and how the latest vintages compare at the same age."*

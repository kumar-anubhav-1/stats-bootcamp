## 2. Concept

=== "Basic → Advanced"

    ### 2.1 Elasticity with the right controls

    Poisson regression of weekly units on log(shelf ÷ list price), with SKU, store and week fixed effects and promotion/display controls,
    excluding stock-out weeks (R13):

    | Category | Elasticity | Without promo controls | Planted |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Snacks | **{{ a.d8_el_snacks }}** (SE {{ a.d8_el_snacks_se }}) | {{ a.d8_el_snacks_nopromo }} | {{ a.d8_el_true_snacks }} |
    | Dairy | **{{ a.d8_el_dairy }}** | {{ a.d8_el_dairy_nopromo }} | {{ a.d8_el_true_dairy }} |

    Without controls, promotions (lower price *and* displays, extra lift) make demand look more price-sensitive than it is — Dairy's
    estimate almost doubles. Displays and promotions are confounders (L4.4).

    ### 2.2 What a +5% price change does (constant elasticity, cost ≈ {{ (a.d8_cost_ratio_snacks * 100) | round(0) | int }}–{{ (a.d8_cost_ratio_dairy * 100) | round(0) | int }}% of list)

    | Category | Units | Gross margin |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Snacks (elastic) | {{ a.d8_p5_units_snacks }}% | {{ a.d8_p5_margin_snacks }}% |
    | Dairy (inelastic) | {{ a.d8_p5_units_dairy }}% | **+{{ a.d8_p5_margin_dairy }}%** |

    With elasticity above 1 in absolute value, the profit-maximising price under constant elasticity is cost × e/(1+e) —
    **{{ a.d8_opt_markup_snacks }} × cost** for Snacks ({{ a.d8_opt_vs_list_snacks }}% vs current list). That is an upper reference,
    not a recommendation: competitor reaction, price image and cross-category effects limit it. For inelastic Dairy, the model says
    "raise prices" without limit — a sign the constant-elasticity model breaks down outside observed price ranges.

    {{ chart("elasticity", "Hover along the curves: Snacks (elastic) loses units fast; Dairy (inelastic) keeps volume — margin moves differently.") }}

    ### 2.3 Promotions: uplift, cannibalisation, ROI

    - **Uplift:** promoted Snacks SKU-store-weeks sell **+{{ a.d8_promo_uplift_pct }}%** vs the same SKU-store's non-promo average
      (average discount {{ a.d8_promo_avg_disc }}%).
    - **Cannibalisation (R10):** promoting Brand X 400 ml cut Brand X 200 ml's share by {{ a.d8_cannibal_victim_pct | abs }}% — about
      **{{ a.d8_cannibal_share }}%** of the promoted SKU's share gain came from its sibling. Net uplift = gross − cannibalised − pull-forward.
    - **ROI:** incremental margin ÷ discount given to baseline volume ≈ **{{ a.d8_promo_roi }}** — each 1.00 of discount on sales that
      would have happened anyway returns {{ a.d8_promo_roi }} of incremental margin (before halo and traffic effects). Promotions earn
      only {{ a.d8_promo_margin_rate_on_promo }}% margin on promoted volume.

    ### 2.4 Marketing-mix modelling (awareness)

    MMM regresses sales on media spend over time with **adstock** (advertising effects decay over weeks) and **saturation** (diminishing
    returns), plus price, promotions, seasonality. It estimates channel ROI and response curves for budget allocation. Our data has no
    media spend; the prompt card shows how to set it up when it does. Calibrate MMM with experiments (geo tests).

    ### 2.5 Finance: rate sensitivity

    Take-up vs offered rate needs **randomised rate tests** or offer-level data (approved but not accepted applications); historical
    rates are set by risk (L5.1) so a naive regression of take-up on rate mixes price sensitivity with risk.

=== "Leader"

    !!! abstract "Refresh"
        Elasticity needs controls (promotions, displays, stock-outs) and fixed effects. Promotions must be judged on net, incremental
        margin after cannibalisation and pull-forward. Optimisation outputs are references bounded by strategy.

    **Judgement points**

    - **Dairy is the safer margin lever** (+5% price costs only ~{{ a.d8_p5_units_dairy | abs }}% of units). A Snacks price rise also lifts
      margin on paper but loses ~{{ a.d8_p5_units_snacks | abs }}% of units — with traffic and basket effects at stake, test before moving.
    - **Promo ROI below 1 is common** — justify promotions by traffic/halo with evidence (tests), or cut depth/frequency.
    - **Price tests beat price models** for big decisions; use models to choose what to test.

    !!! tip "Coaching card"
        Ask: *"Elasticity with and without promo controls, net promo uplift after cannibalisation, and the margin impact in money."*

## Design decisions worth your review

1. **One shared core, two layers per lesson.** Each lesson page has tabs: `Foundation` and `Leader`. The shared parts (business question, prompt cards, output anatomy, exercises) appear once; the layers diverge at concept depth, scenario matrix depth, drills and quiz.
2. **Rebalanced arc.** Your suggested arc is kept, with two moves: Benford and Monte Carlo sit in Day 2 (they need distributions, not tests); insurance frequency-severity and VaR move from Day 8 to Day 9 to relieve Day 8. Day 5 keeps the Part A/B checkpoint capstone.
3. **Reference lesson for Step 2** is L3.4 *Two-group comparison: promo lift and default-rate difference*, built to the full 12-section template in both tracks, because it exercises the Leader layer hardest (SRM, Simpson's, base rates, practical vs statistical significance).
4. **Anomaly Hunt has 42 planted items** (24 retail, 18 finance), each with an automated detector test in CI, so the generator fails the build if any anomaly drifts outside its tolerance band.
5. **Answer keys are computed, never typed.** A `validator/` package recomputes every data-based answer from the generated files; quiz YAML stores the formula reference and tolerance, and CI fills the numeric answer.

## Lesson ID scheme

`L<day>.<n>`, e.g. `L3.4`. Concepts are `C001`–`C296`. Navigator nodes are `N-<tree>-<node>`, e.g. `N-TEST-2G-PROP`. Anomalies are `R01`–`R24` (retail) and `F01`–`F18` (finance). Recipes are `rcp_<name>`. Every quiz question carries `concept`, `node`, `lesson`, `track`, `difficulty` tags.
